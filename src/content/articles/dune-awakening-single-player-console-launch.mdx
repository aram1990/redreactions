Yes — as of **September 22, 2026**, *Dune: Awakening* can be played entirely solo, on every platform it's available on. Funcom's own description: "the entire game can now be played solo. The story, the open world, the crafting, the Deep Desert, all of it." That date is also when the game reaches consoles for the first time, launching on PS5 and Xbox Series X|S alongside the finale of the game's Book One story.

## What Single-Player Actually Means Here

*Dune: Awakening* isn't being rebranded as a single-player game — Funcom's own console announcement describes it as an open-world survival game inspired by Frank Herbert's *Dune*, and it still supports large-scale multiplayer. What's new is that none of the game is walled off from solo players anymore. Single-player mode ships with three preset difficulty levels plus manual customization: you can adjust harvesting rates, XP gain, combat difficulty, and toggle how destructive the Coriolis storms are. Two mechanics specific to solo play are worth knowing — bases in the Deep Desert aren't wiped weekly the way they are on official servers, and a base reconstruction tool lets you save and restore full snapshots of your build, so you don't lose progress the way multiplayer's more punishing rules allow.

## Single-Player vs. Private Servers vs. Official Servers

These are three different things, and Funcom's own materials keep them separate:

- **Single-player** runs entirely on your own save, with the relaxed rules above, and no risk of other players raiding your base.
- **Private servers** let you play with a self-selected group — "just me and my friends" is how Funcom frames it — without opening your game to the wider public matchmaking pool.
- **Official multiplayer servers** are the full shared experience: optional PvE throughout, with dedicated PvP zones for players who want that competitive layer.

All three access the same story and Book One finale — Funcom isn't gating the ending behind multiplayer participation.

## What "Final Chapter of Book One" Means

Dune: Awakening has told its story in chapters since its original PC launch. The console release coincides with the last chapter of that first overarching arc — Funcom describes it as providing the complete story "from the opening cutscene to the final confrontation" for players jumping in fresh on console, while giving existing PC players who've followed along since launch its actual payoff. It's the conclusion of Book One specifically, not a statement about the game's story ending altogether.

## Platforms, Editions and the Xbox-Specific Details

*Dune: Awakening* launches on **PS5**, **Xbox Series X|S**, and PC, with additional support for Steam Deck, ROG Xbox Ally, and GeForce Now. Three editions are available: Standard ($49.99), Deluxe ($69.99, adding a Season Pass with four DLCs plus 5-day early access starting September 17), and Ultimate ($89.99, adding four more DLCs plus extra cosmetics, building sets, an artbook and soundtrack).

On Xbox specifically, the game launches day one on **Xbox Game Pass** and as an **Xbox Play Anywhere** title, meaning progress and purchases carry over between Xbox consoles and the Microsoft Store PC version. That cross-play and cross-save link is currently limited to Xbox and Microsoft Store PC — it does not extend to Steam or PlayStation at launch. Funcom has confirmed full cross-play across PC, PlayStation and Xbox is planned for a later date, without giving a specific timeline yet.

## What We Still Don't Know

Funcom hasn't given an exact date for full cross-platform play beyond "planned for later," hasn't detailed what happens to the game's story structure after Book One concludes, and hasn't confirmed whether private servers will be available on console the way they are on PC. Treat those as open questions rather than assumptions either way.
