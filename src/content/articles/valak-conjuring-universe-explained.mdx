Valak is one of those horror villains whose movie image has completely overtaken the thing that inspired it. Say the name now and most people picture Bonnie Aarons staring out from beneath a black veil. The old demonology behind the name looks nothing like that.

Inside **The Conjuring Universe**, Valak is a demon that repeatedly takes the form of a corrupted nun, possesses Maurice "Frenchie" Theriault, battles Sister Irene in the 1950s, and later becomes the hidden force behind the Enfield haunting in *The Conjuring 2*.

The important part is that the movies built their own mythology. The historical Valac supplied a name and some occult flavor; the Demon Nun, her powers, Saint Lucy storyline, and relationship with Lorraine Warren are franchise inventions.

For the older grimoire history, Morbid Index has a separate guide to [the historical Valac and what the demonology texts actually say](https://morbidindex.com/articles/valac-valak-explained-history-grimoires/). This article is about the movie version.

## Why Does Valak Look Like a Nun?

Because James Wan changed the villain late in the making of *The Conjuring 2*.

The original antagonist was **not the Crooked Man**. Wan had designed a separate horned, winged demon for the film. An animatronic suit was built, with CGI wings planned for the finished creature.

Then Wan changed his mind during editing.

He later explained that the horned creature felt too fantastical for the grounded world he wanted for *The Conjuring*. He wanted something more personal to Lorraine Warren, something that could attack her faith rather than simply look monstrous.

Wan remembered the real Lorraine Warren speaking about her love and reverence for nuns. That gave him the idea of twisting the image into something blasphemous.

The team cast Bonnie Aarons and shot the Demon Nun material during **additional photography**, replacing the horned demon in existing scenes and creating new sequences such as the painting scene.

That is why Valak's most famous design has no real connection to the appearance described in old grimoires. It was built specifically for *The Conjuring 2*.

## The Historical Valac Looks Nothing Like Her

The name comes from the demonological tradition behind the *Ars Goetia*, part of the *Lesser Key of Solomon*.

In the best-known Goetic description, Valac is the 62nd spirit and appears as a **boy or child with angelic wings riding a two-headed dragon**. The text attributes to him knowledge of hidden treasure and the locations of serpents.

No nun. No convent. No Saint Lucy. No feud with Lorraine Warren.

The Conjuring films essentially borrowed the name and then created a new character around it.

That separation matters because a lot of online lore works backward from the films, presenting Valak's nun appearance as though it came from medieval demonology. It did not.

## Valak's Story Starts with *The Nun*

In story chronology, Valak's first major appearance is *The Nun*, set in **1952**.

The film places the demon beneath the Abbey of St. Carta in Romania. According to the movie's fictional history, a duke once used occult rituals to open a gateway that allowed Valak into the world. Christian knights later sealed the rift using a relic said to contain the blood of Christ.

Centuries later, bombing during World War II damages the abbey and reopens the gateway.

By 1952, the remaining nuns are trying to contain the evil through constant prayer. When Sister Victoria kills herself rather than allow the demon to take her as a host, the Vatican sends Father Burke and the young Sister Irene to investigate.

Valak spends much of the film wearing the form of a nun, but its immediate goal is more practical than the later mythology sometimes makes it sound: **it needs a human host to escape the abbey fully**.

Irene and Burke believe they stop it by resealing the rift with the blood-of-Christ relic.

They do not.

## Maurice Is the Link Between *The Nun* and the Warrens

Maurice, better known as Frenchie, saves Irene during the confrontation.

Valak uses that moment to possess him.

The final reveal shows an inverted cross on Maurice's neck, confirming that the demon escaped St. Carta inside him.

That twist is one of the most important connections in the entire franchise. Footage of the Warrens participating in an exorcism involving Maurice had already appeared in *The Conjuring*. *The Nun* retroactively turns that case into part of Valak's history.

It also explains why Valak's path eventually crosses Lorraine Warren's.

The exact gaps in Maurice's story have never been completely filled on screen, but the films establish that the apparent victory in Romania did not end the possession.

## *The Nun II*: Valak Goes After the Eyes of Saint Lucy

*The Nun II* moves the story forward to **1956**.

Maurice is living in France and working at a boarding school, but Valak is still using him. A trail of violent deaths across Europe brings Sister Irene back into the story.

This time the demon has a more specific objective: **the Eyes of Saint Lucy**, a holy relic the film says carries supernatural power.

The movie's mythology describes Valak as a fallen angel and links the murdered victims to the bloodline of Saint Lucy. Irene eventually learns that she belongs to that lineage.

That becomes crucial during the climax. Valak gains the relic and attempts to burn Irene alive, mirroring the movie's version of Saint Lucy's martyrdom. Irene survives the flames.

She and Sister Debra then pray the Words of Institution over spilled wine, which the film transforms into the blood of Christ. Valak is burned and banished, and Maurice appears to be freed.

"Appears" is doing a lot of work there.

The franchise has already shown that Maurice's possession eventually leads to the Warren exorcism, so *The Nun II* cannot be the permanent end of Valak's connection to him.

## Are Sister Irene and Lorraine Warren Related?

*The Nun II* strongly says yes, although it stops short of drawing a neat family tree.

Irene learns that she descends from Saint Lucy. During the sequence connecting her clairvoyant gift to that bloodline, the film includes imagery of Lorraine Warren.

Director Michael Chaves later confirmed that the bloodline connection was intentional and said the franchise had long been interested in linking the two women.

So Irene and Lorraine are not secretly the same character, and the movies do not establish them as sisters. The implication is that they are **distantly connected through the Saint Lucy lineage**.

The casting makes the connection even more obvious: Taissa Farmiga, who plays Irene, is Vera Farmiga's real-life sister.

## *Annabelle: Creation* Does Not Make Valak Annabelle's Demon

This is an easy piece of franchise lore to get wrong.

Bonnie Aarons does appear as the Demon Nun in *Annabelle: Creation*, but the film does **not** reveal that Valak is the demon attached to Annabelle.

The clearest Valak appearance is a post-credit scene teasing *The Nun*: the Romanian abbey is shown and the Demon Nun approaches through the darkness.

It functions as franchise setup.

That is very different from saying Valak secretly caused the events of *Annabelle: Creation* or is the entity associated with the doll. The movies have never established that.

## *The Conjuring 2*: Valak Becomes Lorraine's Enemy

By **1977**, Valak has become the central supernatural antagonist of *The Conjuring 2*.

Lorraine first encounters the Demon Nun during the film's Amityville sequence and later experiences visions suggesting that the entity will kill Ed.

At the Enfield house, the apparent haunting centers on the spirit of former resident Bill Wilkins. The Warrens eventually discover that Bill is not the true force behind what is happening. He is being manipulated by something stronger.

That something is Valak.

The demon uses the Enfield activity to obscure its presence, interferes with Lorraine's perception of the case, possesses or controls Janet during the climax, and pushes events toward the death Lorraine has been seeing in her visions.

The historical Enfield case did not involve a demon called Valak. That connection exists only inside the movie's fictionalized version of events.

## Why Does Knowing Valak's Name Matter?

The climax of *The Conjuring 2* hinges on Lorraine discovering the demon's name.

Valak has effectively given it to her earlier, and the letters V-A-L-A-K are hidden around the Warrens' home as visual clues. Lorraine finally remembers the name and confronts the demon with it.

Within the movie's rules, naming Valak gives Lorraine authority over it. She condemns the demon back to Hell and saves Janet and Ed.

That does **not** establish a universal Conjuring rule that learning any demon's name automatically destroys it.

It is a rule used in this particular confrontation.

The films are loose with supernatural mechanics when they need to be, so turning the scene into a rigid franchise-wide magic system creates more problems than it solves.

## Is the Crooked Man Another Form of Valak?

This is less certain than fan wikis often make it sound.

The Crooked Man appears in *The Conjuring 2* through Billy Hodgson's zoetrope and becomes one of the film's most memorable secondary monsters.

Because Valak is eventually revealed as the force manipulating the haunting, it is reasonable to connect the Crooked Man to Valak's attack on the family. But the movie does not cleanly establish whether the Crooked Man is:

- Valak taking another form,
- a separate supernatural entity under Valak's influence,
- or a manifestation produced as part of the larger haunting.

The fact that Warner Bros. and James Wan later developed a **Crooked Man spin-off** as its own concept makes the "they are definitely the same being" reading even less safe.

The best answer is that Valak is behind the larger attack, but the Crooked Man's exact supernatural status remains ambiguous.

## What Powers Does Valak Actually Show?

There is no official RPG-style power sheet, but the films consistently show several abilities.

### Possession

This is the clearest one.

Valak possesses Maurice and later uses possession as part of its attack on the Hodgsons. A human host is central to the plot of *The Nun*.

### Physical manifestation

Valak is not limited to visions. The Demon Nun can appear physically, attack characters, move through environments and interact directly with them.

### Illusions and fear manipulation

The films repeatedly use Valak to distort what characters see and experience. It creates frightening manifestations, manipulates religious imagery and weaponizes personal fears.

### Telekinesis and environmental control

Objects move, doors slam, crosses rotate, characters are thrown or lifted, and spaces seem to respond to the demon's presence.

### Manipulating other supernatural activity

*The Conjuring 2* presents Bill Wilkins as a pawn being used by Valak. That gives the demon a level of influence over the haunting beyond simply appearing in the house itself.

### Concealment and disguise

The nun form is itself a chosen image rather than Valak's historical Goetic appearance. The films also show the demon using deceptive apparitions and mimicry, although not every monster seen around Valak should automatically be classified as one of its forms.

### Psychic interference

Valak can interfere with Lorraine's perceptions and use visions against her, including repeated imagery of Ed's death.

## Why Does Valak Hate Lorraine?

The films never give us a single speech explaining one grand motive.

What they do show is a conflict built around **faith and threat**.

Lorraine can perceive supernatural entities, identify Valak and ultimately banish it by name. That makes her dangerous to the demon. Wan also designed the Nun specifically as an image that would corrupt something Lorraine revered.

By *The Conjuring 2*, Valak attacks her psychologically, blocks her ability to understand the Enfield case and repeatedly shows her a vision of Ed dying.

So "Valak hates Lorraine because of X" is too simple.

The stronger reading is that Lorraine is both a spiritual threat and a deliberately chosen target whose faith Valak wants to break.

## Is Valak the Strongest Demon in The Conjuring Universe?

The movies never establish an official ranking.

Valak is clearly one of the franchise's most dangerous recurring entities, but statements such as "Valak is stronger than the Annabelle demon" or "Valak is the most powerful demon in Hell" are fan power-scaling, not canon.

Different antagonists operate under different rules because the films are built around different stories.

Valak's importance comes from recurrence, not from an official league table.

## Does Valak Appear in *The Conjuring: Last Rites*?

Not as the movie's demon.

*The Conjuring: Last Rites* takes place in 1986 and centers on a different supernatural threat connected to the Smurl-family storyline.

Bonnie Aarons does make a cameo in the film's wedding sequence, but she appears **out of Valak makeup as a wedding guest**. It is an actor cameo and franchise Easter egg, not a return of the Demon Nun in canon.

That means Valak's confirmed character appearances remain:

1. *The Conjuring 2* (2016)
2. *Annabelle: Creation* (2017) — post-credit cameo
3. *The Nun* (2018)
4. *The Nun II* (2023)

*Last Rites* does not add a fifth Valak appearance just because Aarons is on screen.

## Is Valak Dead After *The Nun II*?

No clean answer exists.

Irene and Debra banish Valak at the end of *The Nun II*, and Maurice appears normal afterward.

But story chronology already tells us Valak returns.

The Warren exorcism of Maurice happens later, and *The Conjuring 2* places Valak at Enfield in 1977. So whatever happened in 1956 was not a permanent destruction of the demon.

As of August 2026, Warner Bros. has **not officially announced *The Nun III***. There are plenty of fan trailers, rumored release dates and speculative listings online, but no reliable studio announcement confirming a third movie.

The story still leaves room for one. There is a large gap between *The Nun II* and Valak's later confrontation with the Warrens.

Room for another movie is not the same thing as a confirmed movie.

## Valak: Canon vs Fan Theory

**Canon:** Valak takes the form of a demonic nun.

**Canon:** The nun design was created for *The Conjuring 2* and is not the historical Goetic appearance.

**Canon:** Valak possesses Maurice.

**Canon:** Valak seeks the Eyes of Saint Lucy in *The Nun II*.

**Canon:** Sister Irene is part of Saint Lucy's lineage, with the film and director strongly connecting Lorraine to the same bloodline.

**Canon:** Valak manipulates Bill Wilkins and is the hidden force behind the Enfield haunting in *The Conjuring 2*.

**Not established:** Valak is the demon attached to Annabelle.

**Not established:** Valak and the Crooked Man are definitely the same entity.

**Not established:** Valak is officially the strongest demon in the franchise.

**False:** The historical Valac was depicted as a nun.

**False:** Valak is the villain of *The Conjuring: Last Rites*.

## Why Valak Became the Face of The Conjuring Universe

The funny part is that the franchise's most recognizable monster was created as a late replacement.

Wan already had a demon. It had been designed, built and shot.

Then he replaced it with something simpler: a nun standing in the dark.

That decision gave the franchise an image strong enough to carry two spin-off films, recur across its central mythology and become recognizable even to people who have never read a line of demonology.

The old Valac gave Hollywood the name.

The Demon Nun is what Hollywood invented around it.
