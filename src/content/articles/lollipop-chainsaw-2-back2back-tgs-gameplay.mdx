Dragami Games and Nada Holdings used Tokyo Game Show 2026 to officially reveal *Lollipop Chainsaw 2 Back2Back*, the first proper sequel to 2012's *Lollipop Chainsaw* — distinct from *RePOP*, last year's remaster of the original. Juliet Starling returns, and for the first time since the announcement, there's actual gameplay to look at rather than just a logo and a synopsis.

## An All-New Sequel, Not Another Remaster

This is a genuinely new game, not a repackaged version of the 2012 original. Developer JP Universe is building it fresh, with Dragami Games and Nada Holdings publishing. That distinction matters because Dragami previously handled *RePOP*, the 2023 remaster — this is a different kind of project entirely, continuing the story rather than re-releasing it.

## Jaredwood: San Romero, Rebuilt Around the Movies

Official material confirms the story sends Juliet back to a transformed version of San Romero, the original game's setting — now rebuilt as **Jaredwood**, a metropolis built around the film industry. A mysterious director traps Juliet inside his own twisted zombie movie, and she has to fight through it, uncover the conspiracy behind what's happening, and stop a new threat called the **Corrupted Realm zombies**. Beyond that setup, Dragami and Nada haven't detailed who the director is, what the Corrupted Realm actually represents mechanically or narratively, or how the "trapped inside a movie" premise resolves. Nick — Juliet's boyfriend, reduced to a talking severed head in the original game — is confirmed returning in the same role, visible in official artwork still hanging from Juliet's belt.

## What Dragami and Nada Actually Confirm

Juliet returns with a new outfit and what official material describes as more power than before. Confirmed platform is **PlayStation 5**; the developers haven't stated PS5 exclusivity, so treat it as currently PS5-only rather than permanently locked to the platform. The release window is **2027**, with no exact date announced — any specific date circulating elsewhere should be treated as unconfirmed placeholder information rather than official.

On the creative side, Hajime Tabata — the *Final Fantasy XV* director who founded JP Universe in 2022 after leaving Square Enix — leads the studio behind the game, though Dragami and Nada haven't published his specific title or day-to-day role on this project beyond his position at JP Universe. Masahiro Yuki, who co-produced and wrote the scenario for the original *Lollipop Chainsaw*, and NekoshowguN, who did the original's main character concept art, are both confirmed returning. Notably absent from any official credit: James Gunn and Suda51, the original's writer and director. Neither Dragami nor Nada has said anything about either creator's involvement, and no material suggests they're attached to the sequel.

## What the First Gameplay Actually Shows

<img src="/images/articles/lollipop-chainsaw-2-back2back-tgs-gameplay/lollipop-chainsaw-2-back2back-combat.webp" alt="Juliet Starling swinging her chainsaw at zombies in a shower of sparks in Lollipop Chainsaw 2 Back2Back gameplay footage." loading="lazy" />

A playable demo is running at Tokyo Game Show 2026, and roughly eight minutes of footage from it has circulated since the show floor opened. The footage shows Juliet fighting with her chainsaw alongside cheerleading-style combat moves, carried over conceptually from the original game, plus a new mobility option: roller skates that let her move around environments at speed. Enemies shown are slow-moving zombies dispatched through chainsaw attacks. Coverage of the footage — notably from Push Square — has flagged rougher elements consistent with early, pre-release work: choppy animation, weak hit feedback, and dark, desaturated visuals that read as a difference from the original's more colorful palette. Treat all of that as a description of an early TGS build specifically, not a verdict on the finished game; Dragami and Nada haven't said how far along this build is relative to the eventual 2027 release.

Because the only footage circulating publicly so far is third-party capture from the show floor rather than an official trailer upload from Dragami, Nada, or PlayStation, this piece doesn't embed a video — the observations above come from written coverage of that footage rather than an official, citable upload.

## What Remains Unknown

Dragami and Nada haven't detailed a full moveset, weapon variety beyond the chainsaw, a level structure, boss design, difficulty options, or performance targets. Nothing here should be read as confirming final combat balance, mission count, or how directly the "trapped in a movie" premise connects to the Corrupted Realm threat — TGS established what kind of game this is and its basic setup, not its finished systems.
