Alice Hoffman's *Practical Magic* series is four novels written across 26 years, in an order that doesn't match the timeline they actually cover. If you've only seen the 1998 film — or the Owens family's curse lore — and want to go deeper into the books, here's the difference between reading them as Hoffman wrote them and reading them as the story unfolds.

## The Quick Version

| | |
|---|---|
| **Publication order** | *Practical Magic* (1995) → *The Rules of Magic* (2017) → *Magic Lessons* (2020) → *The Book of Magic* (2021) |
| **Chronological order** | *Magic Lessons* → *The Rules of Magic* → *Practical Magic* → *The Book of Magic* |
| **Author** | Alice Hoffman |
| **Series spans** | 17th-century Salem through modern-day New England |
| **Central family** | The Owens family, bound by a generations-old curse |
| **Book that must come last, either way** | *The Book of Magic* (2021) |

## Why the Two Orders Exist

*Practical Magic*, published in 1995, was written as a standalone novel — Hoffman had no plans for a series at the time. It follows sisters Sally and Gillian Owens as adults, dealing with the family's reputation for witchcraft and a curse that seems to doom the men who love Owens women. It became a bestseller and a 1998 film starring Sandra Bullock and Nicole Kidman.

Hoffman didn't return to the Owens family until 2017's *The Rules of Magic*, a prequel following Sally and Gillian's aunts, Franny and Jet, decades earlier. *Magic Lessons* (2020) went further back still, telling the origin story of Maria Owens, the ancestor whose choices created the family's curse in the first place, set in the 1600s. *The Book of Magic* (2021) closes the series out as a direct sequel to the original *Practical Magic*, reuniting with Sally and Gillian's generation to resolve the curse's story.

So the publication order and the in-universe timeline diverge because Hoffman wrote the middle of the story first, then filled in the beginning, then wrote the ending last.

## Publication Order: Practical Magic → The Rules of Magic → Magic Lessons → The Book of Magic

Reading in the order Hoffman wrote them means starting with the most plot-driven, tightly focused entry point — the original *Practical Magic* — before working backward through prequels that add context to characters and family history you'll already have met. This is generally considered the better entry point for new readers, since it mirrors how the series was designed to be discovered and avoids knowing the outcome of *Practical Magic*'s events before you get there.

## Chronological Order: Magic Lessons → The Rules of Magic → Practical Magic → The Book of Magic

Reading by in-story timeline means starting with Maria Owens in the 1600s and following the curse forward through each generation until it reaches Sally and Gillian in the present day. Readers who prefer this order tend to cite *The Book of Magic*'s reliance on events and details from across the whole family history — reading strictly in sequence means nothing from earlier books gets referenced before you've encountered it firsthand.

There's a real tradeoff either way: publication order preserves the surprise of *Practical Magic*'s original 1995 reveals, while chronological order preserves a cleaner sense of cause and effect across the curse's history, at the cost of knowing *Practical Magic*'s ending before you actually reach that book.

## Which Order Should You Actually Use?

There isn't a single confirmed statement from Hoffman declaring one order mandatory — both are legitimate ways into the series, and the choice mostly comes down to what kind of reader you are. If you want the experience closest to how audiences originally discovered the Owens family, publication order is the safer choice. If you'd rather experience the curse's cause and effect in a straight line and don't mind knowing where the story ends up before you finish, chronological order works too. What both orders agree on is that *The Book of Magic* belongs last — it's written as a conclusion to the whole series and assumes you already know these characters.

## Bottom Line

New to the series: read *Practical Magic*, *The Rules of Magic*, *Magic Lessons*, then *The Book of Magic* — the order they were published in, and the order most readers recommend for a first pass. Already know the story's shape and want the family history to unfold in sequence: read *Magic Lessons*, *The Rules of Magic*, *Practical Magic*, then *The Book of Magic*. Either way, you'll end in the same place.
