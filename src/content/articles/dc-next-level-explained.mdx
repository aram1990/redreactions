DC Next Level is a wave of new #1s, and the single most important thing to know about it is what it isn't: it's not a separate universe. Unlike the Absolute line, which runs on its own alternate Earth, DC has been explicit that Next Level books are **set in continuity** — the same DC Universe where *Superman*, *Batman* and *Justice League Unlimited* are still publishing. Think of it less as a new reality and more as a batch of approachable jumping-on points into the one DC already has.

## Is Next Level a reboot?

No. Nothing about the main DC Universe has been erased or rewritten to make room for it. DC describes these books as "independent reads" that aren't "heavily tied to other books" — meaning you can pick up *Batwoman* #1 without having followed *Batwoman*'s last several years of continuity in detail. But independent isn't the same as disconnected: these characters carry their established history forward rather than starting from a blank slate the way an Absolute Universe character does.

## DC All In, Next Level and the Absolute Universe — how they actually relate

All of this sits under one larger umbrella: **DC All In**, the publishing strategy Scott Snyder and Joshua Williamson have run since October 2024. As All In entered what DC calls its second act in 2026, DC described it as resting on three pillars: **Next Level**'s new #1s, the ongoing **Absolute** line, and DC's evolving flagship titles like *Batman* and *Wonder Woman*.

Sharing an umbrella doesn't mean sharing a universe. Here's the distinction that actually matters:

| | DC Next Level | Absolute Universe |
|---|---|---|
| Continuity | Main DC continuity | Separate reality, its own Earth |
| New-reader friendly | Yes | Yes |
| Existing character history | Carries forward, but #1s minimize what you need to know | Reimagined from the ground up |
| Part of DC All In | Yes | Yes |
| Must read every title? | No | No |

If you want new-reader-friendly books that still count toward the Superman and Batman you already know, that's Next Level. If you want the same characters rebuilt from scratch on a different Earth, that's Absolute — our [Absolute Universe explainer](/articles/absolute-universe-explained/) and [Absolute Universe reading order](/articles/dc-absolute-universe-reading-order/) cover that line specifically.

## Do you need to read DC K.O. first?

Mostly no. Next Level launched as All In's second act began, right after DC's *DC K.O.* event concluded on March 4, 2026, and DC itself notes that Next Level books "stay true to the characters as they've appeared in *DC All In* and *DC K.O."* — meaning some status quo details carry over. But that's context, not a prerequisite. DC built these specifically as jumping-on points, and nothing about the initiative requires reading *DC K.O.* first. If a specific issue leans harder on recent continuity, it'll typically make that clear within the first few pages rather than assuming you already know it.

## Which Next Level books are already out

The line launched with three titles. DC's original December 2025 announcement listed staggered dates through March — Lobo on March 4, Batwoman on March 11, Deathstroke on March 18 — but Lobo slipped, and all three actually debuted together:

- **Lobo #1** and **Batwoman #1** and **Deathstroke: The Terminator #1** — all released **March 18, 2026**. *Lobo* is written by Skottie Young with art by Jorge Corona; *Batwoman* by Greg Rucka with art by DaNi; *Deathstroke: The Terminator* by Tony Fleecs with art by Carmine Di Giandomenico.

More titles followed through the spring and summer:

- **The Fury of Firestorm #1** — April 8, 2026, by Jeff Lemire and Rafael de Latorre
- **Zatanna #1** — April 29, 2026
- **Barbara Gordon: Breakout #1** — May 13, 2026, by Mariko Tamaki and Amancay Nahuelpan

There's also **Next Level: One Shot #1**, released August 19, 2026 by Gerry Duggan and Fernando Blanco. It centers on Deadshot investigating a Cadmus conspiracy that touches the wider Next Level cast, and it's genuinely connective tissue rather than a single character's story — worth reading if you're following more than one Next Level book and want to see how they relate, but not required for any individual series on its own.

## What's launching September 2

Tomorrow, three more Next Level books arrive together, all bringing back long-dormant DC teams with new lineups:

**Legion of Super-Heroes #1** — Joshua Williamson and Hayden Sherman. Set a thousand years in the future, superheroes are outlawed and enforced against by the Persuaders. After R.J. Brande is murdered, DC describes a mysterious figure called "Brainiac 1 of 5" as the one who has to solve the murder and assemble a new legion of young rebels from across the United Planets to restore hope.

## Where should a beginner start?

There's no mandatory order here — these aren't chapters of one story, they're separate ongoing series that happen to share a continuity and a launch initiative. Pick based on what you actually want to read:

- **Want Batman/Gotham?** *Batwoman* or *Barbara Gordon: Breakout*.
- **Want violent action and antiheroes?** *Deathstroke* or *Lobo*.
- **Want magic and horror?** *Zatanna* or *The Deadman*.
- **Want classic superhero sci-fi?** *The Fury of Firestorm*.
- **Want cosmic, far-future team sci-fi?** *Legion of Super-Heroes*.
- **Want younger team heroes?** *Teen Titans*.
- **Want something stranger?** *The Doom Patrol*.

If you want the strongest newcomer starts specifically, *Batwoman*, *Legion of Super-Heroes* and *The Doom Patrol* are the most self-contained of the group — each opens with a clear new status quo that doesn't lean on much prior reading to follow.

## Do you need to read all of them?

No. That's true of the whole initiative the same way it's true of the Absolute line: these are separate #1s you can enter individually, not a single serialized event. Reading one Next Level book doesn't obligate you to read the others, and skipping *Zatanna* won't leave you lost in *Deathstroke*. Pick the character or genre that interests you, start at #1, and treat the rest of the line as something to explore later if you want more.
