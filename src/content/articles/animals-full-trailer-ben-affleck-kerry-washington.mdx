Netflix's August teaser for *Animals* was dialogue-free — just mood, a ringing phone, and the shape of a crisis. The full trailer released September 15 fills in what that teaser left blank: whose voice is narrating the film's themes, what the fixer actually tells the Kimballs, and how deep the "choices that could tear their world apart" really cut.

<YouTube id="mlBfQJ8oxB0" title="Animals | Official Trailer | Netflix" />

## What Netflix Has Officially Confirmed

Netflix's synopsis is direct: "When their son is kidnapped, an LA mayoral candidate and his wife scramble to raise the ransom while making choices that could tear their world apart." Ben Affleck plays **Mark Kimball**, a Los Angeles mayoral candidate; Kerry Washington plays his wife **Abigail**; their son **Denny** (Christopher Woodley) is the one taken.

The trailer opens with Mark narrating over the footage: "In a crisis, we discover who we are, and what's important to us. I see now more than ever how true those words are." Affleck, who also directs and co-wrote the film, has framed that line as the film's actual thesis rather than just trailer narration: "Who are we really? Who are we when our masks come off? Ultimately, this is a story about whether people have a real moral compass or seek to do what's in their best interests."

## Why the Ransom Crisis Gets Complicated

The new footage introduces **Dayle**, played by Gillian Anderson, as what Netflix's own material calls "the mysterious fixer." Her line to Mark and Abigail is the trailer's sharpest new piece of information: "Your son's life is at stake. So whatever secrets you've kept from one another need to come out now."

That's a meaningful escalation beyond "pay the ransom." Netflix is confirming — through Dayle's own dialogue — that Mark and Abigail are each hiding something from the other, and that the kidnapping is forcing those secrets into the open alongside the ransom demand itself. What those secrets actually are isn't shown or stated. The trailer raises the existence of a deception problem inside the marriage; it doesn't resolve what that deception is.

## A Marriage Story Wrapped in a Thriller

Affleck has been explicit that the marriage, not just the kidnapping plot, is the film's real subject: "The movie is really about marriage — what it means to attach yourself to someone, the bargains and exchanges." That's Affleck's own framing of the film's intent, not a Red Reactions interpretation of the footage — worth noting because it reframes Dayle's fixer role as less about solving the kidnapping mechanically and more about forcing Mark and Abigail's relationship into crisis alongside it.

## The Cast Beyond the Kimballs

Netflix's own material confirms Steven Yeun, Adriana Paz, and Luis Gerardo Méndez as part of the ensemble, without specifying Yeun's character or role in currently available official materials — so treat his part in the story as confirmed-cast-but-unconfirmed-role for now rather than assuming a specific function. Affleck has said he cast the ensemble for being "good collaborators," and the cast rehearsed for several weeks before shooting on location at the Kimballs' Los Angeles home.

## What Changed Since the August Teaser

The teaser worked entirely without dialogue — footage of the Kimballs' comfortable life intercut with the ringing phone that signals Denny's disappearance, and nothing more. The full trailer adds three things the teaser didn't have: Mark's opening narration establishing the film's thematic stakes, Dayle's introduction and her specific line about secrets, and a clearer sense that the ransom crisis and the marriage crisis are the same story rather than two separate threads. Where the teaser sold mood, the full trailer sells structure — what kind of film this actually is, beyond "a politician's son gets kidnapped."

## What Remains Deliberately Unknown

Nothing here confirms who orchestrated the kidnapping, what specific secrets Dayle is referring to, whether Mark or Abigail commits any particular act over the course of the film, or how the crisis resolves. The trailer is built around tension and thematic framing rather than plot mechanics, and none of its editing should be read as confirming betrayals, deaths, or a specific ending.

## Release: October 9

*Animals* arrives in select theaters and on Netflix on **October 9, 2026**. The film runs 1 hour 48 minutes and is rated R.
