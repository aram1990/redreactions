Hulu isn't waiting to see how Season 1 of *Furious* ends before committing to more of it. The streamer confirmed on August 28 that the Emmy Rossum thriller has been renewed for Season 2, with four days still left before the Season 1 finale even airs.

## Hulu renews Furious early

The Season 1 finale lands August 31, which makes this one of the earlier renewals Hulu has handed out in recent memory — the streamer has typically preferred to let a show's full run play out and long-tail viewership settle before making that call. Disney has said viewership on *Furious* grew week over week since the show's July 27 premiere, and pointed to one specific data point: one-day views for Episode 7 were up 40% compared to the premiere. Variety has since described the series as a genuine breakout hit for the platform. *Furious* airs on Hulu in the U.S., with Disney+ carrying it internationally.

## Why the show has caught on

*Furious* follows FBI agent Alice Black, played by Rossum, as she tracks a calculating female serial killer played by Lola Petticrew. The premise draws loosely from Bob Rafelson's 1987 psychological thriller *Black Widow*, though the series reworks the core dynamic rather than restaging it directly. Elizabeth Meriwether created the show, writes it, and executive produces alongside Rossum, whose Composition 8 banner is also attached; 20th Television and Searchlight Television produce. Season 1 runs eight episodes, and the show has held critics' attention as much as audiences', with reviewers pointing to Rossum and Petticrew's dynamic as the driving force behind the tension.

## What we know about Season 2

Not much yet, and Hulu isn't pretending otherwise. There's no release date, no word on when production might begin, no confirmed episode count, and nothing on where the story goes from here. The streamer also hasn't detailed which cast members return, though nothing suggests Rossum or Petticrew are stepping away from the roles that got the show renewed in the first place — Hulu simply hasn't made any formal statement on casting for the new season.

What Hulu has done is make its intentions clear well ahead of schedule. Locking in a second season before Season 1 even wraps is a statement about confidence in the numbers, even with the biggest chapter of this story — how it all ends — still four days away.
