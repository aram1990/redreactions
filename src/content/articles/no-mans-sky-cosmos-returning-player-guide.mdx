No Man's Sky's Cosmos update landed September 9 as the game's biggest overhaul of its space layer since launch — [our full breakdown of what Cosmos actually adds](/articles/no-mans-sky-cosmos-update-space-stations-alliances/) covers station ownership, Galactic Alliances and orbital base-building in detail. This isn't that article. This is for players coming back after a break, who don't need a systems explainer so much as a "what did I miss and is my save still good" answer.

## Is Your Old Save Still Good?

Almost certainly yes, with one real caveat tied to timing. No Man's Sky has never required players to start over for a major update, and every significant system since launch has been retrofitted into existing saves — Cosmos follows that same pattern, adding to what you already have rather than replacing it.

The caveat: **if you quit before the Waypoint update (October 2022)**, your old save predates a complete inventory overhaul — items used to have variable sizes, and Waypoint changed everything to occupy a single slot regardless of type. Old saves still load, but the transition can feel disorienting, and a fresh character introduces you properly to everything added since (Corvettes, Xeno Arena, and more) instead of skipping past it. If you quit any time after Waypoint, your bases, ships and currency are intact and there's no real reason to restart.

Cross-Save, added in a 2024 update, also means your progress now follows you across PC (Steam and GOG), PlayStation 4/5, Xbox One/Series, Game Pass, Nintendo Switch and PSVR — worth knowing if you're picking the game back up on a different platform than you left it on.

## What to Do First When You Log Back In

1. **Check your tech slots for broken upgrades.** Anything showing a warning icon needs dismantling and reinstalling — a common source of confusion for returning players who assume their old build still works exactly as configured.
2. **Visit the Space Anomaly**, the game's multiplayer hub, to pick up new NPCs and quests that have been added since you last checked in.
3. **Check any settlement you previously claimed** for queued decisions waiting on you.
4. **Browse base-building parts in the menu** to see what's been added — the part list has grown substantially update over update.

## Which Old Systems Changed Materially

Not everything from years ago works the way you remember it:

- **Inventory (2022):** Fully reworked to single-slot-per-item, as covered above.
- **Sentinels (2022):** No longer passive drones you could mostly ignore. Sentinel encounters now escalate into actual combat waves with mech types and coordinated AI.
- **Portal travel (2020):** The old restriction against calling your ship or making progress after using a portal is gone — portals work freely now.
- **Space stations (2024, and again with Cosmos):** A full architectural redesign hit stations in 2024, and Cosmos adds ownership and customization on top of that redesign — so even players who remember the post-2024 stations are looking at a further layer of change now.
- **Ship building (2024 onward):** You're no longer limited to finding pre-built ships. Salvage-hunting to assemble custom vessels, and building modular Corvettes at station workshops, are now core systems.

## Cosmos's Space-Station and Alliance Mechanics, in Short

Cosmos lets players become directors of their own space station — customizing its lobby, adding rooms, and changing its exterior — and found a Galactic Alliance with a name, tag and banner that other players join by visiting an owned station. That's genuinely new as of September 9, not something a returning player would have encountered even on a recent break. For the full mechanical breakdown, [see our dedicated Cosmos article](/articles/no-mans-sky-cosmos-update-space-stations-alliances/).

## Essential vs. Optional New Systems

If you only have time to engage with one or two things that have accumulated since you last played, prioritize based on what you actually enjoy rather than trying to catch up on everything:

**Worth prioritizing:** Fishing, added in a 2024 update, gives you a genuinely relaxing side activity via a Fishing Rig blueprint available from station NPCs — useful if you want something low-stakes to ease back in with. Cosmos's station ownership is also worth engaging with early since it's the newest system and the one most likely to be referenced by other returning or active players.

**Optional, engage only if it interests you:** Corvette construction is a significant time and resource investment (base components reportedly run into the millions of units), and Xeno Arena creature battles are their own self-contained side system. Neither is required to enjoy Cosmos or the core game loop — they're there if you want a longer-term project, not prerequisites.

## What Hasn't Changed

Some of the game's core loops are exactly as you left them: base resource farming and Nanite refining still work the way they always have, and frigate expeditions function identically to before. If you're worried a break means relearning everything from zero, that's not the case — the fundamentals are stable even as the systems built on top of them keep expanding.

## Common Returning-Player Confusion Points

The two things that trip up returning players most consistently: expecting the inventory system to work the way it did before 2022 (it doesn't, and won't again), and assuming a system you remember — stations, in particular — looks the same as when you left. Stations have been redesigned more than once since 2022, and Cosmos is the latest layer on top of an already-changed base. When in doubt, treat any specific mechanic you remember clearly as a starting assumption to verify in-game, not a guarantee.
