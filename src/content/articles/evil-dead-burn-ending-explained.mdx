> **Full spoilers for *Evil Dead Burn* follow, including both credits scenes.**

*Evil Dead Burn* does not build its climax around somebody discovering the Book of the Dead and foolishly reading a fresh passage aloud. The book is already part of the Price family's buried history. What the film puts at the centre of the final act is the **Kandarian Dagger** — a weapon Benjamin Price spent years hiding from the same evil his family would eventually have to face.

That makes the ending less about closing a book and more about whether Alice can finally cut herself free from two things at once: the Deadites hunting her and the abusive marriage everyone around her kept making excuses for.

She survives. Most of the Price family does not. The dagger works, but not like a magic reset button. And the film leaves two separate threats alive after the main story is over.

## What Happens Before the Ending

Alice Price (Souheila Yacoub) is married to Will (George Pullar), whose death forces her into an already tense mourning gathering with his family: parents Edgar and Susan, grandmother Polly, brother Joseph and Joseph's partner Thya.

The marriage was not what Will's family wants to remember it as. Flashbacks and Alice's burn scar make clear that Will was abusive, while the rest of the Price family had learned to minimise, excuse or ignore what was happening.

The supernatural problem arrives through the family's other secret.

Will's grandfather, Benjamin Price, researched the Book of the Dead, the Kandarian evil and ways of fighting it. Joseph uncovers Benjamin's old material, including recordings and the Kandarian Dagger. The Deadites want the dagger gone, and the possessed Jessica from *Evil Dead Rise* becomes the link that brings the infection to Will and, through him, back into the Price family.

By the final act, the family gathering has collapsed into exactly what the title promised.

## The Ending, Step by Step

### Alice finds the real Kandarian Dagger

Alice reaches Benjamin's old research space after most of the house has already turned against her.

The film plays with the franchise's own visual history by showing more than one blade, including a more familiar-looking ornate dagger. The important reveal is that the functional weapon Benjamin protected is the less theatrical one.

When possessed Joseph comes after Alice, she uses it on him.

The effect is crucial: **Joseph briefly returns to himself.**

That is the clearest evidence *Burn* gives for the dagger's function. It can break or expel the possession affecting a Deadite host. What it cannot do is undo whatever physical damage that body has already suffered. Joseph gets a brief moment as himself again, but he is already dying.

That distinction matters later with Will.

### Alice finally confronts Susan about Will

The last stretch inside the house is not just a pile-up of Deadite kills.

Alice confronts Susan about what Will did to her and shows the scar left by his abuse. Susan's response exposes the ugliest part of the Price family dynamic: they had explanations ready for Will long before any demon arrived.

Once Susan fully turns, that family argument becomes literal violence. Alice eventually kills her with a rotary saw while the house itself becomes impossible to save.

The supernatural horror is doing what *Evil Dead* often does best — taking an already poisonous human situation and making it physically monstrous.

### Alice burns the Price house

With Edgar still attacking and the Deadites closing in, Alice ruptures the gas line and sets the house ablaze.

For a moment, it looks like the obvious ending: destroy the house, destroy the Deadites, crawl away.

*Evil Dead Burn* is not that generous.

Will emerges from the fire as a charred Deadite and follows Alice to the nearby construction site.

### Alice vs. Will is the real final fight

The construction-site fight is where the film's emotional and supernatural plots finally become the same story.

Will traps Alice near a pit of hot tar and continues trying to control her even as a Deadite. Alice fights back with a jackhammer before getting the Kandarian Dagger into him.

Just as it did with Joseph, the dagger allows a glimpse of the person underneath.

Will appears human again long enough to tell Alice he loves her.

Her answer is only three words:

**"That's not enough."**

Then she finishes him.

The moment is not a romantic reconciliation. It is the opposite. The film lets Will become recognisably human again and still refuses to make that humanity an excuse for what he did to her.

## Alice Survives — but the Final Shot Is Deliberately Uncomfortable

Police and paramedics eventually find Alice alive.

When she is asked who did this to her, her answer is:

**"My ex-husband."**

The line lands because she is talking about more than the injuries from the last few minutes. For the first time, she describes Will plainly instead of participating in the version of him his family wanted preserved.

The following close-up is less tidy.

Alice's eye appears to change briefly before the cut to black. The film never stops to confirm a new possession, and it would be a mistake to write one into canon on the basis of a split-second image.

The safest reading is that the ending is **intentionally ambiguous**. It can function as a Deadite tease, a visual scar from what Alice has survived, or simply one last refusal to let an *Evil Dead* survivor walk away feeling completely safe.

Until another film addresses Alice again, she is alive at the end of the main story. Anything beyond that is interpretation.

## The Mid-Credits Scene: Polly Gets Out

Burning the house does not contain the outbreak.

The mid-credits scene reveals that Polly is still active as a Deadite. She has escaped the wreckage after severing her trapped leg and reaches the roadside, where a passing driver stops to help her.

That goes about as well as you would expect.

Polly attacks the driver, giving the film a deliberately nasty answer to the question of whether Alice ended the immediate outbreak: **no**.

The Price house is gone. The evil is not.

## The Post-Credits Scene: Ellie Bixler Is Back

The second credits scene is the bigger franchise connection.

Back at the crematorium, the manager's young daughter comes across unclaimed remains, including an urn labelled **Ellie Bixler**.

A voice draws her toward a mirror. Instead of her own reflection, she sees Deadite Ellie — Alyssa Sutherland's character from *Evil Dead Rise*.

Ellie kills the girl and caps the scene with:

**"Mommy's back."**

It is a crowd-pleasing return, but it also creates an obvious continuity question.

At the end of *Rise*, Ellie is part of the Marauder that Beth feeds into a wood chipper. The remains are thoroughly destroyed and mixed with other bodies. *Burn* does not explain how a cleanly identified urn of Ellie's ashes later reaches this crematorium, nor does it explain the mechanism that allows her to manifest again.

So the scene confirms **Ellie's return in some form**. It does not explain the logistics behind that return.

Bloody Disgusting raised the same issue after release, and the movie itself offers no missing scene that resolves it.

## What the Kandarian Dagger Actually Does

The dagger is the most important new rules question in *Burn*.

Earlier *Evil Dead* stories have used the Kandarian Dagger as part of the franchise's occult toolkit, but *Burn* makes its purpose unusually direct.

Against Joseph and Will, the dagger appears capable of **breaking the possession and bringing the human host back**, at least briefly.

That does not mean:

- every stab automatically saves the victim;
- the body heals afterward;
- the dagger permanently destroys the larger Kandarian evil;
- every earlier film followed this exact rule.

It means *Burn* gives its characters one practical way to separate a Deadite possession from its host.

Joseph demonstrates the tragedy of that rule. The evil can leave and the victim can still die.

## What Is the Circle of the Wisemen?

Benjamin Price's research also expands the franchise beyond one isolated scholar messing with a forbidden book.

*Burn* introduces the **Circle of the Wisemen**, a group connected to the study of the Book of the Dead and methods of resisting the Kandarian evil. Benjamin's material also connects Professor Raymond Knowby — the archaeologist whose recordings are central to the original *The Evil Dead* and *Evil Dead II* — to that wider history.

The name obviously echoes the Wise Men from *Army of Darkness*.

That echo is interesting. It is **not yet proof** that the modern Circle is literally the same organisation carried forward from the Middle Ages.

The film never gives us a clean institutional history linking the two. /Film likewise treated the connection as a possibility raised by the naming and by the group's function, rather than settled canon.

For now, the useful distinction is:

**Confirmed:** Benjamin Price and Knowby are tied to a group studying the supernatural threat.

**Strong implication:** the Circle's name and purpose deliberately evoke the Wise Men from *Army of Darkness*.

**Not confirmed:** that one continuous secret order has existed unchanged from medieval times to the modern films.

## How Evil Dead Burn Connects to Evil Dead Rise

The strongest continuity link is not a prop or an Easter egg. It is **Jessica**.

The possessed Jessica from the lake sequence in *Evil Dead Rise* returns in *Burn* and becomes part of the chain of events that brings the Deadite threat to Will Price.

Then the post-credits scene brings Ellie back.

Those are direct character connections between the two films, which makes *Burn* much more explicitly connected to *Rise* than the modern standalone entries initially appear.

The older films are present through lore: Knowby, the dagger, the Book of the Dead and the Wisemen references. That is enough to place *Burn* inside the wider *Evil Dead* mythology without pretending the franchise has suddenly solved every contradiction in its continuity.

## Is Bruce Campbell in Evil Dead Burn?

Yes, but not in the way one early fan theory suggested.

Bruce Campbell has a **photograph cameo** in the Price house. A separate theory claimed that another image of Benjamin Price was also Campbell in disguise or makeup.

Campbell later denied knowing anything about playing Benjamin.

That matters because the theory spread quickly enough that some coverage treated it as fact. The safest version is simple:

- Campbell has a confirmed photo cameo.
- Benjamin Price is voiced by Michael Hurst.
- Campbell did not confirm that he secretly played Benjamin in another photograph.
- Ash Williams does not return as a character.

Campbell has also said in 2026 interviews that Ash is done as far as he is concerned, even though he remains connected to the franchise as an executive producer.

## Box Office and Reception

By August 27, 2026, *Evil Dead Burn* had grossed about **$72.28 million worldwide** against a reported **$20 million production budget**.

That is profitable-looking theatrical performance in simple gross-vs-budget terms, but it is well below *Evil Dead Rise*, which finished at roughly $147 million worldwide.

The critical response is also more mixed than some early snapshots suggested.

As of August 27:

- **Rotten Tomatoes:** 71% from 205 critic reviews; 74% verified audience score.
- **Metacritic:** 54/100 from 36 critic reviews.

That split fits the reviews themselves. Variety found it effective as an aggressively nasty piece of franchise horror, while RogerEbert.com was far harsher on its cruelty, visual style and plotting.

## What's Next: Evil Dead Wrath

The next confirmed film is **Evil Dead Wrath**, written and directed by Francis Galluppi.

Warner Bros. has dated it for **April 7, 2028**.

More importantly for the lore, producer Rob Tapert has said that *Wrath* takes place in **1972**, before the events of the original film. That makes it the earliest-set feature in the franchise.

What it does **not** confirm is that *Wrath* will explain the Circle of the Wisemen, resolve Ellie's return, bring back Alice or connect every Book of the Dead into one neat timeline.

Those are logical possibilities fans can discuss. They are not announced plot points.

For now, *Burn* leaves the franchise in a deliberately untidy place: Alice gets out, Polly carries the outbreak beyond the house, Ellie is somehow back, the dagger works without solving everything, and the Price family's research reveals that people have been trying to understand this evil for much longer than the surviving characters realised.
