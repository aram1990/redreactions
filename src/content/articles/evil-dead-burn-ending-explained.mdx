Full spoilers ahead for *Evil Dead Burn*, including both credits scenes.

*Evil Dead Burn* isn't really about a haunted book this time. Sébastien Vaniček's entry into the franchise trades the Necronomicon-in-a-cabin formula for a family that was already broken long before anything demonic showed up, and it uses the Kandarian Dagger — not the book — as the thing everyone's fighting over. That swap changes the shape of the ending, and it's left a fair number of people leaving the cinema with questions about what actually got destroyed, what got left dangling, and what those two credits scenes are setting up.

Here's the full breakdown.

## What Evil Dead Burn Is About

Alice Price (Souheila Yacoub) is a recent widow. Her husband Will died in a car crash caused, unknown to anyone at the time, by a Deadite. Alice reluctantly joins Will's family — his parents Susan and Edgar, his grandmother Polly, his brother Joseph, and Joseph's partner Thya — for a mourning gathering at the family's remote lake house, the same house where Will's grandfather, Dr. Benjamin Price, once lived and did decades of quiet occult research nobody in the family took seriously. Will's body reanimates as a Deadite at the crematorium, and from there the outbreak spreads through the family across a single, increasingly brutal night.

## The Ending, Scene by Scene

By the time the film reaches its final stretch, Joseph has been fatally wounded, Susan has been infected through a kiss from possessed Edgar, and Polly has fully turned. Alice is the last uninfected person in the house.

**The attic and the dagger.** Alice retreats upstairs into Benjamin's old research space, where Joseph's grandfather kept his journals, tape recordings, and — hidden inside the walls behind rune-marked cloth — a whole collection of daggers, only one of which is the genuine article. Earlier in the film, someone tries the decoy first: a miniature replica styled after the dagger from *Evil Dead II*, which does nothing except get a Deadite's deadpan response of "Wrong one." Only the real Kandarian Dagger does anything against a Deadite. Possessed Joseph follows Alice into the attic, and rather than fight him off, Alice drives the Kandarian Dagger into his chest. It's the film's clearest demonstration of what the dagger actually does: it severs whatever's holding the demon in place, and for a few seconds Joseph is fully, horribly himself again before he dies from the wound.

**Burning the house.** Downstairs, Alice overpowers a fully-turned Polly and gets her restrained near the stairs before turning to deal with Susan, who's locked in a bathroom taunting her about Will's death and about the divorce nobody in the family ever wanted to talk about honestly. Alice finally says the thing she's clearly been holding in for the whole film: she shows Susan the scar on her stomach from an incident where Will pushed her into boiling water, and calls out the family for quietly knowing about the abuse and doing nothing. Susan turns fully into a Deadite mid-conversation and comes at her; Alice kills her with a rotary saw. Edgar attacks around the same time and Alice wounds him badly before rupturing the house's gas line and setting the whole building on fire.

**The construction site.** Alice thinks it's over. It isn't. A charred, reanimated Deadite version of Will — infected earlier off-screen, before the events of the film even began, by a Deadite connected to *Evil Dead Rise* — comes out of the burning wreckage and chases her to an adjacent construction site. He corners her at the edge of a pit filling with boiling tar. She fights back with a jackhammer, buys herself an opening, and drives the dagger into him. For a moment his face briefly reverts to human, and he begs her forgiveness, telling her he loves her. Alice's answer is flat: *"That's not enough."* She finishes him off by hand, crushing what's left of him beneath the jackhammer and the tar.

## The Final Line and That Eye Flash

Dawn breaks, and paramedics find Alice crawling out of the wreckage covered in blood, soot, and tar. When a medic asks who did this to her, she looks straight into the camera and says: *"My ex-husband."*

It's a good line on its own terms — it's Alice reclaiming the story of her own marriage on her own terms, in front of witnesses who'll actually write it down. Then the film cuts to a close-up on her face as her eyes shift colour, just slightly, before the hard cut to black.

The film doesn't clarify what that means, and it isn't supposed to. Three readings have circulated since release, and none of them can be ruled out from what's actually on screen: that Alice picked up an infection during the fight with Will and the transformation is only delayed; that it's a purely visual gesture toward what she's survived, with no literal possession implied; or that it's simply the franchise doing what it's done since 1981 — no one gets out of a Kandarian Demon story completely clean. Take it as intentional ambiguity rather than an unanswered plot hole.

## The Two Post-Credits Scenes, Explained

*Evil Dead Burn* has both a mid-credits and a post-credits scene, and they're doing very different jobs.

**Mid-credits: Polly.** The film cuts back to the smouldering ruins of the house, where Polly — still a Deadite, having freed herself from the wreckage by cutting away the trapped limb pinning her down — drags herself out to the roadside. A passing driver stops to help. Polly plays injured just long enough to get close, then attacks. The scene exists to confirm one simple, unpleasant thing: burning the house down did not contain the outbreak. It's loose, and it's already spreading.

**Post-credits: Ellie.** This one reaches further back into the franchise. At the crematorium seen earlier in the film, the manager's young daughter is left alone near a shelf of unclaimed urns. One is labelled with the name Ellie Bixler. The girl looks into a nearby mirror, and the reflection staring back isn't hers — it's Deadite Ellie (Alyssa Sutherland), the mother from *Evil Dead Rise* who died at the end of that film. Ellie steps out, snaps the girl's neck, and says: *"Mommy's back."* It's a direct callback to her own line from *Rise* — "Mommy's with the maggots now" — and it marks the first time in the film franchise that a Deadite character has returned after apparently being destroyed.

It's worth being honest that this scene doesn't cleanly reconcile with how *Rise* actually ended. Ellie's body was destroyed on screen in that film, fed into a wood chipper during the final fight, which has led plenty of viewers and reviewers to point out that a tidy urn of remains showing up at a small-town crematorium doesn't obviously square with what we watched happen to her. The film doesn't explain how her ashes got there. Take the scene as a statement of intent about where the franchise is heading rather than a scene that answers its own logistics.

## The Kandarian Dagger and the Circle of the Wise Men

*Burn* skips the franchise's usual "someone reads from the book" catalyst almost entirely. There's no cabin scene where a character stumbles onto the Necronomicon and starts translating passages aloud. Instead, the outbreak starts because Joseph, going through his late grandfather's belongings, plays Benjamin Price's old tape recordings and unwraps the hex-warded cloth hiding the Kandarian Dagger. That act alone is what draws Deadite activity toward the family — the dagger's protective ward, once broken, effectively signals its location to whatever's still roaming out there.

The dagger itself gets clearly established as the film's one reliable weapon against possession: stabbing a Deadite host with it severs the possession outright, restoring the person's humanity for a few seconds before the body gives out. That's a more decisive and immediate rule than most of the franchise has used before — earlier films treated banishment as messier and more ritual-dependent.

The bigger lore swing is the Circle of the Wise Men, introduced here for the first time: a secretive historical group of archaeologists and occultists, of which Benjamin Price and, per his own recordings, Professor Raymond Knowby — the archaeologist from the original 1981 film and *Evil Dead II* — were both members. The name is an obvious echo of the Wise Man character from *Army of Darkness*, and the film clearly wants you to make that connection. It's worth flagging that this link is suggestion rather than confirmation — nothing in *Burn* explicitly states that its Circle is the same organisation as the medieval Wise Man's order, only that the naming and the shared mission make the connection hard to miss. Consider it strongly implied, not locked-in canon, until a future film says otherwise.

## How Evil Dead Burn Connects to the Rest of the Franchise

The clearest, most direct connection is to *Evil Dead Rise* (2023). The Deadite that caused Will's fatal car crash, seen in the film's opening, is Jessica, the same possessed figure from *Rise*'s lake-cabin cold open — meaning the outbreak that eventually reaches the Price family traces back to an entity that's been active, unchecked, since that film. The post-credits Ellie reveal deepens that link considerably.

The connection to the original trilogy is real but more textural than direct. Benjamin Price's recordings explicitly reference Raymond Knowby, tying his research to the character who translated the Necronomicon in *The Evil Dead* and *Evil Dead II*, and the Kandarian Dagger itself is visually and functionally tied to the version that appeared in *Evil Dead II*. None of this means Ash Williams' story and the Price family's story are the same timeline in any literal sense — it means the films exist in the same wider world, with overlapping objects and organisations rather than shared characters.

That wider continuity is still deliberately loose. *Burn* connects directly to *Rise* while borrowing names, objects and research history from the older films without suddenly forcing every entry into one perfectly tidy timeline.

## Is Bruce Campbell Actually In This Movie?

Sort of, and less than early reports suggested. Campbell's confirmed appearance is a photograph glimpsed among a collection of family photos in Polly's house — not a speaking role, not motion-captured, just a still image. For a while after release, a separate theory circulated that Campbell had also appeared as a stand-in for Benjamin Price himself in a second photo shown in the attic. Campbell addressed this directly in a later interview and denied it was him, saying only that he wasn't aware of playing that role. So: one confirmed photo cameo, one debunked rumour. Ash Williams himself does not appear, in voice or otherwise, and Campbell has since said in interviews that he considers his run with the character finished.

## Box Office and Reception

*Evil Dead Burn* opened domestically below *Evil Dead Rise* and ultimately finished with about **$72.28 million worldwide** against a reported **$20 million production budget**. That's well below *Rise*'s roughly $147 million worldwide total, but still 3.6 times *Burn*'s production budget in theatrical gross.

The reviews were more mixed than the early buzz suggested. As of August 27, 2026, Rotten Tomatoes lists the film at **71% from critics and 74% from audiences**, while Metacritic sits at **54/100 from 36 reviews**. The practical gore and Souheila Yacoub's performance drew praise, but the plotting around the dagger, the family mythology and the film's relentless cruelty split reviewers more sharply than the first wave of reactions implied.

## What's Next

*Evil Dead Wrath* is officially confirmed as the franchise's next film, scheduled for **April 7, 2028**. Francis Galluppi, who made *The Last Stop in Yuma County*, is writing and directing. Producer Rob Tapert has also confirmed that the film is set in **1972**, which makes it the earliest-set feature in the franchise.

That does **not** mean *Wrath* is confirmed to explain the Circle of the Wise Men, Ellie's return, Alice's future or every Book of the Dead. Those are possibilities, not announced plot points. No crossover movie uniting Mia, Beth and Alice has been announced by Warner Bros. or Ghost House Pictures either.

What *Burn* actually leaves behind is a family destroyed from the inside well before the fire started, a dagger that works exactly once as advertised and still isn't enough to close the story, and at least two things loose in the world by the time the credits finish rolling.
