You need three things before *VisionQuest*, not forty. Here's the actual minimum, what's worth adding if you have time, and what's genuinely optional — verified against what Marvel has said the show is actually about, not against "everything with Vision in it."

> **Heads up:** this guide spoils *WandaVision* and *Agatha All Along* in detail, because you can't build an honest watch order without describing what those endings did. Nothing here spoils *VisionQuest* itself. For the full release date, cast and premise breakdown, start with our [complete VisionQuest guide](/articles/visionquest-guide/).

## Essential

**1. *WandaVision* (2021).** Non-negotiable, full stop. This is where White Vision — the version *VisionQuest* is entirely about — gets created. Thanos kills the original Vision in *Infinity War* by tearing the Mind Stone from his head; S.W.O.R.D. later recovers the body and rebuilts it, colorless and weaponized, aimed at Wanda. The finale has that reconstructed Vision confront the version Wanda built for herself inside the Hex, work through the Ship of Theseus paradox with him, and receive his memories before flying off alone. Every open question *VisionQuest* is built on — who he is, what he remembers, what he can't feel — starts here. For the mechanics of exactly what happened to his body, see [who White Vision really is](/articles/white-vision-explained/).

**2. *Avengers: Age of Ultron* (2015).** This is where Vision is built, and where Ultron — now confirmed as one of the AI personas living inside Vision's head in the new series — gets his ideology and his grudge. Marvel's official *VisionQuest* synopsis names Ultron directly as a presence Vision has to resist, and the show's marketing has been explicit that James Spader is back playing real scenes, not a voice cameo. Skip this and Ultron's entire presence in the new series reads as a random guest star instead of the one person in the house with a legitimate claim on Vision's origin. Full breakdown: [how Ultron returns in VisionQuest](/articles/visionquest-ultron/).

**3. *Agatha All Along* (2024).** *VisionQuest* is the third chapter of a trilogy Marvel has repeatedly and explicitly called a trilogy — *WandaVision*, then *Agatha All Along*, then this. *Agatha*'s finale has Billy Maximoff locate his twin's lost soul and go looking for him. Marvel's own synopsis introduces a character in *VisionQuest*, Thomas Shepherd, described carefully as a boy who "may be" Vision's son, reincarnate — which is Tommy, approached from the other direction. Skipping *Agatha* means the entire Thomas Shepherd thread arrives with no context at all.

## Recommended

**4. *Avengers: Infinity War* (2018).** The film where the original Vision dies, and the thing White Vision now has to process purely as archived data rather than lived memory. You can follow *VisionQuest* without it, but the show's core idea — a man who has the facts of his own death and can't feel anything about them — lands harder if you've watched the death happen.

**5. *Captain America: Civil War* (2016).** Vision at his most tentatively human: falling for Wanda, wrong about a tactical call that costs her freedom, quietly wrecked by it. It's the clearest evidence of the emotional range *VisionQuest*'s premise says he's lost access to.

**6. *Iron Man* (2008).** Origin point for J.A.R.V.I.S., D.U.M.-E, U, and the Ten Rings leader Raza — four names Marvel has confirmed are returning, in human form, as residents of Vision's mindscape. None of it is mandatory to follow the plot, but the joke of casting a butler as an AI and an AI as a person lands better if you know where J.A.R.V.I.S. started.

**7. *Spider-Man: Far From Home* (2019).** The one-scene origin of E.D.I.T.H., Tony Stark's other AI, also confirmed as a character in *VisionQuest*. Genuinely skippable if you just want the plot; useful if "wait, why is there a person named E.D.I.T.H. now" would bother you.

## Optional / comics context

**Not required, but worth knowing exists:**

- **"Vision Quest," John Byrne's *West Coast Avengers* #42–45 (1989)** — the comic the series takes its title from, in which Vision is disassembled by government agents and rebuilt as a colorless android with his memories intact but his emotions gone. It's close enough to *WandaVision*'s ending that it's plainly where Marvel got the name. For how much of the comic the show is actually borrowing versus just the title, see [Vision Quest comics explained](/articles/vision-quest-comic-visionquest-series-explained/).
- **Tom King and Gabriel Hernández Walta's *The Vision* (2015–16)** — not confirmed as source material, but the clearest thematic cousin: Vision building himself a synthetic family and watching it come apart. Useful for tone, not required for plot.
- **Jocasta's comics history** — T'Nia Miller plays Jocasta in *VisionQuest*, and Marvel's only official description is "a cunning and powerful robot driven by revenge." In the comics she was built by Ultron using Janet van Dyne's brain patterns and turned against him. Marvel hasn't confirmed that backstory carries over, but it's the only real precedent for who she might be. Full explainer: [Jocasta explained](/articles/jocasta-explained-visionquest/).
- **Young Avengers / Avengers: The Children's Crusade** — background reading for Billy and Tommy's specific comics history, if the Thomas Shepherd reveal hooks you and you want more.

## What you can skip entirely

Nothing else is load-bearing. You don't need *Multiverse of Madness*, *What If…?*, or any other project where a non-Spader Ultron shows up — Marvel has drawn no connection between those appearances and this series. You don't need the Sokovia Accords documents, Wakanda's vibranium politics, or any wider Phase 4–6 arc that doesn't touch Vision, Wanda, or Ultron directly. And despite two years of speculation, Elizabeth Olsen's Wanda is not confirmed for *VisionQuest* — she's absent from every official cast list, and Olsen has said publicly she didn't know anything about the show until Bettany told her. Don't rewatch anything specifically to prepare for a Wanda appearance that hasn't been announced.

## The bottom line

Three shows and films get you everything you need to follow *VisionQuest* on its own terms: *WandaVision* for who White Vision is, *Age of Ultron* for why Ultron is in his head, *Agatha All Along* for why a teenage boy is about to change everything. Everything past that is depth, not homework.
