Overwatch Rush is now in regional soft launch, and it's not what its name might suggest. Blizzard is explicit about this: Rush is "an entirely new game — not a port — built specifically for mobile players," made by a separate team from the one working on Overwatch itself. Here's what that actually means in practice.

## What Is Overwatch Rush?

Rush is a free-to-play top-down hero shooter set in the Overwatch universe, built from the ground up for phones rather than adapted from the PC/console game. Blizzard has said a dedicated internal team with mobile experience built it, distinct from Team 4, which Blizzard describes as remaining fully focused on Overwatch for PC and console. There's no indication the two projects are meant to compete with or replace each other — they're separate products aimed at different play contexts.

## How Is It Different From Overwatch?

The most immediate difference is the camera: Rush is played from a top-down angle rather than first-person, which changes how positioning, line of sight, and ability aiming all work. Controls are built for touchscreens rather than mouse-and-keyboard or controller, matches run shorter by design, and progression is structured around individual heroes rather than the account-wide systems Overwatch uses. None of this makes one game a replacement for the other — they're different enough in structure that comparing "depth" isn't really the useful question. Familiar heroes carry over, but their kits have been adapted for the new format rather than copied wholesale.

## How Long Does a Match Take?

Rush's own App Store listing describes "fast 3–5 min top-down battles across solo, 3v3, and 4v4 modes" — considerably shorter than a typical Overwatch match, which is the point for a mobile pick-up-and-play format. That's the current official framing; Blizzard hasn't published separate queue or loading-time estimates.

## Which Modes Are Available?

The current soft-launch build includes 4v4 PvP as the core format, alongside two newer additions: **Duo Free for All**, which puts three teams of two into the same match, and **3v3 Control**, an adaptation of Overwatch's objective-based Control format for smaller teams. The App Store listing also lists a **solo** option among its match types — worth being precise about, since Blizzard's own wording describes this as solo free-for-all PvP, not a single-player story campaign. Rush currently has no confirmed story mode.

## Which Heroes Are Playable?

The current soft-launch roster is nine heroes: Tracer, Reinhardt, Soldier: 76, Reaper, Kiriko, Pharah, Mercy, Lúcio, and Brigitte, added as part of this soft-launch update. Brigitte specifically has been reworked into a **Vanguard** role, blending frontline durability and close-range aggression while keeping some of her support kit. Across the roster, Blizzard has adapted abilities and movement for the top-down format rather than porting them directly — recognizable identities, not 1:1 mechanical copies of their Overwatch versions.

## How Hero Levels Work

Progression in Rush runs through a system Blizzard calls **Hero Levels**, tied to individual heroes rather than a single account-wide track. Players earn a progression currency through matches and spend it on the specific heroes they want to level up, unlocking loadout customization options along the way. Blizzard hasn't published detailed numbers on grind pacing or currency value, so this guide isn't estimating either.

## Familiar Locations, New Arenas

Three maps are currently live, each drawing from a recognizable Overwatch location while being built specifically for Rush's gameplay: **Narwhal Cove** (based on Gibraltar), **Hydronet Gardens** (based on Cairo), and **Mansión Portero** (based on Dorado). Blizzard hasn't described these as direct ports of their Overwatch counterparts — they're new arenas designed around the top-down format, using familiar settings as a starting point.

## Where Can You Play It Right Now?

Overwatch Rush's soft launch is currently limited to three countries: **Indonesia, Malaysia, and the Philippines**. That's the extent of official availability — there's no wider regional or global access at this stage, and this guide isn't going to walk through sideloading or region-switching workarounds to get around that. A soft launch exists specifically so Blizzard can test gameplay, matchmaking, server performance, and player feedback with a contained audience before deciding how and where to expand.

## When Does It Release Worldwide?

Blizzard has not announced a global release date or window. The company has said it hopes to expand Rush to more regions in future phases, but hasn't attached a timeline to that. Don't read a global launch date into the current three-country rollout — soft launches can run for extended periods, and Blizzard hasn't committed to a specific next step.

## Does It Affect Overwatch Itself?

No, based on what Blizzard has said. Team 4 continues working on Overwatch for PC and console independently of Rush's team, and Blizzard has framed the two as separate ongoing projects rather than one replacing or competing with the other.

## What's Still Likely to Change

Everything in a soft launch is provisional by definition. Blizzard itself treats the current hero roster, modes, Hero Levels tuning, monetization pricing, control schemes, and regional availability as things still being tested and adjusted based on player data — none of it should be read as the final shape of the game at global release.
