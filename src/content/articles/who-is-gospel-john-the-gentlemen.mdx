The man who appears with Freddy after Eddie leaves in *The Gentlemen* Season 2 Episode 6 is John "The Gospel" Dixon, better known as Gospel John, played by Pearce Quigley. He is not a new character: Gospel John appeared in Season 1 as the brother of Tommy Dixon, the man Freddy killed during the infamous chicken-suit confrontation.

| Relationship | Connection |
| --- | --- |
| Tommy Dixon | Gospel John's brother, killed by Freddy in Season 1 |
| Freddy Horniman | Blood-feud target turned manipulated ally; Gospel John reframes their history as kinship |
| Eddie Horniman | Not personally connected to Gospel John until the finale, when Gospel John's conspiracy against Bella makes him Eddie's target |
| Bella | Eddie's wife; her death is ultimately traced back to a conspiracy involving Gospel John and Freddy |

## Who Is Gospel John / John Dixon?

Gospel John is a cocaine dealer from Liverpool who mixes his criminal business with genuine, Bible-quoting religious conviction — Netflix's own cast guide describes him as a pious Christian who balances his violent world with preaching scripture. He and his brother Tommy ran a profitable cocaine operation in northwest England together, with Tommy handling the day-to-day intimidation side of things.

## Who Was Tommy Dixon?

Tommy Dixon (Peter Serafinowicz) was Gospel John's brother and a loud, no-nonsense cocaine trader known for his tracksuits. When Freddy Horniman got caught in Tommy's orbit in Season 1, it set off the chain of events that defines Gospel John's entire relationship to Freddy.

## What Did Freddy Do to Tommy?

Here's the refresher that makes Episode 6 land differently than it might otherwise: Tommy humiliated Freddy in a confrontation that involved a chicken suit — and Freddy shot and killed Tommy while wearing it. That's the act Gospel John has never let go of.

## Why Gospel John Wanted Revenge on Freddy

Gospel John responded to his brother's death exactly the way you'd expect from someone this serious about blood and scripture: he wanted retribution, framed in explicitly blood-for-blood terms, with Freddy as the target. Their relationship didn't start as anything resembling friendship — it started as a feud, with Gospel John wanting Freddy dead as payment for Tommy. Freddy survived Season 1, but that history never actually got resolved between them; it just stopped being the show's immediate focus.

## Why Gospel John's Episode 6 Visit Is a Big Warning Sign

That's exactly what makes his reappearance in Episode 6 worth paying close attention to. Freddy is in rehab, working through his resentment over Eddie inheriting the Halstead title — and the man who once wanted him dead over Tommy's murder is now meeting with him privately. Gospel John has reframed himself as something like a kindred spirit, someone who understands what Freddy feels has been "stolen" from him, and has used explicitly religious language — comparing Eddie and Freddy's inheritance dispute to the biblical story of Jacob and Esau — to talk about it.

The scene matters because Freddy is now privately meeting a man who once wanted him dead over Tommy's murder. Episode 6 deliberately makes that contact feel ominous without yet explaining everything Gospel John wants from him.

## What to Remember Before Episode 7

Two things worth holding onto: first, Gospel John's history with Freddy is a genuine blood feud, not a new alliance built from nothing — the show is deliberately building on unfinished business from Season 1. Second, the "kindred spirit" framing and the Jacob-and-Esau language are Gospel John's own way of talking about the situation, not necessarily an accurate read on what's actually happening between the brothers. Nothing here confirms what either man ultimately does with that connection.

## What Happens to Gospel John by the Season 2 Finale

> **Full Season 2 spoiler warning: this section reveals how Gospel John's arc ends, including the finale. Stop here if you haven't finished the season.**

The Episode 6 meeting turns out to be the setup for the season's central betrayal, not a passing scene. Eddie and Susie eventually trace the conspiracy behind Bella's death back to Gospel John, working with Freddy. Freddy later admits to Eddie that he knew what Gospel John was planning and helped him — the finale confirms that much, without specifying the exact mechanics of how the plot against Bella was carried out.

Eddie's response is not to kill Gospel John himself. He beats Freddy, pulls a gun on him, then reveals Gospel John bound inside a van next to a chainsaw and tells Freddy, "Someone's gotta die." Freddy is forced to kill Gospel John — the season ends with Freddy emerging from the van holding Gospel John's severed head. *The Gentlemen* writers have described the choice as deliberate: killing Gospel John himself wouldn't get Eddie the specific revenge he wants, which requires breaking Gospel John's hold over Freddy by making Freddy the one who ends it.

That resolves the blood feud that started with Tommy's death in Season 1, but not cleanly. Gospel John dies having gotten his revenge on Freddy for Tommy — engineering Bella's death and implicating Freddy in it — only to be killed by the same man he spent the season manipulating. ## About Pearce Quigley

Pearce Quigley returns as Gospel John for Season 2, having already established the character as one of Season 1's more unsettling threats. Outside *The Gentlemen*, he's known for roles in *Detectorists*, *Showtrial*, and *Small Prophets*.
