Universal+ is coming to Japan, and there's only one place to watch it.

U-NEXT and NBCUniversal announced a broad strategic partnership on August 31 that makes U-NEXT the exclusive Japanese home for Universal+, NBCUniversal's international streaming brand. The rollout starts in October, but the deal is bigger than a single streaming launch — it also covers Universal Studios Japan and a plan to get more Japanese shows and films in front of international audiences.

## What starts in October

Universal+ begins rolling out on U-NEXT "from October 2026," folding NBCUniversal's existing film and TV library into the platform. That library draws from Universal Pictures, Focus Features, DreamWorks Animation, Illumination, Universal Studio Group and Sky Studios.

NBCUniversal is framing this as a genuine milestone rather than a routine content licensing deal: the companies describe it as the first full-lineup Universal+ launch anywhere in the Asia-Pacific region. Neither company has published a title-by-title list of what's actually available at launch, so treat "NBCUniversal's library" as the accurate description for now rather than assuming every Universal-adjacent franchise shows up on day one.

## The bigger change lands in spring 2027

October is the foundation. The more significant shift comes afterward: starting in spring 2027, U-NEXT says it will add newer theatrical releases shortly after their cinema runs, plus major first-run NBCUniversal drama series, rolling out exclusively as they become available.

That's a meaningfully different tier of content than a back-catalog license — it puts U-NEXT in the position of being a day-and-date-adjacent home for new Universal releases in Japan, not just an archive. No specific spring 2027 titles or a release calendar have been announced.

## What "exclusive" actually covers

The exclusivity applies to Universal+ as a Japanese streaming brand — U-NEXT becomes the platform carrying it in Japan, and rival services won't. It does not mean every NBCUniversal theatrical release is contractually barred from Japanese cinemas, and it doesn't mean older NBCUniversal titles disappear from anywhere else they're already licensed. What's changed is specifically where Universal+, as a branded service, lives in Japan going forward.

## What hasn't been announced

A few practical questions remain genuinely open. Neither company has published pricing for Universal+ access, and it hasn't been clarified whether it's included in a standard U-NEXT subscription or sold as a separate tier or add-on. There's no complete content catalog, no confirmed October launch title list, and no spring 2027 release calendar. Anything more specific than what's outlined above is not yet public.

## Why Universal Studios Japan is part of this

The partnership's second piece ties streaming to the physical park. U-NEXT and NBCUniversal say they're working on joint music and live events at Universal Studios Japan, along with exclusive experiences and benefits for U-NEXT members and specially produced USJ video content. None of that has been detailed as finished programming yet — it's described as planned collaboration, timed loosely around USJ's 25th anniversary year, rather than a confirmed events calendar.

The third piece runs the opposite direction: U-NEXT and NBCUniversal say they'll work together to get more U-NEXT-produced Japanese dramas, anime, films and original productions in front of international viewers, using NBCUniversal's own platforms — Peacock in the U.S. and Canada, and Universal+ in Latin America — plus other partner networks. That's also a stated plan rather than a distribution deal with specific titles attached.

Together, the three pieces describe a company betting on Japan as more than a licensing market — streaming, theme park and outbound content distribution, bundled into one long-term relationship rather than three separate deals. What that actually looks like on screen starts to become clear in October.
