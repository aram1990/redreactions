Marvel has released a new trailer and fresh interior art for its **Midnight Universe**, and the publisher is no longer being subtle about what the line is supposed to be.

Spider-Man becomes a body-horror experiment. The X-Men are caught in a war involving vampires and mutant "empyres." The Fantastic Four begin as strangers whose first steps into the unknown leave them transformed in ways Marvel is describing as horrifying.

All three books — *Midnight Spider-Man #1*, *Midnight Fantastic Four #1* and *Midnight X-Men #1* — launch together on **October 7, 2026**.

Marvel says they will be the **only new Marvel comics released that day**, an unusually aggressive way to put the entire spotlight on a new shared universe.

## What is Marvel's Midnight Universe?

The Midnight Universe is a horror-driven alternate Marvel setting built around new versions of familiar characters.

Marvel describes it as a creator-driven line with an interconnected mythology rather than three unrelated horror one-shots. The company has not assigned the universe an official Earth number in the material released so far.

The obvious comparison is DC's [Absolute Universe](/articles/absolute-universe-explained/): recognizable heroes are being rebuilt outside their standard continuity by major creative teams. But the Midnight line has a much narrower organizing idea. Horror is not just one flavor among many here; it is the foundation.

Marvel Editor-in-Chief C.B. Cebulski has pointed to the original New Universe and Marvel's two Ultimate universes when discussing the company's history of creating new worlds. Midnight is another attempt at that kind of reinvention, only with body horror, vampires, occult science and cosmic dread pushed to the front.

## Midnight Spider-Man turns Peter Parker into the horror story

<img src="/images/articles/marvel-midnight-universe-spider-man-x-men-fantastic-four-breakdown/midnight-spider-man-1-cover.jpg" alt="Steve Beach's main cover for Marvel Comics' Midnight Spider-Man #1." loading="lazy" />

*Midnight Spider-Man #1. Cover art by Steve Beach. Credit: Marvel Comics.*

*Midnight Spider-Man* is written by **Phillip Kennedy Johnson** with art by **ScieTronc**.

Marvel's official setup replaces Peter Parker's familiar origin with an Oscorp experiment. A young Peter is transformed into what the publisher calls a **hideous spider hybrid** as Oscorp pursues biological immortality. When the company starts using what it learned from Peter to create more human-animal hybrids, he has to use the monstrous thing he has become against them.

That gives Johnson room to treat the Spider-Man transformation as something frightening rather than empowering.

He has cited **H.R. Giger, David Cronenberg and Junji Ito** as creative reference points for the series, which tells you more about the intended tone than a generic "dark Spider-Man" label ever could.

The newly released pages lean hard into that idea. They show Peter's mutation as invasive and physical rather than a clean superhero makeover. What they do **not** establish is a definitive checklist of every power this version will have, so it is too early to claim that abilities such as Spider-Sense or a healing factor work exactly as they do in other continuities.

Marvel is also already teasing how far the experiment will go.

### Spoiler for Midnight Spider-Man #3

The December solicitation for issue #3 reveals that **Doctor Octavius** uses what Oscorp learned from the "Parker Incident" to create what Marvel calls the world's **second synthetic mutant**.

That is future solicitation material, not something readers will discover in the October debut, but it makes clear that Peter is only the beginning of Oscorp's experiments.

## Midnight X-Men is a vampire war

<img src="/images/articles/marvel-midnight-universe-spider-man-x-men-fantastic-four-breakdown/midnight-x-men-1-cover.jpg" alt="Dike Ruan's main cover for Marvel Comics' Midnight X-Men #1." loading="lazy" />

*Midnight X-Men #1. Cover art by Dike Ruan. Credit: Marvel Comics.*

Jonathan Hickman returns to the franchise with artist **Matteo Della Fonte**, but the central conflict is deliberately different from a normal X-Men book.

Marvel's synopsis says New York is stalked by **vampires and mutant empyres**, with an uneasy peace between the factions threatening to collapse into war. Ordinary "unturned" people are caught in the middle.

The preview art makes the supernatural direction impossible to miss. One page appears to show a vampiric Storm attacking Jean Grey, but the image alone is not enough to map out exactly who has been turned, who belongs to which faction or what Jean's long-term role will be.

Marvel is keeping a lot of those relationships deliberately unclear before launch.

What the publisher has revealed about later issues is just as bleak. New promotional material describes **[Cyclops](/articles/phoenix-five-explained-cyclops-marvel-tokon/) as "the last of the X-Men"** while taking a young mutant under his wing.

Hickman's involvement also means the book is using some of his familiar structural tools. The creative team has confirmed that the Midnight line will include data-page-style material, although this is a different continuity from his Krakoa-era X-Men work.

## Midnight Fantastic Four begins without the family

<img src="/images/articles/marvel-midnight-universe-spider-man-x-men-fantastic-four-breakdown/midnight-fantastic-four-1-cover.jpg" alt="Kev Walker's main cover for Marvel Comics' Midnight Fantastic Four #1." loading="lazy" />

*Midnight Fantastic Four #1. Cover art by Kev Walker. Credit: Marvel Comics.*

**Benjamin Percy** and artist **Kev Walker** are taking the biggest structural swing.

This version does not begin with Reed Richards, Sue Storm, Johnny Storm and Ben Grimm already functioning as Marvel's First Family.

Percy has said **Reed does not know Sue, Johnny or Ben when the story begins**. Their relationship develops as part of a slow-burn origin, and he has described the resulting "family" as much more toxic and complicated than the familiar version.

Marvel's official synopsis keeps the exact transformation mechanism intentionally broad: an obsessive scientist pushes into secrets of the universe that may have been better left alone, leaving himself and three other people warped in strange and horrifying ways.

That is safer than trying to reverse-engineer the entire origin from unlettered preview art.

By issue #2, Reed is beginning a tenuous working relationship with Sue, Johnny and Ben at **Storm Systems**.

Issue #3 then pushes the group toward real-world atomic history. The team travels to **Jornada del Muerto in New Mexico**, the site associated with the 1945 Trinity nuclear test, where Marvel says they discover residue from another world left behind by a crack in the firmament.

Percy has described the series through the language of cosmic horror and scientific obsession. So far, Marvel has not confirmed that familiar cosmic characters such as Galactus are involved.

## Why October 7 is such a big deal

Marvel first announced the Midnight books with a staggered rollout, but later changed course and unified the launch.

Now all three debut issues arrive on **October 7**, and Marvel says no other new Marvel comics will ship that day.

The publisher has called the move a first.

It is also supporting the line with a large variant-cover program and a recurring **Cloaked Cover** presentation. After the debut issues, the main-cover artwork is designed to remain deliberately obscured on the shelf rather than revealing the full image immediately.

The marketing gimmick fits the books unusually well: Marvel is literally withholding part of the picture from readers while the stories themselves are built around discovering what these characters have become.

## Are the three books connected?

Yes, but Marvel has not announced a traditional crossover event.

The company describes Midnight as having **rich interconnected mythology**, and the writers have developed the setting collaboratively. That means the books belong to the same new universe rather than operating as isolated alternate realities.

What Marvel has not yet laid out is a crossover reading order or a specific issue where Spider-Man, the Fantastic Four and the X-Men all meet.

For now, each book has its own horror language:

- *Midnight Spider-Man* — body horror and corporate experimentation
- *Midnight Fantastic Four* — cosmic horror and dangerous science
- *Midnight X-Men* — vampires, mutant factions and supernatural war

That separation is probably the smartest thing about the launch.

Marvel does not need three books doing the same "evil superhero" trick. The Midnight Universe is more interesting if each corner is terrifying for a different reason.

And with all three #1s taking over Marvel's October 7 release day, the publisher is giving readers a very clear place to find out whether the experiment works.
