SteamDB, the third-party site millions of PC gamers use to track Steam pricing, player counts and update history, has joined the same ownership group as Nexus Mods under a company called Chosen. SteamDB isn't merging into NexusMods.com. Its FAQ says the site keeps its existing name, URL, brand and community, its current features and data stay free, and no Nexus Mods account is required to use it.

## Who Actually Acquired SteamDB?

Precisely: Chosen is the parent company. Victor Folmann is CEO of Nexus Mods and founder of Chosen, the company that owns Nexus Mods — and SteamDB is now part of that same ownership family. "Nexus Mods' owner acquired SteamDB" is accurate shorthand; "Nexus Mods bought SteamDB" isn't quite, since Nexus Mods itself isn't the buyer.

## Why xPaw Decided to Hand Over the Project

SteamDB was started in 2013 by Pavel "xPaw" Djundik and Marlamin, initially as a hobby project. Marlamin later stepped back, leaving xPaw carrying a growing workload as Steam's catalogue, users and features expanded. By his own account, running SteamDB had effectively become a full-time job, and he says he hit a point of burnout where continuing alone wasn't sustainable.

He began searching for a long-term partner in January 2026, and says he chose Nexus Mods and Chosen because he believed they understood the responsibility of a community-dependent service. He's staying on to help with the transition over the coming months — this was a search he initiated, not a forced sale.

## What Changes for Users Right Now

Very little that's visible. SteamDB's FAQ says the site keeps the same URL, name, brand, community, current features and current data. The browser extension keeps working and stays free. No Nexus Mods account is required to browse SteamDB as it works today — existing Steam sign-in remains only for the specific features that already use it.

## Will SteamDB Stay Free?

For everything that's free today, yes — and it's a specific commitment, not just an absence of announced plans. SteamDB and Chosen say everything currently free stays free, and nothing currently free will be moved behind a paywall. That promise covers today's service; it doesn't extend automatically to features that don't exist yet. Genuinely new functionality built later isn't guaranteed to be free.

## Ads, Monetization and Privacy

SteamDB's FAQ is direct that the site needs to become financially sustainable, but rules out the two most-feared outcomes for now: no plans for display or programmatic advertising, and no plans to sell personal data. It points instead to lighter-touch options under consideration — affiliate links and partnerships "where they genuinely provide value" — as ways to fund development, rather than turning SteamDB into an ad-supported site.

On privacy, SteamDB says personal account data isn't being merged into Nexus Mods for analytics, advertising or profiling; aggregate analytics may be used to understand feature usage. The game and build data SteamDB has always tracked — version history, depots, manifests — is different from personal user data, and that's the category being connected to future modding tools, not anyone's individual SteamDB activity. The FAQ also commits to keeping SteamDB's editorial independence, including continuing to report public facts that Valve or publishers might not love.

## Why SteamDB's Data Matters to Nexus Mods

SteamDB has spent over a decade tracking Steam's build and version history in granular detail — which depot belongs to which update, what files a given build contains, when a game last changed. Modded setups commonly break for one reason: Steam updates a game, and the installed mods were built against an older version. Nexus Mods wants to use SteamDB's version data to help modding tools recognize which build is installed, compare it against what a mod or Collection expects, and flag a mismatch before it breaks something.

That direction is confirmed. The specific features aren't shipped yet — things like automatic update warnings, version pinning for Collections or exact compatibility checks are described as being built toward, not live tools. SteamDB says ordinary users who don't mod anything will still be able to use the site for the same Steam data they already rely on.

## Why Some Users Are Worried

Community reaction hasn't been uniformly positive, with familiar worries about acquisitions like this: an eventual paywall, discomfort with a modding company holding Steam usage data, and concern about SteamDB's independence under a for-profit parent. Those are real reactions, but they currently run against specific written commitments on pricing, data and editorial independence. Whether those hold up over the years is a separate question from what's actually been announced today.

## What's Still Unknown

SteamDB and Chosen haven't disclosed the purchase price or other financial terms. Specific modding-integration features remain in development, and exactly how any future monetization beyond affiliate links might look hasn't been detailed. What's confirmed is the ownership change itself, xPaw's continued involvement during the transition, and the specific promises above about what stays free.
