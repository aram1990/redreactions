Rockstar Games has confirmed that the Grand Theft Auto VI footage circulating online over the past week is genuine. In a statement posted to its social channels on Wednesday, the studio said watching the clips spread had been "heartbreaking for our team" and admitted this "is obviously not how we intended for you to see the game after all this time."

The timing is what makes it notable. Rockstar published that statement roughly a day before *Grand Theft Auto VI: An Extended Look*, its first proper gameplay presentation, premieres on Netflix this afternoon. The studio spent a decade managing every detail of how this game reached the public. For the past week, it hasn't had that choice.

## What Rockstar Actually Said About the GTA 6 Leaks

The statement, signed simply "Rockstar Games," reads less like a legal notice and more like an apology letter. Beyond the "heartbreaking" line, Rockstar told fans it was sorry for how long everything has taken, from finishing the game itself, which it says is "nearly there," to sharing official details. It also thanked the community members who sent messages of support during the past week, saying their words "meant more to this team than you can imagine."

Notably, Rockstar didn't address how the footage got out, who's responsible, or what it's doing about the leaker. That's consistent with how the studio has handled leaks before: acknowledge, apologise, move forward, say nothing about the investigation.

## What Actually Leaked — and What Rockstar Has Confirmed

The material surfaced in pieces rather than as a single dump. Rockstar's statement confirms that videos of *Grand Theft Auto VI* gameplay leaked online; it does not confirm specific gameplay mechanics, story details or features shown in any individual clip. Anything beyond "yes, that footage exists and it's from our game" is still leaked material, not something Rockstar has put its name behind. Treat clip-by-clip breakdowns elsewhere with that in mind.

Red Reactions isn't linking to or describing the leaked clips here. If you've already seen coverage of the leaks, you know where to find it; this piece is about Rockstar's response and what's happening today.

## GTA 6: An Extended Look Is Happening Today

*Grand Theft Auto VI: An Extended Look* premieres on Netflix at 3 p.m. ET today, August 27. Netflix gets it first, exclusively, for six hours. After that window closes, the same presentation goes up on Rockstar's own YouTube channel and the official *Grand Theft Auto VI* website at 9 p.m. ET.

It's a genuinely unusual distribution choice for Rockstar, which has historically kept everything on its own platforms. Streaming this through Netflix first is new territory for the studio, and it puts the reveal in front of an audience well beyond people already following gaming press. Rockstar's own statement says it's "very excited" for people to see the presentation, while acknowledging that "the intended game experience may now be impacted by some spoilers" because of the leaks.

Beyond confirming the premiere is going ahead as scheduled, Rockstar hasn't detailed what the Extended Look will show. Anything about specific content, run time or structure that you see attached to today's presentation should be treated as expectation, not confirmation, unless it's coming directly from Rockstar or Netflix.

## What Is Still Officially Confirmed About GTA 6

For context: *Grand Theft Auto VI* is currently set to release on November 19, 2026, for PlayStation 5 and Xbox Series X|S. Rockstar has not announced a PC version, and nothing in this week's statement or the Extended Look announcement changes that.

The game is set in the state of Leonida, home to a reimagined Vice City, and follows two playable protagonists, Jason and Lucia.
