Ryan Gosling is heading back to Warner Bros., but this time he is staying behind the camera.

The actor and producer is developing a new live-action **Flintstones** movie with his Open Invite producing partner Jessie Henderson, according to Deadline. The project is still in its earliest stages, but there is already one choice that makes it more interesting than a straightforward Fred Flintstone reboot: the film is expected to center on **Bamm-Bamm Rubble**.

Yes, Bamm-Bamm.

The super-strong kid who spent most of the original cartoons smashing things with a club is apparently the character Warner Bros. wants to build its next trip to Bedrock around.

Gosling is producing, not currently attached to star, and the project does not yet have a writer, director, cast or release date. That distinction matters, because right now this is a movie being developed rather than one that is ready to shoot.

Still, after decades of Flintstones reboots and false starts, a Bamm-Bamm-led movie is at least a genuinely different idea.

## This Is Not Another Fred Flintstone Movie

Deadline reports that the new film would focus on a **grown-up Bamm-Bamm Rubble**, the adopted son of Barney and Betty Rubble.

That immediately moves the project away from the formula used by the two previous live-action movies. The 1994 *The Flintstones* centered on Fred and Barney, while the 2000 prequel *The Flintstones in Viva Rock Vegas* went back to their younger years.

A Bamm-Bamm story gives Warner Bros. a way into the same world without simply recasting Fred, Wilma, Barney and Betty and hoping audiences want the 1994 movie again with newer effects.

It is not the first time Hanna-Barbera has aged Bamm-Bamm up, either. *The Pebbles and Bamm-Bamm Show*, which ran in the early 1970s, followed teenage versions of Bamm-Bamm and Pebbles Flintstone as they attended Bedrock High School, dated and formed a band called the Bedrock Rockers.

That series proves there is already some precedent for a Flintstones story built around the next generation rather than their parents.

Whether the movie uses any of that material is unknown.

## Ryan Gosling Is Producing, Not Playing Bamm-Bamm

This is probably the most important thing to get straight.

Ryan Gosling is **not announced as Bamm-Bamm**.

Deadline and TheWrap both report that Gosling and Jessie Henderson are producing the project through **Open Invite**, their production company. The film is being developed at Warner Bros., and the producers are currently looking for a screenwriter.

There is no casting yet.

That may sound obvious, but Gosling's name attached to a Flintstones movie is exactly the kind of headline that can turn into "Ryan Gosling Is Bamm-Bamm" after a few rounds of social media telephone.

For now, his involvement is on the producing side.

That still makes the project notable. Gosling has become more active as a producer in recent years, and his relationship with Warner Bros. obviously did not end with *Barbie*. A Flintstones movie also gives him and Henderson a very different kind of franchise to shape: one that has enormous name recognition but has struggled to find a modern big-screen identity.

## The Flintstones Has Been Here Before

Hollywood has already tried live-action Bedrock twice.

The 1994 film starred **John Goodman as Fred Flintstone**, Rick Moranis as Barney, Elizabeth Perkins as Wilma and Rosie O'Donnell as Betty. It was a major commercial hit, even if reviews were much less enthusiastic.

Six years later, *The Flintstones in Viva Rock Vegas* arrived with an entirely new cast and a prequel story. That film did not repeat the first movie's success.

Since then, Warner Bros. has never completely stopped looking for ways to revive the property. Animated projects have continued, and different film and television ideas have surfaced over the years, but another major live-action theatrical movie has never made it to the screen.

That is why the choice of Bamm-Bamm is probably the smartest detail in this early announcement.

Trying to recreate John Goodman's Fred Flintstone would invite an immediate comparison to a movie people still remember. Moving the story forward gives the filmmakers more room to decide what a live-action Flintstones movie should even look like in the 2020s.

## Bamm-Bamm Could Actually Be a Good Way Back Into Bedrock

There is a simple advantage to using Bamm-Bamm: the character comes with history, but not much baggage.

Everyone familiar with *The Flintstones* knows who he is. He is Barney and Betty's impossibly strong son, usually seen alongside Pebbles. But unlike Fred or Barney, there is no single definitive live-action performance hanging over the role.

An older Bamm-Bamm also raises obvious questions that a new movie could play with. What does someone with absurd prehistoric super-strength look like as an adult? What kind of job does he have in Bedrock? Is Pebbles part of the story? And how much of the original family is still around?

None of those questions have answers yet, and the project is too early for anyone to pretend otherwise.

There is no official plot, no confirmed tone and no indication that Gosling plans to appear on screen.

What Warner Bros. does have is a recognizable world, a producer coming off some very large studio projects and a lead character who has not already carried two live-action movies.

That is more than enough to make this worth watching.

The next big step will be finding a writer. Until that happens, Bedrock is still very much under construction.
