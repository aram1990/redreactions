*The Last Photograph* finally has a date. It just isn't a release date.

Zack Snyder's independently produced drama will have its world premiere at the Toronto International Film Festival on **Sunday, September 13, at 6:30 p.m. local time**, screening at the John Bassett Theatre in the Metro Toronto Convention Centre. That's the first confirmed public screening this film has ever had, after roughly two decades of Snyder trying to get it made. What it still doesn't have is a distributor — TheWrap describes it plainly as a film Snyder "made independently and is seeking distribution," and as of this week no studio or streamer has acquired it.

## A premiere date, not a release date

Those are two different things, and it's worth being clear about which one this is. TIFF gives *The Last Photograph* its first real-world screening in front of press, festival audiences and potential buyers. It does not put the film in theaters or on a streaming service on any particular date. Festival premieres routinely lead to acquisition deals, sometimes within days of a screening — but that's a possible next step, not something that's happened yet. Until a distributor picks it up, there's no theatrical window, no streaming date, and no way to know how wide a release it will eventually get.

## What the film is actually about

Stuart Martin leads as a former DEA operative who heads into the South American mountains after his niece and nephew are kidnapped and their diplomat parent is killed. Fra Fee plays a war photographer, worn down by his own history, who claims to be the only person who has actually seen the kidnappers' faces. The two end up relying on each other as the search pushes them further from anything resembling safety — and, per Snyder's own description of the film as a meditation on life and death, further from anything resembling a straightforward rescue story. The cast also includes Jackie Kay, Costanza Andrade, Carolina Henao and Marlon Moreno.

Snyder directs from a screenplay by Kurt Johnstad, based on Snyder's own original story — the same writer-director split behind *300*. Snyder also produced and shot the film himself as cinematographer, alongside producers Deborah Snyder, Wesley Coller and Gianni Nunnari. Hans Zimmer, Omer Benyamin and Steven Doar composed the score.

## Nearly twenty years to get here

Snyder has been trying to make this specific film since the mid-2000s. An earlier version of the project had Christian Bale and Sean Penn attached back in 2011, before that iteration fell apart and the rights lapsed. Producer Gianni Nunnari held onto the project through the years that followed, and it wasn't formally revived until August 2025, when the film was greenlit with Martin and Fee in the leads. Production shot across Iceland, Colombia and Los Angeles and wrapped in November 2025 — meaning the film that's premiering at TIFF is a genuinely new production, not a rediscovered cut of the earlier Bale/Penn version, which never got in front of cameras at all.

Snyder gave the world its first look in August, posting a teaser directly to his own social channels rather than through a studio marketing rollout — a reflection of the film's independent status at this stage.

## What happens after September 13

TIFF is the leverage point. An independently financed film without a distributor gets one shot at a splashy public unveiling, and Toronto is where a lot of buyers go looking for exactly this kind of title. Whether *The Last Photograph* gets picked up quickly, takes longer to find a home, or ends up self-released is genuinely unknown right now. What's confirmed is narrower than the premiere itself suggests: a date, a venue, a finished film, and a search for someone to actually put it in front of audiences beyond the festival.
