For years, the safest answer to “will the original Harry Potter cast ever come back?” was some variation of probably not.

Rupert Grint has just made that answer a little more complicated.

The actor will officially reprise **Ron Weasley** in the Broadway production of *Harry Potter and the Cursed Child*, returning to the character that made him famous for the first time in a new performance since the original film series ended.

Grint will begin a **17-week limited engagement at New York’s Lyric Theatre on February 2, 2027**, with his final scheduled performance on **May 30, 2027**.

This is not a cameo, a reunion special or another round of actors talking fondly about their childhoods at Hogwarts. Grint is actually playing Ron again — except this time he is stepping into the older, married, very-much-a-dad version of the character.

And that makes the casting considerably more interesting than simple nostalgia.

## Rupert Grint is coming back as Ron Weasley

The producers of *Harry Potter and the Cursed Child* announced Grint’s casting on August 26, confirming that he will join the Broadway company for a limited run next year.

Grint played Ron in all eight Harry Potter films, beginning with *Harry Potter and the Sorcerer’s Stone* in 2001 and ending with *Harry Potter and the Deathly Hallows – Part 2* in 2011.

He was 11 when Ron became part of his life. Now, more than two decades later, he is returning to a version of the character who has also grown up.

In the announcement, Grint described that strange overlap between actor and character as part of what drew him back. He said there was something “full-circle” about returning now that both he and Ron are fathers.

That is the part of this casting that actually works.

*Cursed Child* is built around what happens after the happy ending. Harry, Ron and Hermione are no longer children trying to survive Hogwarts. They have jobs, spouses, children and a pile of unresolved baggage from the lives they lived before any of them were old enough to properly understand it.

Putting the actor who grew up as Ron into that version of the story gives the role a layer that a straightforward recast never could.

## He will not be appearing with Tom Felton

There is one slightly cruel detail for anyone already imagining a Ron-and-Draco movie reunion onstage.

**Rupert Grint and Tom Felton are not currently scheduled to appear together.**

Felton, who returned to the role of Draco Malfoy in the Broadway production, is scheduled to play his final performance on **January 3, 2027**. Grint’s run does not begin until **February 2**.

So unless the production changes those dates, there is no overlap.

Felton’s casting was already significant because it marked one of the clearest attempts yet to connect the original film generation with the stage sequel. Grint now becomes another major original cast member to make that jump.

That raises the obvious question: who is next?

There is no announcement suggesting Daniel Radcliffe or Emma Watson will follow, and there is no reason to pretend otherwise. But once a production has successfully brought Draco Malfoy and Ron Weasley back using the actors audiences spent a decade watching in cinemas, the idea no longer feels quite as impossible as it did a few years ago.

## Cursed Child gives Grint a very different Ron to play

Anyone expecting Grint simply to recreate the Ron from the films is going to get something else.

*Harry Potter and the Cursed Child* takes place years after the events of *Deathly Hallows*. The story focuses heavily on the next generation, particularly Harry’s son Albus Potter and Draco’s son Scorpius Malfoy, while the original trio are pulled back into another increasingly messy magical crisis.

Ron is older, married to Hermione and a father.

The character still has the humor that made him Ron, but the stage version is not a teenage sidekick frozen in 2011. He exists in a story about parents looking at the consequences of their own past while their children begin making decisions they cannot control.

For Grint, that means revisiting something familiar without merely impersonating his younger self.

It is also worth remembering that this is not his first time on Broadway. Grint made his Broadway debut in *It’s Only a Play* alongside Matthew Broderick and Nathan Lane, and he previously appeared in Jez Butterworth’s *Mojo* in London’s West End.

So while the sight of “Rupert Grint returns as Ron Weasley” understandably dominates the announcement, he is not walking onto a Broadway stage for the first time.

## The timing is impossible to ignore

Grint’s return also arrives during a peculiar moment for Harry Potter.

HBO is developing a [new television adaptation of the books](/articles/kit-harington-gilderoy-lockhart-harry-potter-season-2-recast) with an [entirely new cast](/articles/nicholas-hoult-gilderoy-lockhart-harry-potter), meaning another generation of actors is preparing to inherit roles that are still inseparable from the people who played them in the films.

At the same time, Broadway is doing the opposite.

Rather than asking audiences to accept a new Ron Weasley, *Cursed Child* is bringing back the Ron they already know — now old enough to play the version of the character the story requires.

The two projects are doing completely different things, but together they underline how unusual the franchise has become. Harry Potter is simultaneously being rebooted from the beginning and extending its original screen legacy.

Grint returning to Ron makes that collision impossible to miss.

## When can you see Rupert Grint as Ron Weasley?

Grint’s limited Broadway engagement begins **Tuesday, February 2, 2027**, at the **Lyric Theatre in New York City** and continues through **Sunday, May 30, 2027**.

Early ticket access is scheduled ahead of a presale on **September 1, 2026**, with general public sales beginning **September 2**.

For anyone who spent the 2000s watching Grint grow up on screen, this is probably the closest thing to a proper Ron Weasley return that seemed realistically possible.

The interesting part is that he is not being asked to go backwards.

He is returning because Ron grew up too.
