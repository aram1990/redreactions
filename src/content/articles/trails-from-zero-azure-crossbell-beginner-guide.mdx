## Zero and Azure Are the Crossbell Arc

*Trails from Zero* and *Trails to Azure* follow Lloyd Bannings and the newly formed Special Support Section, a ragtag police unit working the streets of Crossbell — a small, autonomous city-state caught between two much larger nations. Together, the two games make up what fans call the **Crossbell arc**: a complete, self-contained story across two entries, the same structural shape as the *Trails in the Sky* trilogy or the *Trails of Cold Steel* tetralogy elsewhere in the series.

*Azure* picks up directly where *Zero* ends, and Nihon Falcom built real continuity between them: importing your *Trails from Zero* save data into *Trails to Azure* changes the story experience rather than just carrying over cosmetic bonuses.

## Do You Need to Play Trails in the Sky First?

No — not to follow Zero and Azure's own story. Crossbell is a new setting with its own cast, and the games are written to stand on their own. What earlier *Sky* knowledge actually buys you is context: recurring characters, place names, and political background that Zero and Azure reference but don't require you to already know. If you've got the time, playing *Sky* first deepens the experience. If you don't, starting cold with *Zero* works, and you won't be lost.

Don't take that as blanket gatekeeping in the other direction, either — you don't need to have played every prior *Trails* game to enjoy Crossbell. It's a legitimate entry point into the wider series precisely because it was designed to be self-contained.

## How Crossbell Relates to Trails of Cold Steel

Here's where it's worth being careful and spoiler-safe: *Zero* and *Azure* run roughly concurrently with *Trails of Cold Steel* and *Trails of Cold Steel II*, and a handful of familiar faces from the *Cold Steel* cast appear in *Azure*. That overlap is the main reason some longtime fans debate play order — playing Crossbell after both *Cold Steel* games avoids spoiling *Cold Steel*'s own reveals, since Zero and Azure reference events *Cold Steel* hasn't happened yet for a newcomer. But if you're coming to the series through Crossbell first, that's a perfectly fine way in too — you'll pick up on fewer references to *Cold Steel* specifically, without anything in Zero or Azure being spoiled for you in return.

## What the 2026 Re-Releases Actually Improve

The September 10, 2026 PS5 and Switch 2 versions aren't a ground-up remake — they're enhanced ports, built on the same foundation as prior modern re-releases of the games. Confirmed upgrades include **up to 4K resolution** and **up to 120 FPS**, with the option to switch between original and upscaled textures. The Switch 2 version specifically adds **mouse input support**, a first for the games on Nintendo hardware.

Physically, both games are sold as a combined **Deluxe Edition** ($69.99) including a digital soundtrack and collector's card set, or separately as digital editions ($39.99 each). A **Limited Edition** ($109.99), available through NIS America's own store, adds a selected soundtrack, holographic card set, acrylic stand, art book, iron-on insignias and a coaster set. Nintendo Switch 2's physical release ships as a **Game-Key Card** rather than a cartridge holding the full game data.

## Bottom Line

If the question is simply "can I start Trails with Trails from Zero," the answer is yes. Play *Zero*, then *Azure*, treat *Sky* as valuable optional context rather than required homework, and know that a few *Cold Steel* faces will show up in *Azure* without anything being spoiled the other way. The 2026 PS5 and Switch 2 releases are the most technically polished way to experience Crossbell yet, whether it's your first stop in the series or your way back in.
