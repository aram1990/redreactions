Mega Lucario Z, Mega Garchomp Z and Mega Absol Z are now battle-legal in Pokémon Champions, arriving with Regulation Set M-C. If you're wondering what "Z Mega Evolution" actually changes, the honest answer splits in two: it means something specific and real-time in *Pokémon Legends: Z-A*, and something different — typing, stats and Abilities — in Champions' traditional turn-based battles. They're not the same mechanic wearing two skins.

## What Z Mega Evolution Actually Is

Z Mega Evolutions are a distinct category of Mega Evolution, not a separate evolutionary stage. The Pokémon Company's own explanation for Mega Lucario Z lays out the logic: "It is called Mega Lucario Z in order to differentiate it from the previously discovered Mega Lucario," and it "was determined to be a Mega Evolution rather than a regular Evolution, based on how its form changes through exposure to a Mega Stone." So a given species can now have two separate Mega forms — an ordinary one and a Z one — each requiring its own, differently named Mega Stone.

## The Three Z Mega Evolutions in Champions

As of Regulation Set M-C, three exist in Pokémon Champions: **Mega Absol Z**, **Mega Garchomp Z**, and **Mega Lucario Z**. Their ordinary counterparts — regular Mega Absol, Mega Garchomp and Mega Lucario — remain separate forms with their own stones; Z Mega Evolution adds new options rather than replacing what already existed.

## How Z Mega Evolution Works in Legends: Z-A

In *Pokémon Legends: Z-A – Mega Dimension*, Z Mega Evolutions are built around that game's real-time battle system. The Pokémon Company's own description: "Z Mega Evolutions such as Mega Lucario Z need less time to fire off moves after receiving an order. But Z Mega Evolutions also burn through Mega Power very quickly, making them less suited for long battles." It's a speed-for-stamina trade built specifically for fast, decisive real-time fights.

## Why That Doesn't Carry Over to Champions

Pokémon Champions doesn't use that system at all. Game Freak has been explicit that Champions exists specifically to preserve the classic turn-based format — a deliberate contrast to Legends: Z-A becoming the first mainline game to move to real-time battles. There's no "Mega Power" resource, no faster move execution, and no time pressure in a Champions battle. Instead, each Z Mega Evolution is differentiated the way any other Pokémon is here: by its type, base stats, and Ability. The "Z" is a naming and stone distinction carried over from the games' shared lore, not a battle mechanic imported wholesale.

## Mega Lucario Z

Mega Lucario Z keeps the Fighting/Steel typing of ordinary Mega Lucario, but its Ability is different: **Aura Guard**, a new Ability making its Pokémon Champions debut. Nintendo's official description: it "halves the damage the Pokémon takes from contact moves." Its held item is Lucarionite Z, distinct from the ordinary Lucarionite.

## Mega Garchomp Z

This is the one worth double-checking against Legends: Z-A rather than assuming. In *Legends: Z-A*, Mega Garchomp Z is described as losing its Ground typing. In Pokémon Champions, it doesn't — Mega Garchomp Z is confirmed **Dragon/Ground**, the same dual typing as ordinary Mega Garchomp. What actually sets it apart is its Ability, **Levitate**, which grants immunity to Ground-type moves and Ground-based hazards despite Garchomp still technically being part Ground-type. It's an unusual combination: the type stays, but the Ability cancels out the type's usual downside. Its item is Garchompite Z.

## Mega Absol Z

Ordinary Mega Absol is pure Dark-type. Mega Absol Z isn't — it's confirmed **Dark/Ghost**, a genuine typing change rather than just an Ability swap. Its Ability is **Sharpness**, which increases the power of slicing moves like Shadow Claw and Night Slash. Its item is Absolite Z.

## Can You Use a Z Mega and a Normal Mega in the Same Battle?

No. Nothing in current Champions material suggests the "Z" designation grants a second Mega Evolution slot. Champions' existing one-Mega-per-battle rule applies to Z forms the same as ordinary ones — you're choosing which Mega Evolution to bring, not unlocking an extra one.

## What Regulation Set M-C Actually Changed

Regulation Set M-C runs worldwide from September 9 through December 2, 2026. Alongside the three Z Mega Evolutions, it adds three ordinary new Mega Evolutions — Mega Salamence, Mega Golisopod and Mega Baxcalibur — plus 24 additional Pokémon newly eligible for battle, including Rillaboom. Pokémon legal under the prior Regulation Sets M-A and M-B remain available; M-C adds to the roster rather than replacing it.

## Is Z Mega Evolution Replacing Ordinary Mega Evolution?

No. Both continue to coexist as separate, independently available options. Nothing in current official material suggests Z forms are intended to phase out standard Mega Evolutions for the same species.

## What Could Still Change

Pokémon Champions is a live, ongoing competitive game, and future Regulation Sets can add, remove, or restrict which Pokémon and Mega Evolutions are legal. The Z Mega Evolutions themselves — their typing, stats and Abilities — are fixed game data, but their tournament-legal status is tied to Regulation Set M-C specifically, and that's worth keeping separate from the forms' underlying design.
