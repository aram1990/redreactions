You don't need to have finished the original *Silent Hill*, *2*, or any other entry to follow what happens here.

## What Townfall Actually Is

*Townfall* is set in 1996 on **St. Amelia**, a fictional Scottish island, and follows **Simon Ordell** (voiced by Jovan Adepo), who returns to the island after receiving cryptic messages through a strange handheld device. It's the first full-length *Silent Hill* game told entirely in **first person**, including combat — a real structural departure from the series' traditional third-person and over-the-shoulder camera work.

## Who's In It

Three named characters are confirmed so far. **Simon Ordell** is the sole playable protagonist. **Terry O'Quinn** voices **Richard**, described by Konami as the man who summons Simon back to St. Amelia and whose connection to the island's past becomes harder to ignore as the story unfolds. **Kezia Burrows** voices **Zoe**, a nurse whose voice reaches Simon through static on his device. Konami hasn't detailed how the three actually relate to each other beyond that setup, and nothing here should be read as more than what's been shown.

## The CRTV, Explained

The series' iconic radio has a new form here: the **CRTV**, a portable television Simon carries throughout the game. According to Konami, it does three distinct jobs — it picks up unstable analog signals carrying story fragments and clues, it helps solve puzzles (Konami's own example: powering a fuse box with a prepaid card, or cracking a code-secured safe), and it detects nearby threats by showing screen interference and outlining monster silhouettes through walls, which the game uses to support stealth routes around enemies rather than fighting them head-on.

That last point matters for expectations: PlayStation's own hands-on report describes combat as actively discouraged, with hiding — closets, sheds, anything that breaks line of sight — treated as the more reliable survival option over confrontation.

## Do You Need Any Silent Hill History?

Not for the story itself. Konami has been explicit that *Townfall*'s plot doesn't require outside knowledge, and McKellan's own description — a story that's "completely ours" — points the same way. What's worth knowing walking in isn't lore, it's tone: this is a slower, exploration-and-puzzle-driven horror game built around a first-person perspective the series hasn't used before, closer in spirit to the psychological dread of the *Silent Hill* franchise's reputation than to any specific returning character or location. If you're new to the series, you're not missing homework — you're just meeting it on its own terms.

## Platforms, Editions and What's Actually Confirmed

*Townfall* launches **September 24, 2026** on **PS5**, and on PC via **Steam and Epic Games Store**. The Standard Edition runs $49.99; the $59.99 Deluxe Edition adds a bonus in-game application and an alternate outfit for Simon. Pre-ordering either edition currently comes with two cosmetic CRTV skins. Konami hasn't announced any early-access window for either edition — if you've seen claims of a 48-hour head start, that hasn't been confirmed anywhere in official material as of this writing.

On PS5 specifically, Sony's own reporting confirms DualSense haptic feedback during combat, gyroscope aiming for firearms, and gyroscope-driven CRTV tuning, along with 30 FPS Quality and 60 FPS Performance display modes and PS5 Pro support via PSSR upscaling and ray tracing.

## What We Still Don't Know

Konami hasn't revealed how Simon, Richard and Zoe's stories actually connect, hasn't detailed the game's ending structure beyond confirming multiple endings exist, and hasn't said whether *Townfall* ties into any other *Silent Hill* game's continuity. Treat all of that as open until Konami says otherwise — and treat this guide as scene-setting, not a review. That comes later, after actual hands-on time with the finished game.
