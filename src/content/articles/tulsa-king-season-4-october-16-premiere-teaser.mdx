*Tulsa King* returns **Friday, October 16** on Paramount+, with Sylvester Stallone back as Dwight Manfredi. Paramount released the first official teaser and a batch of first-look photos on September 1, alongside a synopsis that's more specific than the usual season-four boilerplate: Dwight spends the season trying to legitimize his empire and protect his crew while corrupt politicians, dangerous new enemies and what Paramount calls "a crushing personal loss" all hit at once. What that loss actually is hasn't been said, and nothing in the teaser confirms it beyond that phrase.

## What the teaser actually shows

<YouTube id="yaCHj96d1PA" title="Tulsa King | Season 4 Official Teaser | Paramount+" />

The teaser leans on Dwight's own read on where things stand: "It's a dirty business," he says, "and everyone gets a little stained." Paramount's synopsis adds that every gain now comes "with even harder truths — about loyalty, leadership and the cost of ambition," and that Dwight gets pulled back toward "the street instincts he left behind in New York." That's the extent of what's officially confirmed about the season's shape. Treat any funeral imagery, gunfire or political intrigue in the actual footage as tone-setting, not plot confirmation — Paramount hasn't spelled out who the new enemies are or whose loss the season is built around.

## Who's back, and who's new

Stallone leads a returning cast that includes Martin Starr, Jay Will, Vincent Piazza, Annabella Sciorra, Chris Caldovino, McKenna Quigley Harrington, Frank Grillo, Mike "Cash Flo" Walden, James Russo, Kevin Pollak, Neal McDonough, Garrett Hedlund and Dana Delany.

The notable new addition is **Gretchen Mol**, joining as a series regular. Paramount's first-look photos identify her character as Tulsa politician Amanda Clark, pictured opposite McDonough's Cal Thresher. Other names have circulated in earlier casting reports — Eden Lee as Maya, chief of staff to Oklahoma's governor, and Flula Borg as tech billionaire Bradley Van Heusen — but those come from prior Deadline casting coverage rather than Paramount's September 1 announcement, so treat them as previously reported additions rather than something newly confirmed this week.

## Terence Winter is back — but "showrunner" isn't his title

This is the part worth getting right. Paramount's official credit, repeated in both the September 2025 renewal announcement and the September 1 premiere-date release, is that **Terence Winter serves as head writer and executive producer** for Season 4. That's not a new development — Winter's Season 4 role has been public since Paramount renewed the show back in September 2025, well before Season 3 had even premiered. He's not being introduced now; he's just back in the marketing.

It's also not the same title Dave Erickson held. Erickson ran Season 3 as showrunner and didn't return for Season 4, moving instead to focus on the fellow Taylor Sheridan series *Mayor of Kingstown*. But Paramount has not given anyone the Season 4 showrunner title in Erickson's place — Winter's official credit stops at head writer and EP.

That gap drew real industry scrutiny. Variety reported in November 2025 that Season 4 began production without a traditional on-set showrunner, describing creative friction between Erickson and Stallone as part of the backdrop, and that 101 Studios executive Scott Stone was filling day-to-day leadership on set instead — alongside production managers, none of whom hold writing or directing credits. That's Variety's reporting on how the season was actually run on the ground; it's a different question from what title Paramount has put on the credits, and the two shouldn't be blurred together.

## What's still unconfirmed

No premiere-date material addresses a fifth season. Separately, Variety has reported that a Season 5 writers' room has opened and that the show may relocate production to New York — but that's development activity, not an official renewal. Paramount has not ordered Season 5, and nothing here should be read as confirmation that Season 4 is anything other than a continuing, ongoing series.

## The timeline, briefly

Paramount renewed *Tulsa King* for Season 4 on September 17, 2025 — before Season 3 had even premiered — with Winter's head writer/EP role announced at the same time. Season 4 production followed through late 2025 and into 2026, wrapping ahead of this week's announcement. Paramount's September 1, 2026 release is what actually set the October 16 date and released the first teaser and photos — putting the premiere about six weeks out from today.
