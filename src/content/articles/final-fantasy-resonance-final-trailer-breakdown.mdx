Square Enix released a new trailer for *Final Fantasy Resonance* during its Tokyo Game Show 2026 spotlight, and it's the fullest look yet at the story driving the game's turn-based HD-2D campaign — reporting that's been corroborated across multiple outlets describes it as an extended cut weaving new narrative scenes together with exploration and battle footage.

## The Story the Trailer Actually Tells

Current reporting on the trailer's footage identifies the central conflict as protagonist **Rain**, alongside allies **Lasswell** and **Fina**, working to protect the world's remaining Crystals from the **Sworn Six**, a group of dark knights bent on destroying them. The trailer's footage reportedly opens on Rain and Lasswell investigating the Earth Shrine, where a Sworn Six member called **Veritas of the Dark** destroys the Earth Crystal — the inciting event that sets the wider conflict in motion. Other Sworn Six members, including Veritas of the Light, Veritas of the Earth, and Veritas of the Flame, have also surfaced in official screenshots, though Square Enix hasn't detailed each one's specific role beyond their shared allegiance to the Sworn Six.

That framing lines up with how Square Enix has described the adaptation more broadly: *Resonance* draws its opening story arc from the mobile game *Final Fantasy Brave Exvius*, and the Rain/Lasswell/Fina trio and the Sworn Six are carried over from that source material rather than invented for this console version. What's new here isn't the underlying cast, but how much of that setup Square Enix is now willing to show ahead of launch.

## A New Theme Song, Confirmed

Square Enix's own trailer premiere confirms the Japanese artist **WurtS**'s song **"INSIDE"** as the trailer's image song — a detail multiple outlets, including Gematsu and Anime News Network, independently reported alongside the trailer's release. That's a marketing confirmation specifically, not something that necessarily plays as the game's actual theme in-game; Square Enix's framing is about the trailer itself.

## Release Date: One Real Wrinkle

*Final Fantasy Resonance* is confirmed for **October 22, 2026** on PlayStation 5, Xbox Series X|S, Nintendo Switch, Nintendo Switch 2, and PC via Steam and the Microsoft Store, priced at $49.99 for the standard edition, with a Digital Deluxe Edition at $59.99 and a Collector's Edition at $209.99. There's one date discrepancy worth flagging directly: Square Enix's Japanese-facing announcement lists the Steam version separately as launching October 23, one day after the other platforms, while Steam's own US storefront currently displays October 22. Reporting attributes that gap to a timezone difference in how the Japan-dated announcement counts the release day rather than an actual delayed PC version — but until Square Enix clarifies it directly in English-language materials, Steam players should treat the exact regional rollout time as the detail still worth double-checking closer to launch.

## What This Trailer Adds to What's Already Confirmed

Square Enix's prior official material — covered in depth in Red Reactions' [Final Fantasy Resonance beginner guide](/articles/final-fantasy-resonance-beginner-guide-visions-stagger-espers/) — already established how the game's core systems work: the visible action timeline, the Stagger mechanic, equippable Visions drawn from classic Final Fantasy characters, Sweeping Stagger and Resonance finishers, and Esper summons. This trailer doesn't overhaul any of that. Its information gain is narrative: it's the first trailer to put the Sworn Six, Veritas of the Dark, and the Earth Shrine incident in front of players as the game's actual inciting conflict, rather than leaving the story premise to a one-line synopsis.

Square Enix has also previously confirmed officially advertised side content players can expect at launch, including a Gilgamesh encounter, a Colosseum, the Chamber of Arms, and an Ultima Weapon superboss — none of which is new to this trailer specifically, but all of which remains current as of this release window. Screenshots accompanying that earlier reveal also showed off HD-2D environments like the clocktower-lined city street above, giving a sense of the scale Square Enix is building beyond the game's opening areas.

## What's Still Unknown

Square Enix hasn't detailed how the individual Sworn Six members beyond Veritas of the Dark factor into the story, what happens after the Earth Crystal's destruction, or how directly the trailer's narrative beats connect to the demo's Prologue-through-Chapter-1 content already available. Nothing here should be read as confirming a boss order, a character's fate, or how the conflict with the Sworn Six ultimately resolves — the trailer sets up the stakes, and reporting on its footage doesn't go further than that.

*Final Fantasy Resonance* launches October 22, 2026, with a free demo already available on every platform covering the Prologue through Chapter 1, and progress carrying over into the full game on the same platform.
