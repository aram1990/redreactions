Sony owned the top of the North American box office this weekend.

*Spider-Man: Brand New Day* stayed at No. 1 for a fourth straight frame with **$39 million**, while *Insidious: Out of the Further* opened at No. 2 with **$25.3 million**. That gave Sony the top two films on the chart with two releases aimed at very different audiences. [Deadline's Sunday estimates](https://deadline.com/2026/08/box-office-insidious-out-of-the-further-brand-new-day-1237047405/) match the final weekend picture reported across the major trades.

## Spider-Man Is Now No. 3 All Time Domestically

*Brand New Day* has reached **$854.9 million in North America**, passing *Spider-Man: No Way Home* and moving into third place on the all-time domestic chart before inflation adjustment.

Only *Star Wars: The Force Awakens* and *Avengers: Endgame* remain ahead of it. The gap to *Endgame* is now only a few million dollars, making second place the next immediate milestone.

Worldwide, the Tom Holland sequel has climbed to roughly **$2.22 billion**. [TheWrap reports](https://www.thewrap.com/industry-news/business/spider-man-insidious-box-office/) that the film is now on course to challenge the small group of releases that have reached $2.5 billion globally.

That run has moved quickly. Earlier this month, we tracked how [*Brand New Day* reached $700 million domestically faster than any previous film](/articles/spider-man-brand-new-day-700-million-box-office/).

## Insidious Opens Below The Red Door, but on a Much Smaller Scale

![Official Insidious: Out of the Further theatrical poster showing a woman facing a red door surrounded by spirits.](/images/articles/spider-man-brand-new-day-insidious-box-office-august-2026/insidious-out-of-the-further-official-poster.jpg)

*Official theatrical poster for Insidious: Out of the Further. Credit: Sony Pictures / Screen Gems / Stage 6 Films / Blumhouse.*

*Insidious: Out of the Further* opened with **$25.3 million domestically from 3,303 locations**. That is below the **$33 million** opening of *Insidious: The Red Door* in 2023, but the comparison looks less alarming next to the new film's reported **$18 million production budget**.

International markets added about **$35 million**, putting the worldwide opening at roughly **$60.5 million**. The film also earned a **C+ CinemaScore**, the same grade as *The Red Door*.

It is too early to call the movie profitable from opening-weekend grosses alone; theatrical revenue is split with exhibitors and marketing costs are separate from the production budget. What the opening does show is that the franchise is still generating a sizable audience without blockbuster-level spending.

## The Odyssey Keeps Breaking Records

Christopher Nolan's *The Odyssey* stayed strong in third place with **$19.5 million** in its sixth weekend, lifting its domestic total to **$539 million**.

That is enough to make it Nolan's highest-grossing film ever in North America before inflation adjustment. Its worldwide total has also reached about **$1.44 billion**, after recently passing *Deadpool & Wolverine* to become the highest-grossing R-rated film worldwide. The [Associated Press](https://apnews.com/article/a0a87cd3b8c8e64952e16ec4e1cc7090) confirmed both the weekend hold and Nolan record.

*PAW Patrol: The Dino Movie* followed with **$9.1 million**, just ahead of *The End of Oak Street* at **$9 million**.

Jason Statham's *Mutiny* opened in sixth with **$7.5 million**. The Lionsgate action film arrived only days after the full movie was [accidentally made available on Prime Video](https://www.thewrap.com/creative-content/movies/jason-statham-mutiny-leaks-on-prime-video/) before being removed. The leak is real; how much it actually cost the theatrical opening is much harder to prove, so it is safer not to treat the two as a direct cause-and-effect result.

The broader theatrical market remains healthy by 2026 standards. Year-to-date domestic grosses have now passed **$7 billion**, running about **20% ahead of the same point in 2025**, according to AP's weekend report.
