Capcom used its September 16 TGS 2026 spotlight to release an extended cut of the *Monster Hunter Wilds: Ascendance* trailer, and it's a genuine information dump rather than a rehash: a returning Elder Dragon, a new hunting mechanic, and a set of gameplay refinements that Capcom says aren't staying exclusive to the expansion. Producer Ryozo Tsujimoto and director Takuro Hiraoka also used the showcase to walk through several of these systems directly, rather than leaving the extended footage to speak entirely for itself.

<YouTube id="EWvjrwfYztQ" title="Monster Hunter Wilds: Ascendance - 1st Trailer | TGS 2026 Extended Cut" />

## Teostra's Confirmed Return

The extended cut confirms **Teostra**, the fire-breathing Elder Dragon that's appeared across the series since *Monster Hunter 2*, as a huntable monster in *Ascendance*. Capcom hasn't detailed Teostra's specific role in the expansion's story or where it fits relative to the other confirmed Elder Dragons — the confirmation, at this stage, is that it's back and huntable, not how or when players encounter it.

Teostra isn't alone. Current reporting also confirms **Lao-Shan Lung** — an Elder Dragon last seen in *Monster Hunter Generations Ultimate*, here said to assault the game's Eastlands region — along with **Gundoraja**, a newly introduced flying Elder Dragon, and **Araketa**, a new bird wyvern described as flying in flocks.

## The Boost Bracer, According to Capcom

<img src="/images/articles/monster-hunter-wilds-ascendance-tgs-trailer/monster-hunter-wilds-ascendance-hud-toughness-display.webp" alt="Side-by-side comparison of the new monster hide toughness display in Monster Hunter Wilds: Ascendance." loading="lazy" />

Capcom's own official site describes the system directly: "Activating the Boost Bracer temporarily enhances certain actions and enables new attacks. Increase your odds of taking down fearsome monsters by activating the Boost Bracer to unleash powerful new attacks known as Boost Drivers and enhance certain actions while boosted." In practice, that means every one of the game's 14 weapon types gets its own enhanced move set once a hunter activates Boost — Capcom's site lists individual videos for each weapon, from Great Sword and Long Sword down to Bow, each showing that weapon's specific **Boosted Actions**.

Capcom hasn't specified an exact resource mechanic for building or maintaining Boost beyond what's shown in the weapon-specific footage, so treat "how you keep yourself boosted mid-hunt" as a detail the individual weapon trailers are still filling in, not something confirmed by the extended cut alone. Capcom has also confirmed the Boost Bracer becomes available specifically as hunters progress into Master Rank, which ties the entire system to the expansion's higher-difficulty content rather than something available from the very first hunt.

## Gameplay Changes — And Which Ones Hit the Base Game Too

This is the detail with the widest reach: Capcom confirmed that several quality-of-life changes shown in the *Ascendance* footage are also coming to the existing *Monster Hunter Wilds* base game, not just the expansion. Specifically:

- **Faster weapon swapping** that no longer requires mounting a Seikret first
- **Equipment changes outside the tent**, handled through the Item Box instead of requiring a trip back to camp
- **More detailed monster hide toughness information**, shown in the comparison footage above

None of Capcom's current materials attach a specific patch date to when these changes land in the base game — they're confirmed as planned, not confirmed for a specific update. That distinction matters for anyone who owns *Monster Hunter Wilds* but hasn't decided whether to buy *Ascendance*: these specific improvements aren't a reason to wait for the expansion, since they're coming either way.

## A New Sky-High Location

<img src="/images/articles/monster-hunter-wilds-ascendance-tgs-trailer/monster-hunter-wilds-ascendance-skybound-eyrie.webp" alt="Floating rock formations in the Skybound Eyrie, a new location in Monster Hunter Wilds: Ascendance." loading="lazy" />

The extended cut also gives a clearer look at the **Skybound Eyrie**, a new area built around floating sky islands rather than the ground-level terrain of the base game's maps. Capcom's official description frames *Ascendance* as continuing the story "years after" the base game, introducing a new stronghold, though the extended cut and current coverage stop short of detailing how the Skybound Eyrie connects to that story or how much of the expansion takes place there versus other locations.

## Platforms and Release Window

*Monster Hunter Wilds: Ascendance* is confirmed for PlayStation 5, Xbox Series X|S, Nintendo Switch 2, and PC via Steam. Capcom's own site currently lists the release simply as "Coming 2027" — no specific month or quarter has been confirmed, so treat any more precise date circulating elsewhere as unsourced until Capcom says otherwise. The base *Monster Hunter Wilds* game separately arrives on Switch 2 on December 4, 2026, ahead of the expansion.

## What's Still Unconfirmed

Capcom hasn't detailed Teostra's story role, the resource system behind maintaining Boost, an exact patch date for the base-game QoL changes, or how the Skybound Eyrie fits into *Ascendance*'s broader map. Nothing in the extended trailer should be read as confirming quest structure, monster difficulty, or story beats beyond what's described above.
