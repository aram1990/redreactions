*Lanterns* opened big for HBO and DC Studios.

The series drew **9.3 million global cross-platform viewers across HBO and HBO Max in its first three days**, according to Warner Bros. Discovery figures reported by [Variety](https://variety.com/2026/tv/news/lanterns-premiere-viewership-9-3-million-hbo-max-1236839414/) and [Deadline](https://deadline.com/2026/08/lanterns-premiere-ratings-viewers-hbo-max-dc-studios-1237046768/). Of that total, **6.6 million viewers were in the U.S.**

That makes *Lanterns* the biggest HBO Max series premiere for a DC Studios show to date and places it among the platform's five biggest series launches. James Gunn marked the result on Threads with a short victory lap: "Biggest DC debut ever. Top 5 all time debuts on HBO."

If you are still catching up, our [*Lanterns* guide](/articles/lanterns-everything-you-need-to-know/) covers the cast, schedule and DCU setup without requiring a stack of Green Lantern homework.

## How Lanterns Compares With Other HBO Launches

The useful comparison is the **6.6 million U.S. three-day figure**, because HBO has reported several recent launches using a similar cross-platform window.

*A Knight of the Seven Kingdoms* remains slightly ahead with **6.7 million U.S. viewers in three days**. *IT: Welcome to Derry* opened to **5.7 million** over its first three days, while *The Penguin* reached **5.3 million U.S. cross-platform viewers over its first four days**.

That last comparison needs context. *The Penguin* later climbed past **10.4 million U.S. viewers after 11 days**, so using that larger total against *Lanterns'* three-day number would mix different measurement windows. The cleaner takeaway is simpler: *Lanterns* started faster than *The Penguin* did in its opening window. [TheWrap's ratings breakdown](https://www.thewrap.com/creative-content/tv-shows/lanterns-dc-premiere-ratings-viewership/) makes the same distinction.

The numbers should also be read as **cross-platform viewing**, not as a traditional single-night Nielsen rating. They combine viewing across HBO and HBO Max during the first three days of availability, so they are useful for comparing recent HBO launches measured the same way, but not for treating the premiere like an old-school live cable audience.

## Strong Reviews, but No Season 2 Renewal Yet

The audience arrived alongside strong reviews. As of August 24, *Lanterns* holds a **94% Tomatometer** on [Rotten Tomatoes](https://www.rottentomatoes.com/tv/lanterns/s01) and a **70 Metascore** on [Metacritic](https://www.metacritic.com/tv/lanterns/).

HBO has **not officially renewed *Lanterns* for Season 2**. The creative team is planning ahead anyway. Showrunner Chris Mundy told [The Hollywood Reporter](https://www.hollywoodreporter.com/tv/tv-features/lanterns-season-2-james-gunn-1236676414/) that he and Christopher Cantwell are already breaking story for a possible second season, while making clear that an HBO pickup has not happened yet.

That distinction matters. A writers' room developing ideas is evidence that the producers want to continue the show; it is not the same thing as a renewal order.

Season 1 has eight episodes and continues weekly through **October 4**. The premiere number is an excellent start, but retention across the rest of the season will tell HBO much more than a three-day launch figure alone.

Aaron Pierre's John Stewart already has a future elsewhere in the DCU: he is set to return in **2027's *Man of Tomorrow***.
