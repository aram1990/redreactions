Nintendo closed today's Direct with *Kirby and the World Beyond*, a new 3D Kirby adventure launching **spring 2027** on **Nintendo Switch 2**, developed by **HAL Laboratory**. Nintendo's own product pages describe it as letting players "freely explore an expansive world" and put Copy Abilities to creative use — notably, Nintendo's marketing does not use the term "open world," even though plenty of headlines already have.

## The Story Nintendo Actually Confirmed

Nintendo's US product listing goes further than the trailer alone suggests: "Rescue a mysterious girl from King Dedede while uncovering secrets about her identity, the strange power Kirby gained, and the mysterious wall blocking his path." That's a real, confirmed premise — a mysterious girl, King Dedede on the opposing side of that specific conflict, a new power Kirby has gained, and a wall or barrier standing between Kirby and the wider world. What Nintendo hasn't explained is who the girl actually is, what her powers do, why Dedede has her, or what's on the other side of that wall — those are the mysteries the game is built around, not settled facts.

## Freely Exploring an "Expansive World"

Nintendo's wording is consistent across its US and UK listings: "freely explore an expansive world." That's a real departure from *Kirby and the Forgotten Land*'s stage-based structure, and the trailer's footage backs it up with sweeping, connected-looking fields rather than discrete levels. But "expansive" and "freely explore" aren't the same claim as "open world" in the technical sense — Nintendo hasn't said whether the world is one seamless space, how areas connect, or whether it uses hub-and-spoke design dressed up to look continuous. Until Nintendo describes the actual world structure, treat the footage as suggesting something broader than *Forgotten Land*, not confirmation of a fully open, seamless map.

## Copy Abilities, Confirmed Uses

Nintendo's own description lists specific, confirmed applications rather than vague "powers": bomb jumps to blast into the air, sword abilities used to climb, and high-speed riding across fields to cover ground. The trailer itself, per outlets who saw it, shows Kirby cycling through Cutter, Bomb, Parasol and Sword abilities during exploration and combat. These aren't just combat tools here — Nintendo is explicitly framing Copy Abilities as traversal mechanics too, which is the more significant confirmed change from prior 3D Kirby games.

## What the Trailer Shows Beyond Nintendo's Own Description

Footage-only details, not confirmed by Nintendo's product text directly: King Dedede appears and is knocked through some kind of barrier, which coincides with a crack opening in the sky above Kirby's world — the moment that seems to reveal there's more beyond it. A floating companion character joins Kirby afterward. It's reasonable to connect this floating companion to the "mysterious girl" from Nintendo's official description, but the trailer doesn't confirm that link outright, so this guide is treating it as a strong likelihood rather than a stated fact.

## Kirby's 35th Anniversary

2027 marks 35 years since the original *Kirby's Dream Land*, and Nintendo has tied *World Beyond*'s spring 2027 launch to that anniversary directly. Beyond the timing itself, Nintendo hasn't detailed any specific anniversary content bundled into the release.

| --- | --- | --- |
| Perspective | Full 3D | Full 3D |
| Structure | Stage-based levels | Free exploration of an expansive world |
| Copy Abilities | Core combat mechanic | Combat plus confirmed traversal uses (bomb jumps, sword-climbing, riding) |
| Platform | Nintendo Switch | Nintendo Switch 2 |
| Release | 2022 | Spring 2027 |

## What Nintendo Still Hasn't Explained

The mysterious girl's identity and why Dedede has her, the nature of Kirby's new power, what's actually beyond the wall, whether the world is truly seamless or built from large connected zones, and multiplayer support — Nintendo's listings don't specify player count. Today's reveal established tone, core mechanics and a real story hook; the specifics are still to come.
