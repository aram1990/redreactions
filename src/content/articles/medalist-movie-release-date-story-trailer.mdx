*Medalist* is getting a theatrical continuation, and it now has a date: **February 19, 2027**, in Japan. The official movie site put up a new "Ultra Teaser" visual and trailer alongside the announcement, centered on the All-Japan Novice Championships, the competition where Inori Yuitsuka and Tsukasa Akeuraji finally face their biggest rivals directly.

## When does Medalist: The Movie come out?

**Japan:** February 19, 2027 (confirmed by the official site).

**Everywhere else:** nothing yet. No international theatrical date, streaming date, or distributor has been announced. Whoever currently streams the TV series in a given territory doesn't automatically hold the movie's rights, so don't assume the film lands wherever you already watch the show.

## What is the movie about?

The official synopsis picks up after Inori and Tsukasa have built a real track record together as skater and coach. They win the Chubu Block Tournament, which earns them a spot at the All-Japan Novice Championships — and that's the point of the film. It's the first time Inori skates directly against Hikaru Kamisaki, the rival she's been chasing, and the first time Tsukasa has to contend with Hikaru's coach, Jun Yodaka, across the boards.

On the day of the competition, rumors circulate during official practice that Inori might attempt a quadruple jump. The synopsis frames it as speculation drawing attention to her, not a confirmed outcome — whether she actually lands one, or attempts one at all, isn't something the official material spells out yet.

The official character profiles add some useful shading to that rivalry without giving away how the competition plays out. Hikaru is described as the previous year's All-Japan Novice champion, built around a high-difficulty jump layout, who's genuinely looking forward to facing Inori head-on rather than dismissing her. Jun, her coach, is a retired skater who won gold at every competition he entered, including the Olympics, and has told Tsukasa outright that he intends for Hikaru to follow the same path. The film's tagline, translated loosely, is that there's no such thing as "absolute" on the ice — a fairly direct signal that the outcome isn't being treated as a foregone conclusion.

## Does this continue after Season 2?

Yes. Japanese reporting around the announcement describes the film as picking up the story after the TV anime's second season, rather than adapting earlier material again. Without spoiling how Season 2 ends, it's fair to say Inori and Tsukasa have advanced far enough by this point to be credible national-level competitors, and the film is built around what happens when that progress puts them face to face with Hikaru and Jun for real.

## Who's returning

The official site confirms the same principal cast from the TV series: Natsumi Haruse as Inori, Takeo Otsuka as Tsukasa, Kana Ichinose as Hikaru, and Yuma Uchida as Jun. Yasutaka Yamamoto directs again, Jukki Hanada returns on script, Chinatsu Kameyama is back as character designer and chief animation director, and Yuki Hayashi composes the music. ENGI, the studio behind the TV series, handles animation for the film as well.

## What we still don't know

Beyond Japan, there's no release plan of any kind yet — no international distributor, no confirmed streaming home, no U.S. or European date. The official site also hasn't published a runtime, a full cast list beyond the four principals, or confirmation of exactly what Inori's practice-session jump attempt turns into during the competition itself. The Japanese date is locked in; what happens once *Medalist: The Movie* looks past its home market is still open.
