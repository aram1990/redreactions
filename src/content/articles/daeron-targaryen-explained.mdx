> **Spoilers** through the *House of the Dragon* Season 3 finale.

For two seasons, *House of the Dragon* had a Targaryen problem it wasn't discussing: Viserys and Alicent had four children, and only three of them were on screen.

Season 3 finally produced the fourth — and then spent eight episodes revealing that Daeron Targaryen's situation was considerably worse than "away at school." He wasn't a prince serving his mother's house. He was a dragonrider being held by it, and by the finale he'd been knighted, crowned in effigy on coins he never asked for, and lost somewhere in a burning town.

## Who he is

Daeron is the youngest of Viserys I and Alicent Hightower's four children — after Aegon II, Helaena and Aemond — which makes him Rhaenyra's half-brother and places him behind Aegon and Aemond among the Greens' surviving male claimants by the end of Season 3.

He was sent to Oldtown as a young child to ward with House Hightower, his mother's family. This is normal Westerosi practice: fostering a younger son cements an alliance and educates him away from court. It also meant Daeron grew up entirely outside the Red Keep, which is the single most important fact about him.

Benjamin Evan Ainsworth plays him — previously Miles in *The Haunting of Bly Manor* and the voice of Disney's live-action Pinocchio, and now cast as Link in the *Legend of Zelda* film.

## Why he was missing for two seasons

He wasn't, exactly. Daeron was named in dialogue in Season 2, when Otto Hightower mentioned him to Alicent, confirming he was in Oldtown all along.

Ryan Condal's explanation for holding him back is practical: the show had no point-of-view characters in Oldtown, so there was nowhere to put him. Introducing a fourth royal sibling in a city the story never visited would have cost screen time for no dramatic return. Once the Hightower host marched north, he became necessary.

Whether that was the right call is the main criticism of the character — a major dragonrider arriving in the penultimate season is a lot to absorb.

## How he's different from Aegon and Aemond

The contrast is the point of him.

Aegon was raised pampered and neglected in King's Landing and turned out volatile and self-destructive. Aemond was raised humiliated in the same building and turned out lethal. Daeron was raised somewhere else entirely, by people with their own agenda, and turned out **careful**.

He's thoughtful, dutiful and visibly frightened most of the time. What Season 3 establishes — and Ainsworth has said as much in interviews — is that Daeron's compliance with Ormund Hightower was self-preservation rather than agreement. He goes along with things because the alternative is being alone with a man who terrifies him.

That's a genuinely different flavour of Targaryen: not mad, not cruel, just cornered.

## Ormund Hightower, and what he wanted

The relationship at the centre of Daeron's season is with **Lord Ormund Hightower** (James Norton), and the show made a significant change here. *Fire & Blood* gives Ormund relatively little interior characterization beyond his role as a Hightower commander alongside Daeron. The show turns that thin outline into something much darker: a domineering guardian who manipulates and controls the prince.

<img src="/images/articles/daeron-targaryen-explained/daeron-targaryen-ormund.webp" alt="Ormund Hightower and Daeron Targaryen in House of the Dragon Season 3" loading="lazy" />

Ormund's plan wasn't to serve Aegon II. It was to make **Daeron** king — and he ran a genuine propaganda campaign to do it, minting coins bearing Daeron's face that circulated up the chain until they reached Rhaenyra herself in Episode 5. Daeron knew exactly how unlikely his accession was. Ormund kept pushing anyway.

He also kept Tessarion caged in Tumbleton for more than half the season. Rider and dragon, both held.

**Ser Gwayne Hightower** (Freddie Fox), Daeron's maternal uncle, is the counterweight. When Gwayne arrives at Tumbleton, Daeron openly begs him to stay so he doesn't have to be alone with Ormund — and Gwayne subsequently saves his life, spotting an assassination attempt disguised as a Baratheon delegation, sent by Rhaenyra.

## Tessarion

Tessarion is Daeron's dragon, the distinctive blue dragon nicknamed the Blue Queen.

She is young compared with monsters such as Vhagar and Vermithor, but she remains one of the Greens' few valuable dragons while Sunfyre is out of action.

For most of Season 3 she also isn't flying anywhere, because she's locked up in Tumbleton.

## What happened at Tumbleton

In the finale, Ormund knights him — **Ser Daeron the Daring** — which is the show delivering the book's epithet under considerably darker circumstances than the original.

Then Daemon's host attacks the town specifically to get at him. Daeron watches the slaughter, can't stand it, and resolves to take wing on Tessarion. Gwayne tries to talk him out of it.

Then Ulf the White turns Silverwing on Tumbleton and burns everything, including — by several accounts of the battle — Ormund himself. Gwayne is knocked unconscious. When he wakes, Daeron is gone.

The last we see of the prince, his uncle is crawling across the rubble calling his name. [Our full finale review](/articles/hotd-s3-finale-review/) covers the wider battle.

## Is he alive?

**Unresolved, deliberately.**

What the show confirms: he survived the initial assault, he was knighted, he intended to mount Tessarion, and he was never shown dead, wounded or captured. Gwayne survived and is looking for him.

What it doesn't confirm: whether he got airborne, where Tessarion is, or whether either of them left Tumbleton.

That's a cliffhanger, not an ambiguity — the show leaves him as a deliberate cliffhanger, one the fourth and final season is now positioned to answer.

## Why he matters for the final season

Strip out the sentiment and Daeron is a serious problem for Rhaenyra.

She has just executed the High Septon, alienated the Faith and lost her last claim to moral authority. Aegon is broken and at Harrenhal. Aemond has no dragon to hand. And out in the Reach there's a young, sympathetic, freshly knighted Targaryen prince whose face is already on coins as a rightful king, with the wealth of Oldtown behind him and a dragon nobody has managed to kill.

He never asked for any of it. That's rather the point of him — and it's why the last thing the season does with him is take him away before we find out what he'd have done with it.
