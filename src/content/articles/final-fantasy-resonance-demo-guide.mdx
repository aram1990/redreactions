*Final Fantasy Resonance*'s demo has been out on every launch platform since September 9, and with the full game arriving October 22, this is a practical checklist for what to actually do with your demo time before then — not a review of the demo itself. For how the game's core combat systems work in detail, see [our full beginner guide](/articles/final-fantasy-resonance-beginner-guide-visions-stagger-espers/).

## Demo Facts at a Glance

- **Available since:** September 9, 2026 on PlayStation 5, Nintendo Switch, Nintendo Switch 2, Xbox Series X|S, and PC (Steam and the Microsoft Store). PS5 players got early access starting September 3.
- **Content covered:** The Prologue through the entire first chapter — Square Enix puts that at roughly 3–5 hours of gameplay.
- **Save transfer:** Confirmed. Square Enix's own announcement states demo save data carries over to the full game at launch.
- **Platform lock:** Save transfer only works within the same platform — a Switch demo save won't carry over to a PS5 purchase, so start the demo on whichever platform you plan to buy the full game on.
- **Full game release:** October 22, 2026 worldwide (Steam's Japanese-facing listing shows October 23; Steam's US storefront currently shows October 22 — [our final trailer breakdown](/articles/final-fantasy-resonance-final-trailer-breakdown/) covers that discrepancy in more detail).

## What Carries Over — And What Square Enix Hasn't Specified

Here's the honest version: Square Enix's official demo announcement confirms that "save data" carries over to the full game, but its public material doesn't itemize exactly what that includes — it doesn't specifically break out whether levels, gold, inventory, Vision progress and settings all transfer individually, or whether "save data" simply means your save file picks up exactly where you left off. Functionally those likely amount to the same thing, but Square Enix hasn't published a line-by-line list, so treat "your progress continues" as confirmed and any more granular claim about specific stats or items as unconfirmed until the full game actually loads that save.

## What to Finish Before Launch

Since the demo covers the Prologue through the end of Chapter 1, there's a natural stopping point already built in — you don't need to rush to "finish" anything beyond reaching that chapter break, since your progress carries over regardless of exactly where within it you stop. What's worth doing deliberately before then:

- Get through the opening story beats at a normal pace rather than rushing, since this content won't need replaying once the full game arrives.
- If Square Enix's demo build includes any optional encounters or side content within Chapter 1, use the demo to try them — anything you do here is time you won't need to spend again later.
- Confirm you're playing on the platform you intend to buy the full game on. This matters more than any in-game task, since a save on the wrong platform won't transfer at all.

## Systems Worth Learning Now

The demo is long enough to actually get comfortable with *Resonance*'s combat loop before the full game's difficulty ramps up. Two systems are worth prioritizing:

**Reading the action timeline.** Every combatant's upcoming turn is visible in advance, and the entire Stagger system is built around disrupting that visible order. Getting used to reading it during the demo's lower-stakes early fights pays off once encounters get harder.

**Experimenting with Visions.** Because abilities a character learns from one Vision stay available even after switching to a different Vision, the demo is a good place to try multiple Vision loadouts without worrying about "wasting" a build — nothing you learn is lost. [Our beginner guide](/articles/final-fantasy-resonance-beginner-guide-visions-stagger-espers/) breaks down exactly how Stagger, Visions, Sweeping Stagger and Resonance connect if you want the full mechanical picture before diving in.

## What Not to Grind

Don't treat the demo as a place to farm levels or gear for their own sake. Since Square Enix hasn't confirmed exactly which numerical progress carries forward, and the demo is capped at the Prologue through Chapter 1 regardless of how much you grind within it, time spent over-leveling for an early-game encounter is time that won't meaningfully change your position once the full game's later chapters open up. The demo is better used for learning the systems than for stockpiling resources against an unconfirmed transfer mechanic.

## Launch Dates and Platform Differences

*Final Fantasy Resonance* launches October 22, 2026 on PlayStation 5, Xbox Series X|S, Nintendo Switch, Nintendo Switch 2, and PC. The one wrinkle: Square Enix's Japanese-facing announcement separately lists the Steam version as October 23, while Steam's US storefront currently shows October 22. Reporting has attributed that to a timezone quirk in how the Japan-dated announcement counts the release day rather than an actual delayed PC version, but Square Enix hasn't clarified it directly in English-language materials — Steam players should double-check closer to launch rather than assume either date.

## What the Final Trailer Newly Confirms

Square Enix's Tokyo Game Show trailer, released September 17, doesn't change anything about the demo itself, but it does confirm the story stakes waiting past where the demo ends: Rain, Lasswell and Fina's fight to protect the world's remaining Crystals from the Sworn Six, beginning with the destruction of the Earth Crystal by Veritas of the Dark. [Our full trailer breakdown](/articles/final-fantasy-resonance-final-trailer-breakdown/) covers what else it reveals — including a new WurtS theme song for the trailer itself and confirmed post-launch side content like a Gilgamesh encounter and an Ultima Weapon superboss.

## Bottom Line

Play the demo at a normal pace through the Prologue and Chapter 1 on the platform you intend to buy, use the extra time to get comfortable with the action timeline and Vision experimentation rather than grinding, and don't stress about exactly what numerical progress transfers — Square Enix has confirmed save data carries over, even if it hasn't itemized precisely what that covers. Everything else is five weeks away.
