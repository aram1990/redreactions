Stephen King's *Carrie* has been a movie four times over. Mike Flanagan's version, premiering October 7 on Prime Video, is the first full series adaptation — eight episodes instead of a two-hour runtime — and the trailer makes clear that extra length is being used to expand the world around Carrie White, not just retell the same beats slower.

## CONFIRMED BY PRIME VIDEO

- All **eight episodes** release together on **Prime Video, October 7, 2026**
- **Mike Flanagan** (*The Haunting of Hill House*, *Midnight Mass*) serves as showrunner, writer and director
- **Summer H. Howell** stars as Carrie White, cast after more than 1,000 actors read for the role
- **Samantha Sloyan** plays Margaret White, Carrie's mother
- Supporting cast includes **Siena Agudong** (Sue Snell), **Alison Thornton** (Chris Hargensen), **Joel Oulette** (Tommy Ross), **Josie Totah** (Tina), **Arthur Conti** (Billy), **Thalia Dudek** (Emaline), **Amber Midthunder** (Miss Desjardin), and **Matthew Lillard** (Principal Grayle)
- The series is billed as the **first-ever series adaptation** of King's 1974 novel

## SHOWN IN THE OFFICIAL TRAILER

Prime Video's own description of the trailer frames it directly: it "expands the world, bringing new depth and complexity to its beloved characters while exploring the forces that shape Carrie White as she navigates the cruelty and pressure of the modern world." The footage shows Carrie isolated in a crowded school hallway as classmates record her on their phones — a modernized version of the novel's locker-room bullying scene that swaps whispered cruelty for viral humiliation.

## Eight Episodes Changes What "Adaptation" Means Here

Every prior *Carrie* has had to compress King's novel — which itself is structured as a fragmented collection of interviews, news clippings and reports assembled after the story's climax — into a two-hour feature. That format has always meant certain characters exist mainly to serve the plot's momentum toward prom night. A full series gives Flanagan room to do what he's built a career on: slow-burn character work, extended family and small-town dynamics, and a larger ensemble getting real screen time before the story's back half kicks in. Margaret White in particular tends to be reduced to a single controlling-parent function in the films; a series format is a real opportunity to build her out as a fuller character rather than a single-note antagonist.

That's a structural fact, not a spoiler — Prime Video's own materials describe the expanded world and character depth directly, without detailing specific new plot content beyond that framing.

## A Modernized Setting, Not a Different Story

The trailer's viral-bullying imagery signals a contemporary setting rather than the 1970s of King's original novel. That's consistent with how prior adaptations have handled the material — the core story of Carrie's abusive home life, her telekinesis, and the prom-night climax has stayed intact across every version regardless of when it's set. Nothing in the confirmed material suggests Flanagan is changing that core arc; the modernization appears to be about how bullying and social pressure are depicted, not a departure from King's plot.

## Mike Flanagan's Fit for the Material

Flanagan's prior work — *The Haunting of Hill House*, *Midnight Mass*, *The Fall of the House of Usher* — has consistently paired horror with grief, guilt and family dysfunction rather than pure scares. *Carrie*'s central relationship, a mother and daughter locked in a cycle of religious control and repression, sits squarely in that territory. It's a more natural match for his sensibilities than a straightforward slasher or supernatural horror premise would be.

## What's Still Unknown

Prime Video hasn't detailed episode-by-episode structure, how far into the story the season goes, or whether any characters or subplots have been added beyond what's implied by the "expanded world" framing. No confirmed episode runtimes or episode titles have been released either. What's certain is the release date, full episode count, and the returning core of King's story — Carrie White, her mother, her telekinesis, and the prom night the novel has always built toward.
