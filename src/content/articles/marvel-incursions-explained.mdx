An incursion is Marvel's word for the worst possible outcome of a multiverse: two Earths drifting into the exact same point in space, on a fixed clock, with only one survivable ending.

The concept comes from Jonathan Hickman's *Avengers* and *New Avengers*, starting in 2012, and it's the mechanism that eventually collapses the entire Marvel multiverse into *Secret Wars* (2015). The MCU has already borrowed the term once, in *Doctor Strange in the Multiverse of Madness*. It has not confirmed that *Avengers: Doomsday* or *Avengers: Secret Wars* adapt Hickman's specific mechanics — but the comics version explains exactly why a title like *Secret Wars* keeps surfacing in connection with a story about universes colliding.

## The simple version

When two parallel Earths get too close to each other, a bordered zone forms at the point where they're about to meet. From that moment, there's a fixed window — eight hours, in Hickman's version — before the two planets physically collide. If the collision happens, both Earths and both of their entire universes are destroyed. The only way to prevent that outcome is for someone to destroy one of the two Earths first, saving one universe by ending the other.

There's no third option in the base mechanic. Reroute the incursion, delay it, negotiate with it — none of that stops the clock. Somebody dies so somebody else doesn't.

## How the Illuminati got involved

Black Panther is the one who first discovers the problem, when a rhinoceros wanders through a portal into Wakanda and he follows it through to find an entire alternate Earth — with a second Earth visibly approaching it in the sky. Realizing the scale of the threat, he reassembles the Illuminati, the covert brain trust of Marvel's most powerful minds, specifically to find a way to survive incursions without simply committing genocide against alternate worlds every time one occurs.

That's the moral engine of the whole Hickman run. The Illuminati spend years searching for a version of "solve this without killing an entire Earth," and mostly fail. Their most notorious tool ends up being an antimatter weapon, built to destroy an incoming alternate Earth cleanly before collision — a solution that still requires deciding, again and again, that one populated world doesn't get to survive.

## Why incursions kept getting worse

Here's the part that only becomes clear late in Hickman's run, and it's worth including because it's the twist that makes the whole crisis make sense: incursions aren't a natural multiversal phenomenon. They're the side effect of an attempted fix.

The Beyonders — god-level entities that predate the current Marvel multiverse — had already decided to end it, and built a living weapon called a Molecule Man into every single reality as their mechanism for doing so. Doctor Doom, operating under the secret identity **Rabum Alal**, discovered this and began killing Molecule Men across timelines early, in an attempt to defuse the Beyonders' plan before it could fully activate. It didn't work the way he hoped. Removing each universe's Molecule Man didn't stop the collapse — it triggered the incursions, pushing neighboring universes into each other in a cascading chain reaction Doom didn't fully anticipate. The secretive all-female order known as the Black Swans served Rabum Alal specifically because they understood, correctly, that he was trying to save the multiverse rather than destroy it — even though the trail of dead universes he left behind made him look like exactly the opposite.

That reveal recontextualizes the entire crisis: the incursions are the visible symptom of Doctor Doom's own failed attempt to outmaneuver a threat bigger than anyone else understood.

## Where it all ends: Secret Wars

The incursions accelerate until they're happening constantly, and the Illuminati's options run out entirely. In the 2015 *Secret Wars* event, Doctor Doom — working alongside Doctor Strange and the Molecule Man — makes a final play: harnessing the power of the dying Beyonders to preserve fragments of the collapsing multiverse instead of letting everything be destroyed, stitching them into a single patchwork world called Battleworld, and ruling over the result as **God Emperor Doom** for eight years. ## How the MCU has already used the term

*Doctor Strange in the Multiverse of Madness* (2022) put the word "incursion" on screen for the first time, tied to the Illuminati of Earth-838 — a different group of heroes on a different Earth who reveal they've already lived through this exact crisis. Their backstory establishes that reckless multiversal travel, like the kind America Chavez's powers or Wanda's dreamwalking enable, risks setting one off, and that they've previously had to make the kind of survive-or-die choice the comics Illuminati faced. It's a direct, deliberate MCU use of Hickman's concept, not just a coincidence of naming.

## What's confirmed for Doomsday and Secret Wars, and what's comic precedent only

Marvel's official synopsis for *Avengers: Doomsday* describes heroes from three distinct universes on a collision course with an existential threat — language that echoes incursions closely enough that the connection is an obvious inference. It is still an inference. Marvel has not stated that *Doomsday* or *Secret Wars* directly adapts Hickman's incursion mechanics, the Illuminati's specific choices, or the Rabum Alal twist. What's actually confirmed is narrower: Robert Downey Jr.'s Doctor Doom, a three-universe collision premise, and a title — *Secret Wars* — that Marvel has used before for exactly this kind of story. ## The bottom line

An incursion is Marvel's cleanest metaphor for a bad multiverse: two worlds, one survivable outcome, and heroes forced to choose. The comics version turns out to be caused by the person most associated with fixing it — Doctor Doom trying to outsmart a threat he didn't fully understand — which is exactly the kind of irony that makes him compelling as *Doomsday*'s central villain, whether or not the films ever use the word "incursion" out loud.
