Batman is teaming up with a Japanese tokusatsu hero for the first time in an official DC comic. At a Batman Day 2026 fan event in Tokyo called "Rule the Night," DC Comics and Toei announced **Batman x Kamen Rider ZERO-ONE LEGENDS UNITE**, an original comic pairing the Dark Knight with **Kamen Rider Zero-One**, backed by planned Bandai merchandise. Here's exactly what that announcement does and doesn't confirm.

## What Was Actually Announced

The project's full styling, per the Japanese-language announcement, is "Batman x Kamen Rider ZERO-ONE LEGENDS UNITE — 日米ヒーロー、新たなる伝説の始まり —," which translates roughly to "Japan-US Heroes, the Beginning of a New Legend." It was revealed September 19, 2026, at the "Rule the Night" Batman Day special fan event in Tokyo, involving DC Comics, Toei, Warner Bros. Discovery Global Consumer Products and Bandai.

What's confirmed: an original comic is being created featuring Batman and Kamen Rider Zero-One together, and Bandai is developing related merchandise. First-look collaboration artwork was unveiled at the event, showing both characters together. **What isn't confirmed:** a release date, whether it's a one-shot or ongoing series, issue count, plot, or a full merchandise lineup. DC's own messaging, per event coverage, is that "project details will be revealed gradually."

## Why Kamen Rider Zero-One?

Toei producer **Shinichiro Shirakura** addressed the pairing directly at the event, calling it a historic moment ("歴史的事件だ" — "a historic incident/event"). Per his own comments, Shirakura pointed to a specific parallel between the two heroes' civilian identities: Bruce Wayne runs Wayne Enterprises, and *Kamen Rider Zero-One*'s Aruto Hiden runs the AI company Hiden Intelligence — both are, in Shirakura's framing, young heads of major corporations leading double lives. That's the connection he cited for selecting Zero-One specifically, rather than any other Kamen Rider era, as Batman's crossover partner.

Shirakura also placed the announcement in a longer franchise history: the original 1971 *Kamen Rider* series featured Shocker monsters explicitly modeled on American pop-culture figures, including kaijin designed after Batman and Spider-Man. Toei later made an official *Spider-Man* tokusatsu series in 1978 — but, per Shirakura, a Batman collaboration had never happened until now. That's a claim about Kamen Rider specifically never crossing over with Batman before, not a claim that this is Batman's first crossover of any kind; DC has paired Batman with outside properties before, including Spawn, Alien and Predator in past comics.

## Confirmed Creative Team

Writer **Kenny Porter** — who has previous DC work on titles including *Batman: Urban Legends*, *DC: Mech* and *Superboy: The Man of Tomorrow* — is attached to script the comic. Artist **tokitokoro**, a Japanese illustrator, is handling art. Neither DC nor Toei has detailed a release date or format for their work beyond the confirmation that the comic is in development.

## What About the Bandai Merchandise?

Bandai's involvement was confirmed as part of the same announcement, framed as a global-scope project rather than Japan-only, but specific products, price points, and confirmed territories haven't been detailed. Don't assume typical Kamen Rider merchandise categories (figures, model kits, henshin belts) apply here until Bandai names actual products.

## Quick Context: Who Is Kamen Rider Zero-One?

For readers unfamiliar with the franchise, *Kamen Rider Zero-One* is the 2019–2020 entry in Toei's long-running *Kamen Rider* tokusatsu series, marking the franchise's Reiwa-era debut. Its lead, Aruto Hiden, inherits his grandfather's AI company and becomes the title Rider to fight a rogue AI threat called Ark. The series is part of a tokusatsu franchise that's been running since 1971 — the same year Shirakura says Shocker's earliest monster designs referenced Batman and Spider-Man.

## What Comes Next

Coverage of the announcement points to future reveals at Tokyo Comic Con and other venues, though DC and Toei haven't published a fixed schedule beyond that. Until then, everything about the comic's plot, format, release window and full merchandise lineup remains unannounced — treat any claims otherwise as speculation rather than confirmed fact.
