Prime Video released the first full trailer for *The Terminal List* Season 2 on September 17, and it's a genuinely different show from the one that ended in 2022 — trading Season 1's contained revenge mission for a globe-spanning conspiracy that puts James Reece on the hunt for someone he once trusted.

<YouTube id="sGmhziz9MoE" title="The Terminal List Season 2 - Official Trailer | Prime Video" />

## Why Reece Is Back in the Field

Season 2 adapts Jack Carr's second novel, *True Believer*, and Prime Video's official material frames it as Reece's "journey of violent redemption" after finishing the list that consumed Season 1. A terrorist attack in London pulls him back into service: Reece is tasked with tracking down Mohammed "Mo" Farooq (Dar Salim), a former Iraqi Special Operations Forces officer described directly by Prime Video as Reece's "former friend and ally." In exchange for taking the assignment, Reece receives a full pardon. That's a meaningful shift in structure from Season 1 — this isn't personal revenge against people who wronged him directly, it's a government asset arrangement, even if the mission still cuts close to home.

## Who Farooq Actually Is

Farooq isn't a new character invented for Season 2 — he was previously introduced in the prequel series *The Terminal List: Dark Wolf*. Prime Video's material describes the London attack as having been carried out using skills Farooq "learned from the CIA's elite Scorpions training program" — specifically, skills Reece himself once taught him. That detail is doing real narrative work: it's not just that Reece is hunting a former ally, it's that the violence he's chasing was built, in part, out of his own training. Treat that connection as confirmed by official synopsis material, not something the trailer's editing alone establishes.

## The Moscow-to-Langley Conspiracy

Beyond Farooq specifically, Prime Video's synopsis confirms a wider conspiracy "that reaches from Moscow to Langley" and ties into Reece's own family history. The season's confirmed geography is genuinely global: Reece's mission moves through the Indian Ocean, Southern and Northern Africa, the Middle East, and Europe. Prime Video hasn't detailed exactly how the family-history thread connects to the Moscow/Langley conspiracy beyond that framing, so treat the specific mechanics of that connection as still unrevealed rather than something the trailer spells out.

## Ben Edwards Is Back — as a Haunting, Not a Resurrection

Taylor Kitsch returns as Ben Edwards, Reece's friend and ally who was killed in the Season 1 finale. His reappearance in Season 2's marketing isn't a retcon: reporting on the trailer describes Edwards' presence as a manifestation of Reece's own troubled mental state rather than the character being alive again. It's a device the show is using deliberately — Edwards' death still stands, and his presence in Reece's head is being used to underline how far Reece hasn't actually moved on since Season 1 ended.

## From Revenge Thriller to Espionage Saga

Prime Video's own language for the shift is direct: Season 1 was a "psychological revenge thriller," while Season 2 expands into a "globe-trotting espionage thriller." That's an official framing, not just critical shorthand — it signals the show is deliberately moving away from repeating its first season's structure rather than just scaling up the same kind of story with a bigger budget.

## Cast, Confirmed

Returning: Chris Pratt as James Reece, Tom Hopper as Raife Hastings, Constance Wu as Katie Buranek, Dar Salim as Mohammed Farooq, and Luke Hemsworth as Jules Landry. New to Season 2: Gabriel Luna as Freddy Strain, alongside Costa Ronin, Olga Kurylenko, Yul Vazquez, Martin Sensmeier, Arnold Vosloo, Jeremy Bobb, and Shiraz Tzarfati. Prime Video hasn't detailed every new cast member's specific role yet.

## Release: All Eight Episodes, One Day

*The Terminal List* Season 2 premieres with all eight episodes at once on **October 21, 2026**, exclusively on Prime Video in more than 240 countries and territories — matching Season 1's same-day rollout rather than a weekly release schedule.

## What's Still Unknown

Prime Video hasn't detailed how directly Season 2 follows *True Believer*'s plot versus adapting it loosely, what specifically ties Reece's family history to the Moscow/Langley conspiracy, or how the new cast additions factor into the mission. Nothing in the trailer should be read as confirming Farooq's ultimate fate, the season's ending, or how closely the show will track the novel's own resolution.
