ABC's new promotional material for *9-1-1: Nashville* Season 2 confirms something bigger than a new cast member: the season's central threat is a criminal recreating the plagues of Egypt across Music City, and Ryan Phillippe's new detective is the one tasked with stopping him.

## Who Is Detective Rogue?

Phillippe plays **Detective Rogue**, described by showrunner Rashad Raisani as a "hard-boiled" homicide detective who relocates to Nashville from New York City. According to Raisani, Rogue is leaving behind cases that were, in his words, "very dark," and arrives in Nashville to lead an investigation the showrunner calls "the case of his life." The early promo footage shows him introducing himself to Captain Don Hart (Chris O'Donnell) at an emergency scene, identifying himself as being "from Metro Police" — brief, visible confirmation of how he enters the story, not a full character introduction.

## The Biblical Plagues Premise, According to Raisani

ABC and Raisani have been direct about what's driving Season 2's larger arc. Raisani has said plainly: "This person is creating the plagues of the Bible to punish Nashville." That's the showrunner's own description of the antagonist's motive and method, not something Red Reactions is inferring from footage.

Raisani has also teased specific disaster set pieces without confirming full episode details: "a horrific swarm" of insects, and separately, "a very 'Rosemary's Baby' thing with blood that's disturbing and amazing." The first of those has already been made concrete in official key art showing Captain Don Hart's face covered by a swarm of murder hornets — the season premiere's central emergency, per Raisani's own account. Treat the hornets as confirmed; treat other plague-style events as things Raisani has teased in general terms rather than shown or fully detailed.

## Man-Made Disasters, Not Supernatural Ones

It's worth being precise about what "biblical" means here. Raisani has framed the criminal arc as giving the season the same "heft" as the natural disasters — hurricanes, floods, lava — that the *9-1-1* franchise typically built its bigger set pieces around, except this time the disasters are engineered by a person rather than occurring naturally. Nothing in Raisani's comments suggests anything supernatural is actually happening in-universe; the "biblical" framing describes the scale and symbolism of the crimes, not their cause. Who that criminal is, and exactly how many "plagues" the season includes, remain unconfirmed.

## What Else Is Confirmed for Season 2

Returning cast includes Chris O'Donnell as Don Hart, Jessica Capshaw as Blythe Hart, Kimberly Williams-Paisley as Cammie Raleigh, LeAnn Rimes as Dixie Bennings, Michael Provost as Ryan Hart, Hunter McVey as Blue Bennings, Hailey Kilgore as Taylor Thompson, and Juani Feliz as Roxie Alba. Raisani has confirmed the season picks up roughly three weeks after Season 1's finale confrontation between Blythe and Dixie, with consequences for both. A crossover with the flagship *9-1-1* series is also planned, expected around episode 8 or 9.

## What Remains Unknown

ABC and Raisani have not confirmed the criminal's identity, motive beyond the general "plagues" framing, how many distinct disaster set pieces the season includes, or how Rogue's investigation resolves. Don't treat any of that as settled based on the promo material released so far.

## Why Rogue Is a Structural Change, Not Just a New Face

*9-1-1: Nashville* has been built around firefighters and paramedics, not detectives — the original *9-1-1* handles the police side of the franchise through Angela Bassett's Athena Grant. Adding a series-regular homicide detective to the Nashville spinoff is a genuine structural addition, not just another guest star for the season's disaster-of-the-week format. It puts an investigative throughline alongside the rescue-driven plots that have defined the show since its first season, which is consistent with how Raisani has described the season's ongoing criminal arc as something the show will return to across multiple episodes rather than resolve in a single premiere.

## Release: October 15 on ABC and Hulu

*9-1-1: Nashville* Season 2 premieres **Thursday, October 15, 2026, at 9 p.m. ET on ABC**, immediately following *9-1-1* Season 10 at 8 p.m. ET, and streams on Hulu.
