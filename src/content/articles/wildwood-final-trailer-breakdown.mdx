LAIKA released the final trailer for *Wildwood* on September 16, timed to tickets going on sale for the studio's sixth stop-motion feature. It's the fullest look yet at Prue McKeel's rescue mission into the Impassable Wilderness, and it clarifies scale and stakes that earlier teasers only hinted at.

## The Rescue Mission, Confirmed

Per LAIKA and Fathom Entertainment's official material, *Wildwood* follows teenager **Prue McKeel** (voiced by Peyton Elizabeth Lee) after her baby brother **Mac** is taken by a murder of crows and a mysterious woman named **Alexandra** into the **Impassable Wilderness** — an enchanted forest hidden just beyond Portland, Oregon. Prue, together with best friend **Curtis** (Jacob Tremblay), enters the forest to get him back. The confirmed premise is a rescue mission specifically, not a generic portal-fantasy adventure; the crows and Alexandra taking Mac is the inciting event driving everything else.

That framing matters for how the film is being sold: this isn't a child discovering a magical world for its own sake, the way many portal-fantasy stories are structured. Prue has a concrete, urgent goal from the opening premise onward, and the Wilderness itself is positioned as an obstacle standing between her and getting her brother back rather than a place she's eager to explore.

## A Forest Built Around Five Factions

The final trailer and LAIKA's official Wildwood site give the clearest picture yet of how the Impassable Wilderness is actually organized. Rather than one unified magical kingdom, the forest is split into named factions: **the Mystics** (North Wood), **the South Wood**, **the Bandit Camp**, **the Avian Principality**, and **the Coyote Army** — five distinct territories arranged around the forest's geography, which the official site describes as centered on a landmark called the Long Gap, with the Ancient's Grove and the Council Tree as other named locations. LAIKA hasn't detailed how these factions relate to each other beyond their names and territories — treat the specific politics between them as something the film itself will establish, not something confirmed by marketing alone.

## What the Final Trailer Clarifies

Earlier Wildwood marketing — the title reveal, teaser trailer, and character-focused featurettes — introduced the world and cast in pieces. The final trailer is the first to put the full shape of the conflict on screen at once: Prue and Curtis moving through multiple distinct environments, armed or armored figures tied to the forest's factions, and a clearer sense that this is a full-scale adventure rather than a contained fable. LAIKA's own framing for the trailer, paired with the "Get Tickets Now" push, positions it as the definitive pre-release look rather than a piece of footage meant to tease further reveals.

## Voice Cast and Creative Team

Travis Knight — LAIKA's president and CEO, and director of *Kubo and the Two Strings* — directs from a screenplay by Chris Butler (*ParaNorman*, *Missing Link*), adapting the novel by Colin Meloy and illustrator Carson Ellis. Alongside Lee and Tremblay, the confirmed voice cast includes Carey Mulligan, Mahershala Ali, Angela Bassett, Awkwafina, and Richard E. Grant, among a larger ensemble.

## Release, Premieres and Premium Formats

*Wildwood* opens in U.S. theaters **October 23, 2026** via Fathom Entertainment, with tickets on sale now. The film will screen in select premium formats where available, including **4DX**, **DBOX**, and **Dolby Cinema**. Ahead of the wide release, *Wildwood* has its world premiere at the **BFI London Film Festival on October 11**, followed by the U.S. premiere as the **opening-night selection of AFI Fest on October 21**.

## What's Still Unknown

LAIKA hasn't detailed Alexandra's motive for taking Mac, how the five factions' politics factor into Prue's journey, or how the story ultimately resolves. Nothing in the trailer should be read as confirming a villain's defeat, an alliance's outcome, or specific magical rules governing the Impassable Wilderness beyond what's shown. Readers who want spoiler-free anticipation should treat the faction names and locations as confirmed world-building, not a roadmap for the plot.
