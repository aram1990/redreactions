LEVEL-5 revealed **Inazuma Eleven: Bold Revolution** during its **LEVEL5 VISION 2026 II "Dream"** broadcast on September 10, 2026, describing it explicitly as **"the full-fledged sequel to Inazuma Eleven: Victory Road."** That's a direct, primary-source confirmation of sequel status — this isn't a spin-off, remaster or ambiguous "new chapter" the way some franchise reveals get hedged.

## What LEVEL-5 confirmed

- **Title**: *Inazuma Eleven: Bold Revolution* (English); *Rekka no Kakumei* (烈火の革命, "Revolution of the Raging Fire") in Japanese
- **Sequel status**: Explicitly called the full-fledged sequel to *Inazuma Eleven: Victory Road*
- **New protagonist**: Furuhoshi Nagi in Japanese, localized as **Shane Starfall** in English
- **Returning character**: *Victory Road* protagonist Sazanami Unmei — **Destin Billows** — is confirmed to return
- **Story link**: LEVEL-5 teased that Shane and Destin will meet during Bold Revolution's story, directly connecting the two games' casts

## What LEVEL-5 has not confirmed yet

LEVEL-5 did not announce a **release window or platform list** at the Vision 2026 II reveal. Company president Akihiro Hino said more details — including, by some outlet accounts, what "Bold Revolution" actually refers to within the story — will come at LEVEL-5's next Vision event.

It's also worth being precise about **data transfer**. Back in January 2026, Hino confirmed he'd begun writing the sequel's scenario, and *Victory Road*'s Player Bank feature was described at the time as designed to carry recruited characters, gear and stats forward into "the then-unannounced sequel." Now that Bold Revolution has been named as that sequel, LEVEL-5 has not yet explicitly reconfirmed that the Player Bank promise applies to Bold Revolution specifically — the January statements were made before this game had a name, and the September reveal didn't repeat or confirm that detail. Treat it as likely but not yet officially restated.

## The context: Victory Road and Hino's January announcement

*Inazuma Eleven: Victory Road* launched November 13, 2025. In January 2026, Hino confirmed during an InaDai program that he'd started writing a sequel — making it, per Automaton West's reporting at the time, the first mainline Inazuma Eleven story written in the Reiwa era, since Victory Road's roots traced back to the *Inazuma Eleven Ares* project that began development years earlier. That January announcement is what Bold Revolution now formally delivers on.

## Why the careful wording matters

LEVEL-5 franchises have a history of games that look like direct sequels but ship as expansions, spin-offs, or mobile-first releases with different scope than their mainline predecessors. Here, the "full-fledged sequel" language is unambiguous and comes straight from the reveal itself — so Red Reactions is comfortable reporting Bold Revolution as a genuine sequel to Victory Road. What we're not doing is assuming its platforms, release date, or exact data-transfer mechanics until LEVEL-5 says so directly. We'll update this article once that information is confirmed.
