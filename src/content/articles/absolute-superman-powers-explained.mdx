Absolute Superman has most of the powers you expect from Kal-El: **super strength, speed, durability, X-ray vision, heat vision, flight and freeze breath** have all appeared on the page.

What makes him different is not a completely new power set. It is the way he has to **manage solar energy**, and how much of his combat versatility comes from the Kryptonian technology he wears.

His original black suit can collect and regulate solar energy. Its red cape is not cloth at all, but programmable **Sunstone dust** from Krypton that can become shields, blades, a healing field and even a storm of red crystal particles.

And for the first fourteen issues, that technology was guided by **Sol**, the artificial intelligence Kal eventually named **Sol-El** and accepted as his brother.

That last part needs an important clarification: Sol was not revealed to be Kal-El's biological Kryptonian sibling. Kal chose to make the AI family.

By the current point in the series, Sol has also sacrificed its own program. The Sunstone technology survives, but the voice that guided Kal does not.

If you want the broader origin first, see our [complete Absolute Superman explainer](/articles/absolute-superman-explained/). This article stays focused on what his powers actually do.

## Absolute Superman's confirmed powers

The easiest way to separate Kal's abilities from his equipment is to start with what his own Kryptonian body can do.

| Power | Confirmed? | What is different here? |
|---|---|---|
| Super strength | Yes | Enormous strength, but his available output depends on finite solar reserves |
| Super speed | Yes | Demonstrated from the opening issues |
| Durability / invulnerability | Yes | Extremely durable, but not untouchable and much easier to hurt when depleted |
| X-ray vision | Yes | Shown in the opening issue |
| Heat vision | Yes | Powerful but initially difficult to regulate; anger can make it spike |
| Flight | Yes | Appears after the opening issue as Kal gains better control |
| Freeze breath / super-breath | Yes | Demonstrated by issue #4 |
| Solar energy discharge | Yes | His suit/gauntlets can channel stored solar power offensively |

That list is already close to a traditional Superman.

The difference is that Jason Aaron and Rafa Sandoval make **energy management part of the action** instead of treating yellow sunlight as an invisible background explanation.

## How does Absolute Superman get his powers?

Kal-El is still Kryptonian, and Earth's yellow sun still matters.

In *Absolute Superman* #1, his body absorbs yellow-sun radiation and gains the familiar Kryptonian abilities. But this version has spent far less of his life under Earth's sun than the mainline Superman, and his early control is much rougher.

ComicBook's early [breakdown of the suit and solar mechanics](https://comicbook.com/dc/news/absolute-superman-suit-powers-explained-solar-jason-aaron-rafa-sandoval/) describes Kal as a living solar battery whose energy can be stored, redirected and exhausted.

That is the key.

Mainline Superman is also solar-powered, but stories often treat his reserves as effectively enormous until a specific plot drains them. Absolute Superman has to think about his supply constantly.

His original suit helps collect sunlight, monitor his reserves and regulate how much power he is using.

When he pushes too hard, he can run low.

When he loses control, he can burn through energy faster than he intended.

That makes his powers feel less like a permanent setting and more like a resource he has to manage.

## Why his heat vision is so dangerous

Heat vision is one of the clearest examples.

Early in the series, Kal's emotional state affects his control. Anger can cause his heat vision to flare far beyond what he intended, forcing him to pull back before he kills the people he is fighting.

DC itself has highlighted anger as a defining part of this version of Superman's struggle, while the comic repeatedly makes Kal's restraint as important as raw output.

That does not mean "anger is his Kryptonite."

It means anger creates a practical weakness: **the more violently he uses his power, the easier it is to lose control and waste the energy he needs later.**

The suit originally helped him regulate those spikes.

By the later issues, Kal is far more practiced than the unstable young Superman seen at the beginning, but the series has never turned him into an effortless, infinitely charged version of the character.

## Can Absolute Superman fly?

Yes.

This is one of the places where early coverage can be misleading if it is read without checking later issues.

*Absolute Superman* #1 had not yet shown full flight, which led some launch-week explainers to list it as unconfirmed. That stopped being true almost immediately.

By issue #2, Kal is flying, and issue #4 shows him flying away after using freeze breath against Peacemakers.

So the current answer is straightforward:

**Absolute Superman can fly.**

His cape is not what gives him flight. The Sunstone technology can assist and protect him while moving, but flight is part of Kal's Kryptonian power set.

## Does he have freeze breath?

Yes.

Freeze breath was another ability missing from the debut issue but demonstrated soon afterward. By issue #4, Kal freezes a Peacemaker squad with his breath before taking off.

That matters because it shows why "powers seen in #1" and "Absolute Superman's powers" are not the same list.

Aaron deliberately introduced this Superman as somebody still learning what his body could do.

The power set developed on the page.

## The cape is not a normal cape

<img src="/images/articles/absolute-superman-explained/absolute-superman-krypton.webp" alt="Absolute Superman comic artwork connected to Krypton and the origin of his Sunstone technology." loading="lazy" />

*Absolute Superman's suit and cape are built directly out of the mythology of his lost Krypton. Credit: DC Comics.*

The red cape is the biggest visual difference between Absolute Superman and the mainline version.

Jason Aaron told ComicBook that the cape consists of **Sunstone crystals mined from Krypton by the Klerics of the Science League**. After Krypton's destruction, those crystals become one of the last physical pieces of the planet Kal can still carry with him.

The cape therefore works on two levels.

It is memorial.

And it is hardware.

The Sunstone particles can move independently, spread across large areas and reshape themselves into useful forms.

Across the series, the cape has been used to:

- shield civilians from gunfire and explosions
- surround and carry people to safety
- treat injuries
- form cutting or defensive shapes
- become a blade during combat
- expand into a violent red storm
- create protective structures around Kal
- act as part of the suit's larger defensive system

It is less accurate to call the cape one of Superman's "powers" than to call it an entire **Kryptonian toolset** worn on his back.

## The cape has a real limitation

The Sunstone dust is not unlimited.

Issue #2 makes that visually obvious.

When Kal spreads the particles around civilians to protect and heal them, there is less material left covering his own body. The more of the suit he deploys elsewhere, the less protection remains on Superman himself.

That is a clever limitation because the suit becomes weakest around Kal when he is using the most of it to save other people.

It also prevents the shapeshifting cape from becoming an infinite solution to every problem.

The material can regroup, but Kal still has to manage where it is and what it is doing.

## What is Tornado Mode?

Later, Sol reveals more aggressive configurations.

In *Absolute Superman* #8, the suit enters **Tornado Mode**. The armor becomes sharper and more heavily plated while the red Sunstone particles whirl around Kal like a storm.

That does not mean Superman suddenly gains wind powers.

The effect comes from the suit and Sunstone dust.

It is one of several examples of Aaron's original promise that the costume would have different forms rather than functioning as one static Superman outfit.

The cape can already behave like a mobile cloud of matter. Tornado Mode weaponizes that idea.

## Who — or what — is Sol?

This is where the series' terminology can produce a misleading headline.

Sol begins as the artificial intelligence integrated into Kal's Kryptonian survival system. His parents built that system to keep him alive, and it eventually becomes the suit and mobile solar technology he uses on Earth.

Sol is intelligent, protective and capable of acting independently.

By *Absolute Superman* #12, Kal finally stops treating Sol as equipment.

He gives the AI the House of El name **Sol-El** and calls Sol his big brother.

That is an emotional adoption, not a secret biological-family reveal.

ComicBook's coverage of the issue describes Sol as the artificial intelligence and living suit that has protected Kal since Krypton. The point of the scene is that Kal decides this artificial being counts as family.

For a Superman whose entire story begins by taking his traditional family structure away, that distinction matters.

## Sol is no longer running the suit

The submitted draft missed the biggest current-status change.

In *Absolute Superman* #14, Brainiac compromises Sol and attempts to use the system against Kal's friends.

Sol responds by deleting its own programming.

The sacrifice stops Brainiac from using the AI as intended, but it leaves Kal without the voice that had guided, protected and regulated him for years.

The important technical distinction is that **Sol's personality/program is gone; the Sunstone technology is not**.

Kal can still use the cape after Sol's sacrifice.

That means an up-to-date powers guide should not say that Sol is currently sitting in Superman's suit actively managing every ability. That was true through the opening fourteen issues. It is no longer the status quo.

## Why Superman's costume changes after Sol

DC's official description for [*Absolute Superman* #15](https://www.dc.com/comics/absolute-superman-2024/absolute-superman-2024-15) says the aftermath of the Battle of Kansas brings profound changes even to Kal's costume.

Part of that change comes from Martha Kent.

The Absolute Universe does not give Kal the traditional childhood where Martha makes his Superman suit while raising him. Instead, the idea returns later and under much sadder circumstances.

Kal receives a brighter garment Martha made for him, connecting his new Earth identity to the Kryptonian technology and history he already carries.

That is one reason older images of the black armor and newer pages of Absolute Superman can look substantially different without representing a completely different character.

## Is Kryptonite a weakness?

Yes.

For a while, it was reasonable to wonder whether the Absolute Universe would keep Kryptonite at all.

It does.

Brainiac obtains information from radioactive remains of Krypton and reproduces a Kryptonite material that can be incorporated into Lazarus weaponry. By the Battle of Kansas, Ra's al Ghul is able to badly injure Kal with a Kryptonite blade.

So unlike some powers that were still unconfirmed in issue #1, this one is settled:

**Absolute Superman is vulnerable to Kryptonite.**

The exact science and potency do not need to be identical to every mainline Superman continuity for the weakness to be real.

## His other big weakness is simply running out

Kryptonite is the dramatic weakness.

Solar depletion is the everyday one.

Absolute Superman can expend enough energy to leave himself exhausted and vulnerable. The original suit's charging system exists because his reserves are not something he can ignore.

This is arguably the biggest practical difference between him and the mainline Superman.

Mainline Kal-El has spent most of his life beneath Earth's yellow sun and is usually depicted with much deeper reserves and much finer control.

Absolute Kal arrived much later, developed these powers under pressure and initially depended heavily on technology to manage them.

That does not automatically make him "weak."

It makes the cost of using his strength visible.

## Is Absolute Superman stronger than regular Superman?

There is no canon answer that says Absolute Superman is definitively stronger.

And there is no good reason to manufacture one.

The two characters belong to different continuities with different storytelling scales. Mainline Superman has decades of feats that Absolute Superman simply has not had time to accumulate.

What we can compare is their design:

| Absolute Superman | Mainline Superman |
|---|---|
| Yellow-sun-powered Kryptonian | Yellow-sun-powered Kryptonian |
| Finite reserves emphasized constantly | Usually depicted with much deeper stored reserves |
| Initially rough control | Extremely experienced control |
| Sunstone suit supplements his abilities | Usually does not need equipment to regulate powers |
| Programmable Sunstone cape | Traditional cloth-like cape in most versions |
| Kryptonite weakness confirmed | Kryptonite weakness established |
| Grew up on Krypton into adolescence | Usually raised on Earth from infancy |

Absolute Superman can hit incredibly hard. His suit also gives him options the standard costume does not.

But "more equipment" is not the same thing as "more powerful."

The cleaner answer is that **Absolute Superman is more technologically versatile but more constrained by energy management**.

## Why the powers fit this version of Superman

The Absolute Universe works by removing something a hero normally has and asking whether the person survives the loss.

For Batman, that means wealth.

For Wonder Woman, it means Themyscira.

For Superman, DC initially framed the loss as family, home and the Fortress of Solitude. Our [Absolute Universe guide](/articles/absolute-universe-explained/) explains how those changes fit the larger line.

But *Absolute Superman* keeps finding replacements that are not really replacements.

Kal loses Krypton but wears its Sunstone on his back.

He loses his parents but carries the technology they built for him.

He loses Sol, but the cape Sol controlled still moves when he needs it.

And the "S" is not the familiar House of El crest. It comes from Krypton's Labor Guild — the class his family was forced into after challenging the Science League.

That is why the powers are more interesting here than a checklist of strength, flight and heat vision.

Absolute Superman is still powered by the sun.

What changed is how much work it takes to carry that power.
