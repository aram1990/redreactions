Chris Hansen says legal professionals are looking at whether he has a case against A24 over *Primetime*, the Robert Pattinson-led dramatization of his *To Catch a Predator* years. No lawsuit has been filed. Hansen has been careful to describe it as something under consideration rather than a legal fight already underway, but his position has clearly moved past simply reacting to a trailer.

That reaction started back when the film was first announced. Speaking on Annie Elise's *Serialously* podcast, the former *To Catch a Predator* host said the trailer for A24's *Primetime* looked "very dramatic" and openly questioned the industry's motives for turning his work into a feature film. As [Entertainment Weekly reported](https://ew.com/chris-hansen-reveals-who-hed-want-to-play-him-instead-of-robert-pattinson-12062640), Hansen said he had not been involved in the production and had not chosen Pattinson to play him.

He didn't dismiss the movie outright at that point. Hansen said he wanted to see the finished film before judging it and had been invited to a private screening. That screening didn't go as planned, and what Hansen has said since has gone considerably further than a wait-and-see reaction to a trailer.

## Hansen says legal professionals are looking at his options

Talking to Dan Abrams on *The Dan Abrams Show*, Hansen said of potential legal action: "People are looking at it." He described an entertainment lawyer telling him he "probably has a decent case," while also raising the more practical question of how much time Hansen would actually want to put into pursuing it. That's a lawyer's assessment relayed secondhand by Hansen, not a filed claim or a formal legal opinion Red Reactions has independently reviewed.

Hansen's underlying objection is about how the film uses his identity, not just how it dramatizes events. He told TheWrap it "feels very exploitive" to him, arguing A24 used his real name for a project he says it didn't need his name to make. That's Hansen's characterization of the film's approach; *Primetime* is a dramatization inspired by real events, and dramatizations are generally allowed to fictionalize material, but Hansen's complaint is specifically about how closely the film ties that fictionalized version to his actual identity.

## Hansen calls Pattinson's imitation "overly dramatic"

Speaking to Entertainment Tonight, Hansen gave his fullest response yet to the film, still based only on the trailer and other promotional material rather than the finished cut. He called Pattinson's impression "overly dramatic," while allowing that "there's a pretty good imitation there in the trailer." He said his own wife and family are more convinced by the performance than he is.

Hansen's bigger objection is to the writing, not the acting. He said the film puts words in his mouth and depicts him doing things he never did, telling ET that "the conversations you heard in the trailer never took place in real life." His stated bottom line: "This is a work of fiction," and he wants audiences to treat *Primetime* accordingly — a dramatization inspired by *To Catch a Predator*, not a documentary reconstruction of it.

## The private screening he didn't watch

Hansen did go to the planned A24 screening, but he never saw the movie. According to Deadline's reporting, he was asked to sign a non-disclosure agreement before being let into the theater, had his attorneys review it beforehand, and declined to sign after they advised against it. He was turned away without watching the film.

Hansen has characterized the agreement as unusually broad, saying the studio's lawyers wanted him to "sign away my rights to my name, image and brand essentially, as well as any potential legal rights that would come out of the movie," and describing it as unlike anything he'd encountered in decades covering the story. A source close to A24 disputes that framing, telling Deadline that Hansen was asked to sign the same standard NDA required of any preview audience for an unreleased film — intended to prevent plot details from leaking rather than to make him give up rights to his name, image or brand. Both accounts remain in direct conflict, and neither side has produced the document itself publicly.

## Hansen once had a very different actor in mind

The strangest detail in Hansen's reaction is that Pattinson was nowhere near his own fantasy casting.

Hansen said he had previously worked on a script based on his story and had discussed **Denis Leary** as someone who could play him. That project never became *Primetime*, which was developed separately with Pattinson in the lead.

The contrast is part of what makes the current situation unusual: Hansen is watching a dramatized version of his most famous work arrive without having shaped the movie himself.

A24's [official film page](https://a24films.com/films/primetime) keeps the premise deliberately simple: "In 2006, *To Catch a Predator* host Chris Hansen sets out to make television history."

## Primetime heads to Venice before its theatrical release

*Primetime* is directed by **Lance Oppenheim**, making his narrative feature debut after documentary projects including *Some Kind of Heaven* and *Ren Faire*. A24 lists Robert Pattinson, Merritt Wever, Skyler Gisondo, Phoebe Bridgers, Matthew Maher and Bokeem Woodbine among the cast.

The film is also set to compete at the 2026 Venice Film Festival before opening in U.S. theaters on **September 25**. [Variety's trailer coverage](https://au.variety.com/2026/film/global/primetime-trailer-robert-pattinson-to-catch-predator-39205/) says the movie focuses on a controversial period in the history of *To Catch a Predator*, which ran as part of *Dateline NBC* during the 2000s.

## Hansen is also advertising TruBlu before Primetime screenings

Separately from the legal question, Hansen has bought advertising space for TruBlu, his true-crime network, to run before *Primetime* showings. TheWrap reports the ad buy covers at least the first four weeks of the film's theatrical run, playing in front of every screening rather than a limited rollout. The spot reportedly features Hansen delivering one of his familiar catchphrases and a QR code pointing viewers to TruBlu's own crime programming. It's a notable detail alongside everything else: Hansen is questioning how *Primetime* uses his name commercially while also using the audience that shows up to see it to promote his own brand.

Hansen still hasn't watched the finished film. The private screening that might have changed that fell apart over the NDA dispute, and nothing since suggests he's seen it another way. What's changed is that he's no longer simply reserving judgment. He's on record calling Pattinson's impression overly dramatic while conceding it's a fairly convincing one, he's drawn a clear line between what he says actually happened and what the film puts on screen, and he says legal professionals are looking at whether that adds up to a case. None of that has turned into an actual lawsuit as of this writing, and Hansen's own comments suggest that's still an open question for him too.
