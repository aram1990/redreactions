Spyro has always been able to glide. **Spyro: A Realm Beyond** is the first time he's actually been able to fly — and Toys for Bob is building the game's exploration and level design around that difference, not just adding it as a bonus move.

## CONFIRMED BY TOYS FOR BOB / ACTIVISION

- Developed by **Toys for Bob**, published by Activision
- An **all-new adventure**, not a remake or remaster of prior Spyro games
- Coming to **Xbox Series X|S, PS5, Nintendo Switch 2, Steam, Epic Games Store and Xbox PC**
- Targeting a **Spring 2027** release window
- **Arcadian Vale** confirmed as an early region in the game, described in official material by its waterfalls, stone structures and greenery
- Spyro gains **full free flight**, distinct from the glide-and-jump traversal of the original trilogy and the Reignited Trilogy remaster

## What "Full Flight" Actually Means Here

The classic Spyro move set was jump, glide, and land — momentum bled off quickly, and glide was fundamentally a controlled fall. Toys for Bob's own description of A Realm Beyond's flight is a different vocabulary entirely: flapping his wings builds speed and momentum, diving increases speed further, and tight turns let him thread through obstacles rather than just aim for a landing spot. That's powered, sustained flight, not an extended jump.

The studio has also confirmed a chaining mechanic tied to flight — stringing moves together is described as the actual challenge of traversal, which suggests flight isn't meant to be a shortcut past platforming so much as a new kind of platforming in its own right.

## Fire Breath Works Differently in the Air

One of the more specific confirmed details: Spyro's signature fire breath changes behavior depending on whether he's grounded or airborne. On the ground, it behaves like the flamethrower players already know. In the air, it becomes a ranged fireball attack instead. That's a real mechanical split, not just a visual flourish, and it implies combat encounters are being designed with both states in mind rather than treating flight purely as traversal.

Fire also has a confirmed traversal use: igniting campfires to create lift. Sky rings are confirmed too, functioning as speed boosts along a flight path — which, combined with the campfire updrafts, points toward routed aerial sequences built specifically around flight rather than open improvisation.

## Arcadian Vale as a Test Case for Vertical Design

Arcadian Vale is confirmed as one of the game's early regions, and Toys for Bob's own description leans on waterfalls, stone structures and greenery — the kind of terrain that reads as built for vertical movement rather than a flat plane with scattered platforms. That's not a stated design philosophy from the studio, but it's a reasonable inference from what they've chosen to describe: a region defined by height and layered structures is a natural place to introduce a traversal tool defined by height and momentum.

Treat that connection as analysis, though, not a confirmed feature. Toys for Bob hasn't said Arcadian Vale was specifically built to showcase flight, how large the region is, or whether flight is available from the moment you arrive there.

## How This Compares to the Reignited Trilogy

Toys for Bob has been explicit that A Realm Beyond builds upon the control feel established in Spyro Reignited Trilogy rather than discarding it. Free flight is described as adding new ways to explore, not replacing ground movement — Spyro still runs, jumps, and charges the way longtime players expect. The studio and its marketing have also emphasized that Spyro's tone and voice are being preserved rather than rebooted; this is a new story, but not a reinvention of the character.

That distinction matters for expectations: A Realm Beyond isn't positioning itself as a totally different kind of game built around flight. It's positioning flight as the biggest addition to a formula that's otherwise recognizably Spyro.

## What's Still Unknown

A lot of the specifics that would tell you what actually playing this feels like haven't been confirmed:

- **World structure** — whether regions like Arcadian Vale are separate levels, open hubs, or something more continuous hasn't been detailed. Nothing here has been called open-world or seamless by Toys for Bob, and it shouldn't be assumed.
- **Flight limits** — there's no confirmed stamina system, altitude ceiling, or cooldown, but there's also no confirmation flight is unrestricted. Both possibilities remain open.
- **Progression** — how flight is unlocked, upgraded, or gated across the story is unconfirmed.
- **Combat beyond fire breath** — melee options, enemy variety, and boss design haven't been detailed.
- **Performance targets** — no confirmed resolution or frame rate across the five confirmed platforms.
- **Switch 2-specific features** — whether the Switch 2 version uses any hardware-specific input or performance modes hasn't been addressed.

Spring 2027 is still several months out, and Toys for Bob has a lot of room to fill in these blanks before launch. For now, the confirmed picture is narrower but more concrete than most reveal-trailer coverage: real powered flight, a distinct airborne fire attack, and a first region built in a shape that suggests the studio is taking that seriously.
