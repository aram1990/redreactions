*Spider-Man: Brand New Day* has crossed **$900 million** at the North American box office — only the second film in history to do it, after *Star Wars: The Force Awakens*. It got there in roughly **36 days**, faster than *The Force Awakens*' own **50-day** pace to the same mark back in 2015. Sony's Spider-Man sequel remained the No. 1 movie in the country through the four-day Labor Day weekend, with industry estimates putting its running domestic total at roughly **$921–922 million** as of Monday, September 7. *The Force Awakens*' unadjusted domestic record of **$936,662,225** is now within striking distance — current estimates put the gap at somewhere around **$14–16 million**, with the exact figure still moving as studios finalize the holiday frame.

## How It Got Past Endgame

*Brand New Day* passed *Avengers: Endgame*'s 2019 domestic total of $858,373,000 on Monday, August 24, moving into second place on the all-time North American chart, unadjusted for inflation. That made it the No. 2 domestic release of all time behind only *The Force Awakens* — ahead of every other Marvel release and every other film except one.

## Crossing $900 Million

Going into Labor Day weekend, *Brand New Day* sat just under $900 million and crossed the mark during the holiday frame, according to Deadline and Variety. The 36-day pace to $900 million is faster than any film has ever managed it, including *The Force Awakens*' own 50 days. That $900 million is a confirmed gross, not a projection.

## The Record Still Ahead of It

*The Force Awakens* has held the all-time unadjusted domestic record since December 2015, finishing its original run at $936,662,225. Against the Labor Day estimate above, that's a gap of roughly $14–16 million — small enough that surpassing the record now looks like a matter of when, not if, but it hasn't happened yet. Treat that Labor Day figure as an estimate until studios report final, audited numbers.

It's worth being precise about which record this is. This is the North American, unadjusted-for-inflation comparison specifically. Adjusted for inflation, *The Force Awakens* sits much further ahead, and nobody currently claims *Brand New Day* is challenging that version of the record. Worldwide, *Brand New Day* has already cleared roughly $2.33 billion — a different total answering a different question from the domestic chart being discussed here.

## Whether It Can Actually Close the Gap

*Brand New Day* has now led the box office for six straight weekends, including Labor Day weekend against *Coyote vs. Acme*'s strong second-weekend hold. That kind of extended run at No. 1 is exactly the condition needed to close a gap this size — the film doesn't need a big new weekend, just continued wide release and a normal, gradual decline rather than a sudden drop-off.

*Avengers: Endgame* itself is also returning to theaters September 25 as *Endgame Encore*, an interesting wrinkle for Marvel's own box office history but not something that affects *Brand New Day*'s chase of *The Force Awakens* — a theatrical re-release doesn't retroactively change a film's original box office total. For more on what that re-release actually includes, see our [Endgame Encore explainer](/articles/avengers-endgame-encore-explained-doomsday/).

## Why the Record Matters

If *Brand New Day* passes $936.66 million domestically, it becomes the highest-grossing movie in North American box office history in nominal dollars — a record *The Force Awakens* has held for almost eleven years. [It hit $700 million domestically faster than any film in history](/articles/spider-man-brand-new-day-700-million-box-office/) back when this run first started drawing attention, and [it was still building toward this position as recently as its fourth weekend](/articles/spider-man-brand-new-day-insidious-box-office-august-2026/).
