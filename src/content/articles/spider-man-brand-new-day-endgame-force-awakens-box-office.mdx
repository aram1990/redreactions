*Spider-Man: Brand New Day* is now within roughly **$14 million** of *Star Wars: The Force Awakens*' all-time North American box-office record. Sony's estimate for the four-day Labor Day weekend puts the film's running domestic total at about **$923 million**, after a sixth consecutive weekend at No. 1 — a rare feat AP notes only one other film has managed this decade. *The Force Awakens* still holds the unadjusted domestic record at **$936,662,225**, set over its original 2015–16 run. On the current estimate, that's a gap of roughly **$13.7 million**. TheWrap is now projecting *Brand New Day* will move past that total by next weekend, but that's a forecast — the record hasn't been broken yet, and the $923 million figure itself is still a studio estimate, not an audited final.

## How It Got Here

*Brand New Day* passed *Avengers: Endgame*'s 2019 domestic total of $858,373,000 on August 24, moving into second place on the all-time North American chart. It then became the fastest film ever to reach $900 million domestically, doing it in roughly 36 days against *The Force Awakens*' own 50-day pace to the same mark in 2015. From there, six straight weekends at No. 1 — including Labor Day weekend against *Coyote vs. Acme*'s second-weekend hold — have chipped the remaining gap down from around $45 million after its fifth weekend to about $14 million now.

## Estimate, Not Final

Treat the $923 million domestic figure as what it is: a four-day studio estimate reported over Labor Day weekend, not a confirmed actual. Studios routinely revise holiday estimates once final numbers come in, and a swing of a few million dollars either way wouldn't be unusual — the remaining gap will move with any revision. TheWrap and AP are consistent on both the $923 million figure and the six-weekend streak, but consistent reporting still isn't the same as an audited final.

It's also worth being precise about which record is in play: the North American, unadjusted-for-inflation domestic record specifically — not worldwide, not inflation-adjusted, not total tickets sold. Adjusted for inflation, *The Force Awakens* remains far ahead, and nothing here challenges that version. Worldwide, *Brand New Day* has separately passed $2.4 billion, per AP — a different total answering a different question from the domestic chase tracked here.

## A $14 Million Gap Is a Different Story Than a $45 Million One

A week ago, closing the gap meant *Brand New Day* needed something close to another full blockbuster weekend. At roughly $13.7 million, that's no longer true — it just needs enough ordinary theatrical business over the coming days to cover what's left. That's why TheWrap is now framing the record as something the film is likely to pass by next weekend rather than a longer chase. Likely isn't the same as done, though, and this article will update once Sony or Comscore report the confirmed number.

*Avengers: Endgame* is also returning to theaters September 25 as *Endgame Encore* — a separate wrinkle in Marvel's own box office history that doesn't affect this specific record chase, since a theatrical re-release doesn't retroactively change a film's original total. For more on what that re-release actually includes, see our [Endgame Encore explainer](/articles/avengers-endgame-encore-explained-doomsday/).

## Why the Record Matters

If *Brand New Day* passes $936.66 million domestically, it becomes the highest-grossing movie in North American box office history in nominal dollars — a record *The Force Awakens* has held for almost eleven years. [It hit $700 million domestically faster than any film in history](/articles/spider-man-brand-new-day-700-million-box-office/) back when this run first started drawing attention, and [it was still building toward this position as recently as its fourth weekend](/articles/spider-man-brand-new-day-insidious-box-office-august-2026/).
