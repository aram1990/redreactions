*Spider-Man: Brand New Day* passed *Avengers: Endgame*'s domestic total on Monday, August 24, moving into second place on the all-time North American box office chart, unadjusted for inflation. Its fifth weekend has widened that lead: Sony's Spider-Man sequel added an estimated **$22 million** through Sunday, pushing its running domestic total to roughly **$891 million**. Only *Star Wars: The Force Awakens* is still ahead of it.

## How it got past Endgame

*Endgame*'s original 2019 domestic run closed at **$858,373,000**. Going into its fourth weekend, *Brand New Day* sat at $854.9 million — close, but still short. It crossed the *Endgame* mark on the Monday after that fourth weekend, a few days before the estimate that closed out weekend five. Deadline confirmed the pass on August 24, putting the film at No. 2 all time domestically, ahead of every other Marvel release and every other film except one.

## The one film still ahead of it

*The Force Awakens* has held the unadjusted domestic record since December 2015, finishing its run at **$936,662,225**. Against *Brand New Day*'s current estimated total of $891 million, that leaves a gap of roughly **$45 million** — TheWrap put it at around $43 million in its own weekend report, with the exact figure moving slightly depending on which running total is used before final numbers settle.

That's a real gap, not a rounding error, but it's also a small fraction of what the film has already made. This is the domestic, unadjusted comparison specifically. Adjusted for inflation, *The Force Awakens* is further out of reach; nobody currently claims *Brand New Day* is challenging that version of the record.

## Whether it can actually close the gap

Nothing here is decided yet. *Brand New Day* has now led the box office for five straight weekends, a stretch of dominance without a real challenger until this past weekend's *Coyote vs. Acme* and *The Dog Stars* both opened well behind it. Weekend-to-weekend decline has been steep and fairly typical for a superhero blockbuster — roughly 45% down week four to week five — which is the normal shape of a film's theatrical run rather than a sign it's stalling early.

Whether $45 million materializes depends on how much longer theaters keep it in wide release and whether anything in September pulls attention away faster than usual. *Avengers: Endgame* itself is also returning to theaters September 25 as *Endgame Encore*, which is an interesting wrinkle for Marvel's own box office history but not something that would meaningfully close *Brand New Day*'s gap to *The Force Awakens* — a theatrical re-release doesn't retroactively add to a film's original box office record, and Encore's own additions would need to be enormous to move Endgame back into the No. 2 slot. For more on what that re-release actually includes, see our [Endgame Encore explainer](/articles/avengers-endgame-encore-explained-doomsday/).

## Why the record matters

If *Brand New Day* does pass $936.66 million domestically, it becomes the highest-grossing movie in North American box office history in nominal dollars — a record *The Force Awakens* has held for almost eleven years. That's the milestone being chased here, specifically the domestic, non-inflation-adjusted chart. Worldwide, the film has already cleared roughly $2.24 billion, a different number from a different conversation; [it hit $700 million domestically faster than any film in history](/articles/spider-man-brand-new-day-700-million-box-office/) back when this run first started drawing attention, and [it was still building toward this position as recently as its fourth weekend](/articles/spider-man-brand-new-day-insidious-box-office-august-2026/).
