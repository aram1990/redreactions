*Spider-Man: Brand New Day* has done it: as of September 16, the film has passed *Star Wars: The Force Awakens* to become the highest-grossing movie of all time at the North American box office, in nominal, unadjusted dollars. Its running domestic total stands at roughly **$936.77 million**, edging past *The Force Awakens*' **$936,662,225**, a record that stood for almost eleven years since the film's 2015–16 run. Variety, The Hollywood Reporter and Collider all reported the milestone on September 16, with the film crossing the mark in its 47th day of release.

## How It Got Here

*Brand New Day* passed *Avengers: Endgame*'s 2019 domestic total of $858,373,000 on August 24, moving into second place on the all-time North American chart. It then became the fastest film ever to reach $900 million domestically, doing it in roughly 36 days against *The Force Awakens*' own 50-day pace to the same mark in 2015. Six straight weekends at No. 1 — including Labor Day weekend against *Coyote vs. Acme*'s second-weekend hold — chipped the remaining gap down from around $45 million after its fifth weekend to roughly $14 million by Labor Day, and continued theatrical business over the following days closed that final stretch.

## Which Record This Actually Is

It's worth being precise about which record is in play: the North American, unadjusted-for-inflation domestic record specifically — not worldwide, not inflation-adjusted, not total tickets sold. Adjusted for inflation, *The Force Awakens* remains far ahead, and nothing here challenges that version. Worldwide, *Brand New Day* has separately passed $2.4 billion, per AP — a different total answering a different question from the domestic record tracked here.

The current domestic figure is still being tracked through ongoing daily grosses rather than a single audited year-end final, which is normal for a still-running theatrical release — Sony and box-office trackers will keep updating the total as *Brand New Day* continues its run. That doesn't change the milestone itself: Variety, The Hollywood Reporter and Collider are all consistent in reporting that the film has passed *The Force Awakens*' total as of September 16.

## How Close the Finish Was

By Labor Day, the remaining gap had narrowed to about $13.7 million — down from around $45 million after the film's fifth weekend. That was already a much shorter distance than a full blockbuster weekend, and it meant ordinary theatrical business over the following days was enough to close it. The final margin between the two films' domestic totals is narrow: *Brand New Day*'s roughly $936.77 million edges *The Force Awakens*' $936,662,225 by close to $100,000, not a wide gap in box-office terms.

*Avengers: Endgame* is also returning to theaters September 25 as *Endgame Encore* — a separate wrinkle in Marvel's own box office history that doesn't affect this specific record, since a theatrical re-release doesn't retroactively change a film's original total. ## Why the Record Matters

*Brand New Day* is now the highest-grossing movie in North American box office history in nominal dollars — a record *The Force Awakens* had held for almost eleven years. [It hit $700 million domestically faster than any film in history](/articles/spider-man-brand-new-day-700-million-box-office/) back when this run first started drawing attention, and [it was still building toward this position as recently as its fourth weekend](/articles/spider-man-brand-new-day-insidious-box-office-august-2026/).
