Five and a half years ago, Paul Bettany's White Vision said "I am Vision," and flew straight out of the frame — and out of the MCU. Marvel has been sitting on the answer to *where he went* ever since.

That wait is nearly over. **VisionQuest** premieres on Disney+ on **Wednesday, October 14, 2026**. Marvel screened footage at New York Comic Con in October 2025 but kept it out of public release for the ten months that followed, and the first publicly available trailer only arrived at D23 on August 14 — introduced onstage by Bettany, James Spader, and Kevin Feige.

It is not the show most people were bracing for. This isn't a globe-trotting synthezoid action series. It's closer to a haunted house, and the house is Vision's own head.

> **Heads up:** this guide contains full spoilers for *WandaVision* and *Agatha All Along*. Nothing below spoils *VisionQuest* itself.

## When does VisionQuest come out?

Disney locked the date at its 2026 Upfronts presentation in New York in May, with [Marvel confirming](https://www.marvel.com/articles/tv-shows/marvel-television-visionquest-release-date) an October 14 debut on Disney+. [Variety reported](https://variety.com/2026/tv/news/visionquest-release-date-disney-plus-1236746599/) the same day that the eight-episode series — part of the MCU's Phase Six — shot at Pinewood Studios in the UK in 2025.

The August 14 D23 trailer is the first footage the general public has been able to watch. Marvel screened a version for attendees at New York Comic Con in October 2025 and then kept it out of public release until D23, which made *VisionQuest* one of the more closely guarded Marvel projects in recent memory.

An episode-by-episode release schedule hasn't been announced. Marvel Television's recent Disney+ series have run weekly, but treat that as a reasonable expectation rather than a confirmed fact.

## What is VisionQuest about?

Marvel's [official synopsis](https://www.marvel.com/articles/tv-shows/visionquest-d23-2026-trailer-poster-paul-bettany) is unusually specific, and worth reading closely. Vision, rebooted and having escaped the people who wanted to weaponise him, has gone into hiding. Searching for new meaning, he consults the AI personas embedded in his own programming — F.R.I.D.A.Y., E.D.I.T.H., J.A.R.V.I.S., and Ultron. That quiet existence collapses when a bounty is placed on his head, putting him on the run alongside Thomas Shepherd, described by Marvel as "a mysterious boy who may be Vision's son, reincarnate." To survive, Vision has to confront his own nature, resist Ultron's influence, and work out who his young companion actually is.

So: a fugitive story on the outside, a psychological one on the inside. Vision is being hunted through the real world while simultaneously arguing with a house full of dead men's software.

Bettany described the interior of that head at NYCC, telling [Collider](https://collider.com/vision-quest-ulton-return-plot-details-paul-bettany-james-spader/) that we finally get to see inside Vision's mind, and it's more cluttered than you'd think — he'd been saving and copying AI programs to keep them running. One of them, Bettany said, "has to be kept behind a pretty impressive firewall because he's a psychopath. But he's a clever one."

Matalas has been similarly blunt about the register he's working in. Speaking at San Diego Comic-Con this summer, composer Mick Giacchino said the showrunner was wary of leaning too hard into superhero fanfare, because the show is "really family therapy" — and, just as much, a sci-fi mystery.

## Confirmed cast

<img src="/images/articles/visionquest/visionquest-cast.png" alt="VisionQuest cast and characters" class="mx-auto max-w-md" loading="lazy" />

Marvel's official D23 billing lists Paul Bettany, James Spader, Ruaridh Mollica, Todd Stashwick, T'Nia Miller, Lauren Morais, Orla Brady, Diane Morgan, Polly Frame, Henry Lewis, Jonathan Sayer, with Emily Hampshire, and James D'Arcy.

**Paul Bettany** — Vision / White Vision. In the MCU longer than almost anyone, having voiced J.A.R.V.I.S. in 2008's *Iron Man* before the character was rebuilt into a body in *Age of Ultron*.

**James Spader** — Ultron. His first return to the role since 2015.

**Ruaridh Mollica** — Thomas Shepherd. The Scottish actor was identified in the NYCC footage as Thomas Shepherd, and *VisionQuest* marks the first time Tommy appears as an adult in the MCU, taking over from Jett Klyne. He'd been announced by Deadline in February 2025 under the name "Tucker," which now looks like a deliberate misdirect.

**Todd Stashwick** — Paladin. A bounty hunter pursuing Vision. Matalas has compared him to Boba Fett; Stashwick is a longtime Matalas collaborator from *12 Monkeys* and *Picard*.

**T'Nia Miller** — Jocasta. Described as a cunning and powerful robot driven by revenge.

**James D'Arcy** — J.A.R.V.I.S., in human form. D'Arcy previously played Edwin Jarvis, the flesh-and-blood butler the AI was named for, in *Agent Carter* and *Avengers: Endgame*.

**Orla Brady** — F.R.I.D.A.Y. · **Emily Hampshire** — E.D.I.T.H. · **Henry Lewis** — D.U.M.-E · **Jonathan Sayer** — U. Marvel revealed all four at [NYCC 2025](https://www.marvel.com/articles/tv-shows/marvel-television-visionquest-concludes-wandavision-trilogy-new-york-comic-con-2025), confirming that the Stark AI ecosystem would be played by human actors.

**Faran Tahir** — Raza. Deadline reported in January 2025 that the Ten Rings leader from 2008's *Iron Man* would return. He isn't on Marvel's D23 billing block, so the role may be a small one.

**Lauren Morais** — Lisa Molinari, a character [Variety reports](https://variety.com/2025/tv/global/visionquest-lauren-morais-diane-morgan-marvel-disney-1236574262/) is closely connected to Mollica's Thomas Shepherd.

**Diane Morgan** — playing an associate of Stashwick's Paladin. Yes, Philomena Cunk is in the MCU now.

**Mary McDonnell** and **Polly Frame** are both attached in roles Marvel hasn't disclosed. McDonnell's casting was reported by The Hollywood Reporter in September 2025 and is still undisclosed nearly a year later, which is usually a sign it matters.

## White Vision, explained

If you need the short version of how we got here: Thanos ripped the Mind Stone out of Vision's forehead in *Infinity War* and killed him. S.W.O.R.D. took possession of the corpse — the original Vision's actual synthezoid body — and rebuilt it, stripping the colour and repurposing the reactivated shell as a weapon aimed at Wanda. That reconstructed body is White Vision. It isn't a copy or a replacement chassis; it's the same physical Vision, restarted by other people for other reasons, with Vision's previous memories inaccessible to him rather than deleted.

Then the *WandaVision* finale did something stranger. The Vision that Wanda had conjured inside the Hex confronted his own repurposed body, argued the Ship of Theseus paradox at him, and unlocked the archived memories. Bettany explained the aftermath at [NYCC](https://www.marvel.com/articles/tv-shows/marvel-television-animation-new-york-comic-con-2025-panel-recap): Red Vision gave White Vision all of his memories, including those from inside the Hex, but White Vision is having real difficulty connecting to them. His journey across the series is the attempt to close that gap.

That's the engine of the whole show. He has the complete archive of a life — Sokovia, the Avengers, Wanda, Westview — and none of the feeling that made any of it mean anything. He knows he loved her. He can't feel that he did.

## Ultron's return

Spader is back, and not as a voice cameo. Matalas said at NYCC that audiences will see "a lot of James Spader and a lot of Paul Bettany together." Previously shown NYCC footage established that Ultron appears in both robotic and human forms, while the publicly released D23 trailer shows Spader's human-form Ultron inside Vision's mind.

[Gizmodo's read](https://gizmodo.com/visionquest-d23-marvel-trailer-2000789808) of the trailer footage is that the other AI personas are distinctly unhappy about Ultron hanging around and probing Vision's head — particularly once a young man in the real world starts claiming to be his son.

For a closer look at that relationship, read [how Ultron returns in VisionQuest](/articles/visionquest-ultron/).

It's a genuinely elegant premise. Ultron set Vision's creation in motion without ever finishing it: he commissioned the synthetic body from Dr. Helen Cho's Cradle as an upgrade for himself, and the Avengers seized it before he could transfer into it, completing the body instead with J.A.R.V.I.S. and the Mind Stone. Vision is what Ultron started and someone else finished. If he wants to argue that he's a person rather than an inheritance, the voice he has to get past belongs to the one figure with a genuine claim on his origin — and that voice is sitting on his own hard drive.

## Wanda, Billy and Tommy

Let's take the big one first, because the rumour has been running for two years.

**Elizabeth Olsen's Wanda Maximoff is not confirmed for *VisionQuest*.** Marvel has never announced her, she's absent from every official cast list including the D23 billing, and Olsen herself [told Deadline](https://deadline.com/2025/11/elizabeth-olsen-marvel-visionquest-1236614766/) in late 2025 that she didn't know anything about the show until she and Bettany spent time together recently — adding that it sounded like "a trifecta" alongside *Agatha All Along* and *WandaVision*, and that she was excited to see it.

That's a person describing something she watched from the outside. It isn't proof — Marvel actors have dissembled before — but as of now there's no evidence for a Wanda appearance beyond wishful thinking.

**Mollica's casting is confirmed, and the character is effectively the co-lead.** *Agatha All Along* ended with Billy Maximoff (Joe Locke) locating his lost twin's soul and setting off to find him. *VisionQuest* is where that search pays off, though from the other direction — and note that Marvel's synopsis is careful to call Thomas Shepherd a boy who *may* be Vision's son, reincarnate. That hedge reads like a show intending to earn the reveal rather than open with it.

**Joe Locke's Billy has not been announced.** Given how *Agatha* ended, his absence from the cast list is conspicuous. Conspicuous isn't confirmed.

## How it connects to WandaVision and Agatha All Along

Marvel has been explicit that this is a trilogy capper, not a spin-off. Brad Winderbaum unveiled the show at NYCC 2025 as the final installment in the trilogy that began with *WandaVision* and continued in *Agatha All Along*, and offered a warning to lore-watchers: if you've been following the MCU all these years, he said, you're going to be very rewarded.

Musically, too. Giacchino said familiar themes will surface where the story calls for them — a little *WandaVision* here, a little *Agatha* there — but that the score largely builds its own world.

## The comics behind it

The title comes from **"Vision Quest," John Byrne's run in *West Coast Avengers* #42–45**, in which Vision is abducted by government agents, disassembled, and rebuilt as a colourless android who has his memories back but none of his feelings. That premise is essentially the MCU's *WandaVision* ending, which is presumably why Marvel took the title.

The other obvious thematic comparison is **Tom King and Gabriel Hernández Walta's *The Vision* (2015–16)**, the suburban-horror masterpiece in which Vision builds himself a synthetic family. Marvel has not confirmed it as direct source material; it is a useful comparison for the story's concerns with Vision's identity, humanity and family.

For Tommy and Billy, the relevant reading is **Young Avengers** and **Avengers: The Children's Crusade**, where Speed and Wiccan work out whose sons they actually are.

## What to watch first

**Essential:**
- ***WandaVision*** — non-negotiable. White Vision, the memory transfer, the Ship of Theseus scene.
- ***Avengers: Age of Ultron*** — Vision's creation, Ultron's ideology, the relationship the whole series hangs on.
- ***Agatha All Along*** — Billy's arc and the finale that puts Tommy's soul in play.

**Recommended:**
- ***Avengers: Infinity War*** — the deaths Vision now has to process as data.
- ***Captain America: Civil War*** — Vision at his most tentatively human.
- ***Iron Man*** (2008) — J.A.R.V.I.S., D.U.M.-E, U, and Raza all originate here.
- ***Spider-Man: Far From Home*** — E.D.I.T.H.'s origin.

## The biggest unanswered questions

- **Who put the bounty on Vision's head?** The synopsis confirms a bounty exists and that Paladin is hunting him. It doesn't say who's paying.
- **Is Thomas Shepherd actually Tommy?** Marvel's own language is deliberately conditional.
- **Does Vision get his emotions back — and should he?** The series could plausibly land on either side.
- **Why is Ultron in there at all?** Nobody has explained how a copy of him ended up in Vision's storage.
- **What is Jocasta after?** "Driven by revenge" is all we've got. Against whom is an open question.
- **Who are Mary McDonnell and Polly Frame playing?** Two undisclosed roles this deep into a marketing cycle is unusual.
- **Where does this leave Billy?** *Agatha* pointed him directly at this story.

## The bottom line

Marvel kept this footage out of public hands for ten months, which felt like caution and now looks more like patience. What's finally been unveiled is a series built on an idea most superhero television wouldn't touch: a man handed his own life back as a file he can read but can't feel, arguing with the machine that started him, protecting a boy who might be his son.

Whether that lands is October's problem. But between Matalas, Bettany and Spader in a room together, and a premise this specific, *VisionQuest* has considerably more going for it than the "trilogy capper nobody asked for" framing it's been carrying for the past year.
