**Spoiler warning:** This article discusses the confirmed ending of *Avengers: Armageddon* #4, on sale now from Marvel Comics, plus officially announced plans for November's *Avengers* #1. Nothing here is speculation about unannounced story details beyond what Marvel has stated.

Marvel confirmed on September 16 and 17 that **Miracleman** — one of comics' most influential, and most legally complicated, superheroes — has returned in the Marvel Universe, and that the character will be a founding member of the publisher's new *Avengers* lineup launching in November. The reveal happened in *Avengers: Armageddon* #4, and it's built around a character readers already know from a very different corner of Marvel's line.

## What happened in Avengers: Armageddon #4

*Avengers: Armageddon* #4, written by Chip Zdarsky with art by Delio Diaz and Frank Alpizar, went on sale September 16, 2026. According to Marvel's own recap, the issue delivered what the publisher calls "a game-changing revelation": the source of the mysterious Origin Box that had already given David Colton unexplained powers was Miracleman himself. CBR's coverage of the issue adds the specific beat that triggers the transformation — after Colton is nearly destroyed by an exploding Red Hulk, visions of his late mother prompt him to speak Miracleman's classic activation word, "Kimota." He does, and the issue ends with him transformed.

## Who is David Colton?

Colton isn't a new character invented for this reveal. Marvel's own description identifies him as "the 21st-century Captain America introduced in the opening arc of Chip Zdarsky's acclaimed *Captain America* (2025) run" — meaning readers following that series already know him as a version of Captain America before this story ever gave him the Origin Box. *Avengers: Armageddon* takes that established character and hands him an entirely different power set and identity, in the middle of the event's climactic battle.

## Why is Marvel calling him Miracleman?

Because, per Marvel's own account and CBR's reporting on the issue's contents, the power inside the Origin Box belonged specifically to Michael Moran — the civilian identity of the original Miracleman — and Colton has now inherited it. Marvel Editor Wil Moss framed the significance directly: "Dave Colton is a fascinating, complicated character, now much more so as he takes on the mantle of Miracleman. Chip has BIG plans for him that will affect not just the Avengers, but the entire Marvel Universe. This is the beginning of something really special."

## Is this the classic Miracleman — the Alan Moore, Michael Moran character?

Not exactly, and Marvel hasn't said that it is. What's officially confirmed is narrower and more specific: David Colton, an established Earth-616 character, has gained the power that belonged to Michael Moran and now holds the Miracleman identity going forward. Marvel has not stated that this merges classic Miracleman continuity into the mainstream Marvel Universe, and nothing here should be read as Michael Moran himself returning as a character.

For context on where that power came from: Miracleman began life in 1954 as **Marvelman**, created by Mick Anglo, before Alan Moore's celebrated and controversial 1980s revival made the character (renamed Miracleman for the U.S. market) one of the most influential and disputed works in the medium's history. Marvel acquired the publishing rights years ago and has released its own Miracleman material since, including 2024's *Miracleman: The Silver Age*. That history explains why the name carries weight — it doesn't establish how much of it is now "canon" for Colton's version, which Marvel hasn't detailed.

## Who's on the new Avengers roster?

Marvel's own cover reveal confirms the founding lineup for *Avengers* #1, written by Chip Zdarsky with art and cover by Marco Checchetto, on sale **November 4, 2026**: **Captain Marvel, Spider-Man, Daredevil, Wolverine, Luke Cage**, and **Miracleman** — described by Marvel as "the final member of the team." Marvel's own synopsis for the series: in the aftermath of *Armageddon*, "the world has turned its back on Earth's Mightiest Heroes," and the team has to assemble against a demonic invasion despite that mistrust — with Marvel's own copy adding a pointed caveat: "the unpredictable David Colton may not be able to be trusted with his godlike new abilities."

## What Marvel has actually said about Miracleman's future

Beyond Wil Moss's comment about Zdarsky having "BIG plans" affecting the Avengers and the wider Marvel Universe, Marvel has confirmed the immediate next steps: *Avengers: Armageddon* #5 — the finale of the event, also written by Zdarsky, with art by Diaz, Alpizar and Ario Anindito and a cover by Dike Ruan — goes on sale **October 21, 2026**, and will show the immediate outcome of Colton's transformation before *Avengers* #1 picks up the thread in November.

## What's still unconfirmed

Marvel hasn't detailed how much of Colton's new power set works, how much control he has over it, or how directly his arc will connect to the "godlike abilities" line in its own *Avengers* #1 solicit beyond calling him untrustworthy with them. The publisher also hasn't clarified the exact rules governing how Michael Moran's power transferred to Colton, or whether any other classic Miracleman story elements are being treated as established fact for this version. Treat all of that as open until Marvel or Zdarsky addresses it directly — the confirmed facts here are the transformation itself, the November roster, and the two on-sale dates for *Armageddon* #5 and *Avengers* #1.
