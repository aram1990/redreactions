Ubisoft is taking Rainbow Six somewhere the franchise has not been in a very long time: back to a dedicated single-player experience.

Revealed during Gamescom Opening Night Live, **Rainbow Six Tactics** is a turn-based tactical game that puts players in command of Rainbow rather than behind the sights of a competitive shooter. Instead of building a loadout for another round of *Siege*, players will run operations from headquarters, assemble squads and plan missions before sending Operators into the field.

It is a dramatic change of perspective for a franchise that has spent the last decade being defined by *Rainbow Six Siege*.

Ubisoft says *Rainbow Six Tactics* will feature **15 playable Operators**, each with unique abilities, specialized gadgets and their own skill trees. Squads can include up to four Operators, and missions will send them across **more than 45 locations around the world**.

The mission list already includes familiar counter-terrorism scenarios such as **Hostage Rescue, Bomb Disposal and Target Elimination**.

## Rainbow Six Goes Turn-Based

The easiest comparison is *XCOM*, but Ubisoft is clearly trying to keep *Tactics* grounded in the systems that make Rainbow Six feel like Rainbow Six.

Preparation starts before deployment. Players review intelligence at HQ, choose the right Operators for the assignment and decide how they want to approach the objective. Once the squad is in the field, combat becomes turn-based, with positioning, recon and Operator abilities deciding how an encounter unfolds.

Destruction also carries over from *Siege*. Walls can be breached to create new entry points, remove enemy cover or open fresh lines of sight.

One of the biggest additions is **Breach Planning**, a system that lets players coordinate actions across their active squad and trigger a synchronized assault. Ubisoft describes it as a way to break the normal turn-based rhythm and recreate the explosive, carefully planned room entries that have always been part of Rainbow Six's identity.

That distinction matters. This is not simply Ubisoft putting Rainbow Six characters into a generic strategy template. The studio is trying to translate the same cycle of intel, planning, breach and adaptation into a slower format.

## The Operators Come From Rainbow Six Siege

The playable roster is built from familiar *Siege* faces.

Ubisoft has confirmed that all 15 recruitable Operators come from *Rainbow Six Siege*, with **Ash, Sledge, Nøkk and Montagne** among the names already shown. Their signature equipment and abilities are being reworked for turn-based combat rather than discarded.

That creates some obvious possibilities for squad building. A breaching specialist can open an unexpected path, a long-range Operator can control sightlines, and defensive or support abilities can take on a completely different role when every move is planned one turn at a time.

Operators can also be trained between missions, while the player's base of operations can be strengthened as the campaign progresses.

The game takes place alongside the broader *Rainbow Six Siege* timeline, with a new global threat forcing Rainbow to operate on a larger strategic scale.

## It Is a Real Single-Player Rainbow Six Again

That may be the biggest story here.

PC Gamer describes *Rainbow Six Tactics* as the franchise's **first dedicated single-player Rainbow Six game in 19 years**, a gap that shows just how thoroughly *Siege* reshaped Ubisoft's priorities.

Older Rainbow Six games were built around methodical planning, squad commands and tightly controlled counter-terrorism missions. *Siege* retained the tactical DNA but transformed it into a competitive multiplayer phenomenon.

*Tactics* goes in the opposite direction.

It is a campaign-focused strategy game where the player controls the broader operation, and Ubisoft's official reveal emphasizes a single-player experience rather than a live-service structure.

For longtime fans who miss the slower, planning-heavy side of Rainbow Six, that alone makes this one of Ubisoft's more interesting experiments in years.

## Ubisoft Paris Is Leading Development

The project is being led by **Ubisoft Paris**, a studio with experience across multiple tactical Ubisoft franchises and work connected to the *Mario + Rabbids* games.

Creative director Elie Benhamou says the team saw a natural fit between Rainbow Six and turn-based strategy because the franchise already revolves around intelligence gathering, preparation, specialized roles and high-stakes decisions.

That philosophy is visible throughout the reveal. Players are not simply moving units across a grid; they are deciding how to enter fortified spaces, where to create new paths and which combination of Operators gives them the best chance of completing an objective.

## When Is Rainbow Six Tactics Coming Out?

Ubisoft has opened wishlists for **PlayStation 5, Xbox Series X|S and PC**, with PC availability planned through Ubisoft Connect, Steam and the Epic Games Store. Ubisoft also lists cloud support through GeForce Now and Blacknut.

PC Gamer reports that *Rainbow Six Tactics* is due in **2027**, although Ubisoft's reveal article itself does not currently provide a more specific launch date.

So there is no exact day to circle yet.

What Ubisoft has shown is already enough to make the pitch clear: *Rainbow Six Tactics* is taking the Operators, destruction and breach-focused planning of *Siege* and rebuilding them around a fully turn-based single-player campaign.

After years of Rainbow Six being synonymous with one multiplayer game, that is a pretty significant change of strategy.
