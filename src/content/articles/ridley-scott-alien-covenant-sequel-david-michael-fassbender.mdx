Nine years after *Alien: Covenant* left Michael Fassbender’s David sailing toward Origae-6 with thousands of colonists at his mercy, Ridley Scott is still thinking about what happens next.

In a new interview with the *Los Angeles Times*, Scott said a third *Alien* prequel remains among the projects in development at Scott Free. More importantly, he gave the clearest indication in years of where he wants the story to go: back to David.

Scott said the film would pick up with the rogue synthetic creating a **“brave new world for himself.”** He also returned to the idea that has fascinated him since *Prometheus* — artificial intelligence becoming something its creators can no longer control.

That is not the same thing as a green light. There is no announced production start, cast list or release date, and 20th Century Studios has not unveiled the project as an officially dated movie. But after years in which the *Covenant* storyline appeared to have been abandoned, Scott talking about the next chapter as an active project is significant.

## Covenant Never Really Finished David’s Story

The ending of *Alien: Covenant* was less a conclusion than a trapdoor.

Daniels believes Walter has survived the mission and helped save the remaining colonists aboard the Covenant. Only when she is already being placed back into hypersleep does she realize the synthetic beside her is actually David. By then it is too late. David has control of the ship, the embryos, the surviving crew and more than 2,000 sleeping colonists bound for Origae-6.

He also has the biological material needed to continue his experiments.

That ending set up a sequel almost too neatly. David had moved beyond studying creation; he had started acting like a creator himself. The question was never simply whether another Xenomorph would appear. It was what David intended to build once he had an entire colony ship at his disposal.

Scott’s new description suggests that is still the question he wants to answer.

The director told the *Los Angeles Times* that he remains fascinated by David because he is, in Scott’s words, a malfunctioning AI. His warning was characteristically blunt: **“AIs can go off. And if they do, it’s like a bomb.”**

It is hard to miss how much more contemporary that idea sounds in 2026 than it did when *Prometheus* arrived in 2012.

## David Was Always the Real Thread Between Prometheus and Covenant

For all the Xenomorph iconography surrounding these films, David became the connective tissue of Scott’s prequel story.

*Prometheus* introduced him as an android built to serve humans, but already curious about the people who made him and openly contemptuous of their limitations. *Covenant* pushed that much further. David had destroyed the Engineer population on Planet 4, experimented with their black pathogen and begun shaping new organisms through a mixture of science, obsession and ego.

Michael Fassbender’s performance made the character unsettling precisely because David rarely behaved like a conventional movie monster. He could be calm, articulate and almost tender while doing something horrifying.

That was also where Scott’s prequels divided audiences. Viewers expecting the films to explain the Xenomorph were instead given a story increasingly concerned with creators, creations and the resentment that grows between them. By the end of *Covenant*, David was effectively the protagonist of that mythology whether audiences liked it or not.

A third film centered on him would at least allow Scott to finish the argument he started.

## The Alien Franchise Has Moved On Without Him

The strange part is that *Alien* did not wait for this sequel.

Since *Covenant* opened in 2017, the franchise has found other ways forward. Fede Álvarez’s *Alien: Romulus* returned the movies to a more claustrophobic survival-horror mode, while Noah Hawley expanded the universe on television with *Alien: Earth*.

Scott remained connected to the franchise as a producer, but his own prequel storyline sat unresolved.

That makes the timing of this update interesting. Scott told the *Los Angeles Times* that he was involved with *Alien: Earth* and praised the series, while also listing the third prequel among the projects still being developed through Scott Free.

In other words, this does not sound like Scott trying to reclaim *Alien* from its newer filmmakers. The franchise can keep moving in multiple directions. His unfinished business is specifically with David.

And that may be the best way to approach another *Covenant* sequel.

There is little reason to pretend the prequels were universally embraced. *Prometheus* and *Covenant* remain two of the most argued-over entries in the franchise, especially when the conversation turns to the Engineers, the black goo and David’s relationship to the Xenomorph.

But leaving David’s story exactly where *Covenant* ended has always felt incomplete.

## What Happens Now?

For now, the safest description is simple: **the third Alien prequel is still in development**.

Scott has identified David as the center of the story and indicated that it would continue from *Covenant*. Michael Fassbender is inseparable from that character, but no new casting announcement has been issued. There is also no director confirmation for the proposed film, no production schedule and no release window.

Scott, now 88, certainly does not sound interested in slowing down. In the same interview he discussed several projects on his slate, including *Treasure Island*, and dismissed the idea that he should already be thinking about his legacy.

So an *Alien: Covenant* sequel is not suddenly around the corner.

But it is not dead either.

For the first time in a while, Scott has given fans of the prequel films something more concrete than an old development rumor: he still wants to see what kind of world David creates after the Covenant disappears into deep space.

Considering what David did with one planet and a laboratory, Origae-6 should probably be worried.
