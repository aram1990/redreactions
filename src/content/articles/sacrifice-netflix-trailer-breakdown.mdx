Netflix released the official trailer for *Sacrifice* on September 16, and the footage makes one thing clear immediately: this is not a straightforward hostage thriller. Romain Gavras's English-language debut, starring Chris Evans and Anya Taylor-Joy, sets its crisis inside a celebrity charity gala and builds it around a premise stranger than the trailer's marketing alone would suggest — a volcanic prophecy that a cult leader believes requires three lives to stop.

## The Hostage Crisis Netflix Has Actually Confirmed

<img src="/images/articles/sacrifice-netflix-trailer-breakdown/sacrifice-netflix-trailer-mike-tyler.webp" alt="Chris Evans as Mike Tyler in a white tuxedo, surrounded by photographers at the Sacrifice gala entrance." loading="lazy" />

According to Netflix's official synopsis, Chris Evans plays **Mike Tyler**, a down-on-his-luck action star chasing a career comeback at a star-studded environmental gala held in an austere Greek mine. The event is hosted by billionaire **Ben Bracken** (Vincent Cassel) and his wife **Gloria** (Salma Hayek Pinault) — the kind of soirée, Netflix's own material notes, where the wealthy "pay lip service to a cause, snap a picture, and go on their way."

<img src="/images/articles/sacrifice-netflix-trailer-breakdown/sacrifice-netflix-trailer-gala.webp" alt="Vincent Cassel as Ben Bracken and Salma Hayek Pinault as Gloria Bracken seated at the gala in Netflix's Sacrifice." loading="lazy" />
<em>Vincent Cassel as Ben Bracken and Salma Hayek Pinault as Gloria Bracken. Credit: Netflix.</em>

That performance gets interrupted. A cult leader named **Joan** (Anya Taylor-Joy) and her followers seize the event and take the guests hostage, including Evans's Mike Tyler. Their stated motive, per Netflix, is a specific and extreme belief: an extinction-level event will happen in three days unless three people are sacrificed to a nearby volcano.

## The Prophecy, According to What's Actually Confirmed

It's worth being precise about what Netflix has and hasn't confirmed here. The official synopsis states Joan and her followers *believe* the sacrifice will prevent catastrophe — it does not confirm the prophecy is real, that the volcano erupts, or how the film ultimately resolves that belief. Treat the volcanic doomsday premise as the cult's conviction, not as an established fact of the film's world, unless later marketing says otherwise.

## Celebrity Culture Is the Real Target

Gavras and co-writer Will Arbery (*Succession*) have been explicit that the satire is the point, not just the hostage plot. Netflix's own framing describes the film taking aim at "celebrity culture, media spectacle, performative activism, and elite hypocrisy," layered with what the studio calls "an earnest exploration of faith." Gavras has also compared Joan's mythology to Joan of Arc, in his own words, which frames her character as drawing on a specific, deliberate reference rather than being a generic cult-leader archetype.

## A Cast Built Around the Satire

<img src="/images/articles/sacrifice-netflix-trailer-breakdown/sacrifice-netflix-trailer-followers.webp" alt="Yung Lean as Arthur, Anya Taylor-Joy as Joan, and Jade Croot as Louise standing together in firelight in Netflix's Sacrifice." loading="lazy" />
<em>Yung Lean as Arthur, Anya Taylor-Joy as Joan, and Jade Croot as Louise. Credit: Netflix.</em>

Netflix's confirmed cast extends well beyond the two leads: Ambika Mod as **Daughter Nature**, Jonatan "Yung Lean" Leandoer as **Arthur**, Sam Richardson as **Oliver**, Jade Croot as **Louise**, Miriam Silverman as **Anna**, Charli xcx as **Mother Nature**, and John Malkovich as **Gunnar** round out the ensemble — with Mod and Charli xcx's character names pairing off directly, per Netflix's own current cast listing. Gavras has said he specifically wanted performers who could handle the film's tonal range — praising Evans's comedic timing and Taylor-Joy's "otherworldly" quality, which he's said he's been drawn to since her breakout in *The Witch*.

*Sacrifice* also marks Gavras's third collaboration with Vincent Cassel, who also produced the director's first feature, *Notre Jour Viendra*.

## What the Trailer Leaves Unanswered

Nothing in the trailer or Netflix's official material confirms who survives the hostage crisis, whether any of the three intended sacrifices actually happens, or how Mike Tyler's arc resolves. The footage is built to sell tone and premise — a gala turned hostage standoff, wrapped in social satire — rather than to preview plot mechanics. Any specific claim about the ending, a character's death, or a hidden motive beyond what's described above should be treated as speculation, not something this trailer establishes.

## Release: October 16

*Sacrifice* streams on Netflix in the **United States and France starting October 16, 2026**, running 1 hour 40 minutes and rated R. Netflix's own material confirms the film was shot entirely on location across **Greece, Bulgaria, and Iceland** — not Greece alone — including scenes filmed at an active volcano and a mine in Northern Greece, where Gavras says the crew brought 200 extras in gala attire and hiked gear up by donkey for roughly an hour each morning.
