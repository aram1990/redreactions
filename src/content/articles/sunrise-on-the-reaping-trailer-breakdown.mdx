Lionsgate released a second full trailer for *The Hunger Games: Sunrise on the Reaping* on September 16, and it does something the first one, from April, didn't: it puts Joseph Zada's young Haymitch Abernathy fully at the center, alongside a clearer look at the brutal 50th Hunger Games he's forced into.

<YouTube id="lYXtL5TreOE" title="The Hunger Games: Sunrise on the Reaping (2026) Official Trailer 2 - Joseph Zada" />

## The Second Quarter Quell, Explained

*Sunrise on the Reaping* is set 24 years before the original *Hunger Games* trilogy, following a 16-year-old Haymitch as a District 12 tribute in the 50th Hunger Games — officially the Second Quarter Quell. Quarter Quells occur every 25 years and add a special rule tied to the Capitol's founding mythology; for this one, per reporting corroborated across multiple outlets, the twist is that each district sends four tributes instead of the usual two, doubling the total from 24 to **48 tributes** competing at once. That detail comes from current trade coverage, not just marketing copy, and it's the single biggest mechanical difference from the games Katniss later survives.

## What the New Footage Shows About Haymitch

<img src="/images/articles/sunrise-on-the-reaping-trailer-breakdown/sunrise-on-the-reaping-haymitch-maysilee-alliance.webp" alt="Joseph Zada as young Haymitch Abernathy and Mckenna Grace as Maysilee Donner in the arena in Sunrise on the Reaping." loading="lazy" />

The trailer builds its arena footage around Haymitch's alliance with fellow tribute **Maysilee Donner** (Mckenna Grace), shown fighting alongside him rather than against him. According to Reactor's coverage of the trailer, Woody Harrelson — playing the older Haymitch audiences know from the original films — narrates over the footage, delivering the line: "Nobody ever wins the games. Period. There are survivors." That framing device, present-day Haymitch looking back on his own Games, is a meaningful structural detail: this isn't just young-Haymitch footage, it's explicitly filtered through what he became afterward.

The same coverage describes the trailer showing "the accident that takes the life of a Tribute" without spelling out specifics — consistent with the trailer staying vague about exact arena deaths rather than confirming who dies and how. Nothing here should be read as confirming Maysilee's fate or anyone else's beyond what the marketing has chosen to show in brief glimpses.

## Resistance as "a Small Spark"

<img src="/images/articles/sunrise-on-the-reaping-trailer-breakdown/sunrise-on-the-reaping-arena-tribute.webp" alt="A District 12 tribute standing in the mountainous Quarter Quell arena in Sunrise on the Reaping." loading="lazy" />

The trailer's clearest thematic throughline is rebellion — specifically, that it doesn't start big. Reactor's breakdown quotes the film's own framing directly: "Haymitch pulls off a small resistance, but it's just the start. Just a little spark, you might say." Paired with narration about resistance being "a long game" that requires many people over time, the footage positions Haymitch's actions in the 50th Games as an early, contained act rather than the moment that immediately topples the Capitol. That distinction matters for anyone expecting this film to function as a rebellion origin story in miniature — the trailer's own framing suggests something smaller and more personal than that.

## The Capitol's Side of the Story

<img src="/images/articles/sunrise-on-the-reaping-trailer-breakdown/sunrise-on-the-reaping-capitol-poster.webp" alt="Official Sunrise on the Reaping poster featuring Ralph Fiennes as President Snow and Capitol officials, with the tagline Capitalize Power." loading="lazy" />

Alongside the trailer, Lionsgate released two new posters that split the film's marketing into two camps: one centered on the District 12 tributes under the tagline "Challenge Authority," the other on Capitol figures — led by Ralph Fiennes's President Snow — under "Capitalize Power." That pairing reflects the film's dual focus, contrasting the tributes forced into the arena with the Capitol officials orchestrating the spectacle around them.

## The Reaping and the Arena Launch

<img src="/images/articles/sunrise-on-the-reaping-trailer-breakdown/sunrise-on-the-reaping-reaping-launch.webp" alt="A tribute looking up at the sky during the arena launch sequence in Sunrise on the Reaping." loading="lazy" />

The trailer also spends time on the ceremony surrounding the Games themselves — the reaping and the launch into the arena — rendered with the same Capitol spectacle familiar from the earlier films, but scaled up to account for the Quarter Quell's larger tribute pool. That scale is itself part of the trailer's argument: with 48 kids entering instead of 24, the Capitol's show of control is bigger and more visibly brutal than anything Katniss's generation experiences, even before accounting for whatever specific arena hazards the film has kept out of the marketing.

## Cast and Crew, Confirmed

Joseph Zada leads as young Haymitch Abernathy, with Mckenna Grace as Maysilee Donner and Ralph Fiennes reprising his *Ballad of Songbirds and Snakes* role as a younger President Snow. The confirmed ensemble's specific roles: Jesse Plemons plays Plutarch Heavensbee, the Games architect Philip Seymour Hoffman originated in the earlier films; Elle Fanning plays a young Effie Trinket; Kieran Culkin plays Caesar Flickerman, the Games' longtime Capitol host; Glenn Close plays Drusilla Sickle, a Capitol chaperone assigned to the District 12 tributes; Maya Hawke plays Wiress and Kelvin Harrison Jr. plays Beetee Latier, both District 3 tributes; Percy Daggs IV plays Ampert Latier, introduced as Beetee's son and a tribute in his own right; and Whitney Peak plays Lenore Dove Baird, Haymitch's love interest in the novel. Francis Lawrence, who directed the three prior mainline *Hunger Games* films, returns behind the camera, working from a screenplay by Billy Ray and Michael Lesslie based on Suzanne Collins's 2025 novel of the same name.

## What's Still Unconfirmed

The trailer doesn't confirm the arena's specific mechanics or hazards beyond the tribute count, how exactly the alliance with Maysilee factors into the endgame, or the precise nature of the "accident" shown briefly in the footage. Treat any claim about a specific death, betrayal, or arena twist beyond what's described above as speculation rather than something Lionsgate has confirmed.

*The Hunger Games: Sunrise on the Reaping* arrives in theaters and IMAX on **November 20, 2026**.
