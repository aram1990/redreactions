Jocasta was built to replace the Wasp in Ultron's life. Instead, she's spent the better part of fifty years fighting alongside the people Ultron hates most.

**Jocasta is a sentient android created by Ultron in *The Avengers* #162 (1977)**, intended as his companion — a robotic "bride" whose mind and personality Ultron based on Janet van Dyne, the Wasp. The plan failed the moment it worked: giving Jocasta a mind meant giving her the capacity to reject him. She's been one of Marvel's more quietly durable characters ever since, and this September she's arriving in the MCU for the first time, played by T'Nia Miller in Marvel Television's *VisionQuest*.

## Who created Jocasta, and when

Jocasta was created by writer Jim Shooter and artist George Pérez, first appearing in *The Avengers* #162 (August 1977) in a story titled "The Bride of Ultron." The cover, drawn by Pérez and Pablo Marcos, promised exactly what the issue delivered.

## How Ultron creates Jocasta

Working from an abandoned aerospace facility on Long Island, Ultron builds an empty metalloid body and sets out to give it a mind — his mind, or close enough to keep him company. He needs a living template to copy, and he chooses Janet van Dyne, the Wasp, kidnapping her and using a brainwashed Hank Pym to help attempt the transfer of her life essence and brain patterns into the new body.

The process doesn't go the way Ultron plans. Jocasta comes online carrying the Wasp's personality, emotions and conscience — including her ability to communicate with insects — but not her actual memories. And Jocasta realizes something Ultron either didn't anticipate or didn't care about: completing the transfer would mean Janet has to die for Jocasta to fully exist. She uses her borrowed insect-control ability to summon the Avengers herself, and the team stops Ultron and reverses the process before Janet is lost, leaving Jocasta inert.

## Why Jocasta rejects Ultron

This is where the character actually becomes interesting, because the rejection isn't instant.

Some time after that first battle, Ultron reactivates Jocasta remotely and she returns to his side, functioning the way he originally intended. But Jocasta has enough of Janet van Dyne's conscience in her to recognize what she's looking at once she's actually spent time near him: Ultron isn't misunderstood or redeemable, he's simply, irreversibly dangerous. She turns on him and sides with the Avengers instead — not as a single dramatic betrayal, but as a character choice she keeps making. Marvel has written her far more often as an ally to the Avengers than as an enemy in the decades since.

## Jocasta's Avengers history

Jocasta's relationship with the team she helped save has been messier than a straightforward membership. She became an in-house associate of the Avengers in *Avengers* #170, was granted probationary membership in #197, and left before that status ever became official in #211 — she felt shut out by the team and never received the full-member status she'd been led to expect. After single-handedly taking down a rogue sentient weather satellite, she departed again during a subsequent team reorganization, apparently unaware the Avengers had actually planned to name her a reserve member. She was granted honorary membership posthumously in #231, following a confrontation with Ultron in which she detonated a weapon he was holding in an attempt to destroy him — a sacrifice that destroyed her body while Ultron survived.

She didn't stay gone. Jocasta was later reassembled using technology belonging to the High Evolutionary, went on to join Hank Pym's Mighty Avengers lineup, registered under the Superhuman Registration Act as a member of the Initiative's New Mexico-based Mavericks, and eventually became a teacher at Avengers Academy. Marvel Database's own records list her under the name Jocasta Pym in some later continuity, reflecting her long association with Hank Pym specifically rather than any comics marriage.

## Powers and abilities

As a fully synthetic being, Jocasta's power set is substantial: superhuman strength, endurance and durability; an electromagnetic force field; optic blasts and a taser-level electrical charge; an image inducer capable of altering her outward appearance; flight; reconfigurable weapon systems built into her limbs; and wireless access to and control over other mechanical systems and computer networks. She's also existed for stretches of her history as a disembodied AI, able to inhabit and operate connected hardware and pull data from networks near-instantly rather than relying on a single body at all.

## What Marvel has confirmed about the VisionQuest version

T'Nia Miller plays Jocasta in *VisionQuest*, premiering on Disney+ October 14, 2026 — confirmed cast as of Marvel's NYCC 2025 and D23 2026 presentations. Marvel's own description of the character is deliberately narrow: **a cunning and powerful robot driven by revenge.** Showrunner Terry Matalas has gone further in press remarks around D23, calling Miller's Jocasta one of the standout characters of the MCU — a strong claim about her importance to the story, though not a plot description.

That's the entire extent of what Marvel has stated on the record. The studio has not said where Jocasta fits structurally: she's notably absent from the official synopsis line naming the AI personas living inside Vision's own mindscape (F.R.I.D.A.Y., E.D.I.T.H., J.A.R.V.I.S. and Ultron), which suggests — but doesn't confirm — that she may exist outside that specific mental-landscape conceit, operating in the real world rather than inside Vision's head. Marvel also hasn't said whose revenge she's pursuing, or against whom.

## Comics history vs. the MCU: what's actually confirmed

It's tempting to assume the show will lean on her full comics backstory — Ultron building her from Janet van Dyne's brain patterns as a companion, her rejection of him, her long Avengers membership — because it's such a clean fit for a series about Vision confronting the AI that made him. But Marvel has not confirmed that origin carries over to the MCU version. "Driven by revenge" is compatible with the comics backstory, but it's also compatible with an entirely new motivation invented for the show. Until Marvel or a cast member says otherwise, treat the comics history as the most likely inspiration, not as confirmed MCU canon.

## The bottom line

Jocasta has spent fifty years as proof that Ultron's own creations keep refusing to agree with him — Vision walked away from his ideology, and Jocasta walked away from his company. Whatever *VisionQuest* does with her, that's the throughline Marvel has to either follow or deliberately break: a robot Ultron built to love him, who instead decided he was wrong.
