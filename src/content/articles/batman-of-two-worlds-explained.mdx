*Batman of Two Worlds* is not a crossover in the way that title might suggest. DC's Batman Day 2026 one-shot puts mainstream Batman and Absolute Batman in the same $2.99 issue, on the same cover, from two different creative teams — but according to reporting on comments from the creative teams, the two Bruce Waynes do not meet. They're each getting their own story, built around the same basic problem, so readers can compare how differently each version of the character actually works.

## What Batman of Two Worlds Actually Is

**CONFIRMED BY DC:** *Batman of Two Worlds* #1 is a one-shot releasing worldwide on September 19, 2026 — Batman Day — for $2.99 at participating comic shops. It contains two all-new Batman stories: one starring mainstream DC continuity's Batman, written by Matt Fraction with art by Javi Fernández, colors by Tomeu Morey and letters by Clayton Cowles; the other starring [Absolute Batman](/articles/absolute-batman-explained/), written by Scott Snyder with art by Nick Derington, colors by Frank Martin and letters by Tom Napolitano. The cover is by Jorge Jiménez, Nick Dragotta and Frank Martin. It's exclusive to comic shops for the Batman Day event — not a digital or big-box retail release.

## Do Mainstream Batman and Absolute Batman Meet?

No — and this is the single most important thing to get right about this book. Despite cover art that places both Batmen in the same frame, reporting on the project is explicit that the two versions "will not be crossing over and meeting each other in this one-shot." The shared cover is a design choice meant to sell the comparison, not evidence of a shared scene. Each story stays inside its own continuity: Fraction and Fernández's story stays in the mainstream DC universe, and Snyder and Derington's stays in the Absolute Universe. If that changes with the actual printed issue, it isn't reflected in anything DC or the creators have said so far.

## One Problem, Two Different Batmen

**CONFIRMED BY DC / CREATOR EXPLANATION:** Both stories are built around theft. Snyder has described the concept directly: "Both stories are about theft, what theft means in one world versus the other, and what Batman's form of justice looks like in each." Fraction's framing is the tighter version of the same idea: "One problem, two solutions. Each version of Batman has his own way of working and his own solution to that problem."

**CREATOR EXPLANATION:** Snyder has said his Absolute Batman story specifically centers on a bank robbery, adding that it's "largely about, for us, not just how he takes down the criminals, but who the real criminals are behind all of it." That detail is attributed to the Absolute story specifically — DC hasn't confirmed that Fraction's mainstream Batman story involves an identical robbery, the same perpetrators, or the same victims. Treat the two crimes as thematically related, not necessarily literally identical, until the issue itself says otherwise.

**STILL UNKNOWN:** The specific plot of Fraction and Fernández's mainstream story hasn't been detailed beyond the shared theft premise.

## How Main DC Batman Approaches Justice

Mainstream Batman operates with the full weight of the Wayne fortune, the Batcave, a trained support network and decades of established Gotham infrastructure behind him. His version of solving a crime like this one draws on resources, technology and institutional relationships — the tools of a billionaire vigilante embedded in his city's power structures. Fraction has summarized his own approach to writing any Batman story in similar terms: "The challenge of any Batman thing for me is, the question is not 'will he solve the problem,' but 'how will he solve the problem.'" For mainstream Batman, the "how" runs through resources most people don't have.

## Why Absolute Batman's World Changes the Answer

[Absolute Batman](/articles/absolute-batman-explained/) has none of that. This Bruce Wayne grew up in Crime Alley rather than above it, works as a city civil engineer, and never inherited the manor, the fortune or a butler in the traditional sense — his version of Alfred is an MI6 operative, not household staff. That's the entire premise of [DC's Absolute Universe](/articles/absolute-universe-explained/): every hero is rebuilt without the advantage that made them comfortable. Facing the same basic problem — someone stealing something, and Batman deciding what justice looks like in response — this Bruce has to solve it with engineering knowledge, physical improvisation and infrastructure access instead of institutional power. That contrast is the actual reason DC paired these two stories in one book rather than publishing them separately: the crime is similar enough to isolate what each world's version of Batman brings to solving it.

## The Two Creative Teams

Fraction is a familiar match for mainstream Batman going into this — he's also writing DC's core *Batman* ongoing series. Snyder, meanwhile, is the writer who built the Absolute Universe's version of the character from scratch, and continues to write *Absolute Batman*'s ongoing series with artist Nick Dragotta. Splitting art duties between Javi Fernández and Nick Derington rather than a single artist drawing both stories reinforces the same point visually — two distinct houses of style for two distinct versions of the same idea.

## Do You Need to Read Absolute Batman First?

No. Nothing about *Batman of Two Worlds* requires prior *Absolute Batman* issues — DC has built the Absolute line specifically so each series works as its own entry point, and a one-shot built to introduce the contrast to a wide Batman Day audience isn't going to assume specialized knowledge. That said, readers who want the fuller picture of why Absolute Bruce operates so differently can get it from [our Absolute Batman explainer](/articles/absolute-batman-explained/), which covers his origin, his suit, and how his rogues' gallery diverges from the mainstream version in detail this piece doesn't repeat.

## Where Batman of Two Worlds Fits in the Reading Order

It's a standalone one-shot, not a required chapter in either ongoing series. For readers who want to actually start collecting the Absolute Universe titles this issue is drawing from, [our DC Absolute Universe reading order](/articles/dc-absolute-universe-reading-order/) lays out where *Absolute Batman* fits alongside *Absolute Superman*, *Absolute Wonder Woman* and the rest of the line.

## What DC Hasn't Revealed Yet

Beyond the shared theft premise and Snyder's bank-robbery detail for the Absolute story, DC hasn't published solicitation-level plot summaries for either story, preview pages beyond the cover, or confirmation of any other returning or new characters. Nothing here should be read as confirming villain identities, the mainstream story's specific crime, or any twist connecting the two tales beyond the thematic pairing DC and the creative teams have already described.
