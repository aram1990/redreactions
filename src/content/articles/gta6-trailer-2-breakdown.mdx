Trailer 1 sold a place. Trailer 2 sold a life.

The first *Grand Theft Auto VI* trailer, back in December 2023, was essentially a mood board for Leonida — alligators, phone-camera chaos, nightlife, beaches, and our first proper introduction to Lucia. Trailer 2, released May 6, 2025, did something harder. It gave us the boyfriend, the landlord, the record label, the bank, and the beginning of an actual plot.

Fifteen months later it's still the deepest look we have. Rockstar's next showcase, **An Extended Look**, lands August 27 — on Netflix first, at 3 p.m. ET. Until then, this is the evidence.

## What Rockstar actually said

The official synopsis is short and does a lot of work. Jason and Lucia have always known the deck is stacked against them — and when an easy score goes wrong, they end up on the darkest side of the sunniest place in America, caught in a conspiracy spanning the state of Leonida, forced to rely on each other to get out alive.

Two things are load-bearing there. **The failed score is the inciting incident**, not the climax. And the conspiracy is *statewide*, which is Rockstar telling you the map matters structurally, not just as scenery.

Rockstar confirmed that all Trailer 2 footage was captured on PlayStation 5, and that it's roughly half real-time gameplay, half in-engine cutscene. That second detail is the one most breakdowns skip, and it matters: not every shot in this trailer is something you'll be holding a controller for.

## The trailer, in order

A note on method: the sequence below is the order events appear. **Precise timestamps circulating in other breakdowns don't reliably agree with each other**, so we've left them out rather than invent precision.

**Jason on the roof.** We open on coastal water, boats and gulls in the Leonida Keys, then find Jason Duval doing maintenance on a roof. Brian Heder interrupts to demand his checks. Jason is unimpressed, but he goes.

**The shakedown.** Jason drives through town — a neighbour calls out to him — and then we're inside a store watching him slap a clerk around and empty the register. "Getting checks," it turns out, means collecting protection money. Whether the register grab is player-controlled or scripted isn't something the footage settles.

**The drive.** Road signs for Ambrosia, Downtown, VCI Airport and Southside. Aircraft overhead. Then Jason bench-pressing on the beach — weightlifting hasn't been a GTA system since *San Andreas* — and leaving a bodega with a six-pack.

**The pickup.** Jason drives past a street arrest and into Leonida Penitentiary's reception to collect Lucia, who walks out wearing an electronic ankle monitor.

**Community service, then the bank.** Lucia in a Leonida Department of Corrections hi-vis vest, picking up roadside litter. Then the hard cut: Jason and Lucia in skull masks robbing **Sinfrontera National Bank**, Lucia holding tellers at gunpoint with an SMG while Jason moves people.

**The good stretch.** A round of shots at the Rusty Anchor with Cal Hampton. Lucia in a gold dress on a yacht. Quiet moments on a pier. Then a tactical police unit breaching a run-down building, and a line about cops protecting their own that points squarely at a corruption thread.

**Escalation.** Drive-by shooting. Lucia in an MMA cage. A truck dragging a chained safe down a street. A grenade launcher turning a police cruiser inside out. A fistfight inside a cargo plane in flight.

**Downtime and swamp.** Jason watching an Ammu-Nation commercial in a motel room while Lucia rests. A helicopter chasing an airboat across the Grassrivers sawgrass. A jet ski escape.

**The close.** Cal in an airfield hangar. Then Jason and Lucia in a red Declasse Tulip, crossing a bridge toward the Vice City skyline at sunset.

## The cast Rockstar named

Rockstar has published official profiles for its core supporting cast, including Jason, Lucia, Cal, Boobie, Dre'Quan, Real Dimez, Raul and Brian. Lori Heder is also officially named through Brian's profile. Everything below is from Rockstar's own materials rather than fan identification.

**Jason Duval** — raised around grifters, enlisted in the U.S. Army to get out, and washed up in the Leonida Keys afterwards. Legitimate work didn't materialise, so he collects debts for Brian Heder in exchange for a place to stay. Tactical training, no ambition to speak of, until Lucia.

**Brian Heder** — a veteran smuggler from the Keys' golden age, still moving product through **Brian's Boat Works & Marina**. Too senior now to do the dirty work himself, which is what Jason and Cal are for.

## What Digital Foundry actually found

This is where most coverage — including the research that fed this piece — gets uniformly glowing. Digital Foundry's analysis was more mixed than that, and the mixed parts are the interesting ones.

**Resolution is the weak spot.** The trailer renders at roughly 1440p — precisely 2560×1152, with the shortfall on the vertical axis being a product of the letterboxed 20:9 framing — then upscales to 4K using what DF characterised as FSR1-like reconstruction. Analyst Oliver Mackenzie's read was that image quality is the element that brings you back down to earth: fine, but not exceptional.

**Ray tracing is doing the heavy lifting.** Ray-traced global illumination and ray-traced reflections are both present, and DF's Alex Battaglia argued RTGI is inherent to how the game works rather than a toggle — reflections on transparent surfaces like sunglasses, mirrors and windscreens simply wouldn't be achievable any other way. Rougher surfaces appear to blend RT with conventional screen-space reflections.

**Shadows are not ray-traced.** They use traditional shadow mapping, which DF read as a sensible trade given how much RT budget is spent elsewhere. Occasional disconnected contact shadows are visible if you look for them.

**Character rendering is the standout.** Hair simulation is a clear generational step up, down to visible arm hair. Sweat rendering on exerting characters uses uneven specular highlights rather than a uniform sheen.

**On 60fps:** the trailer runs at 30. DF's position is that a 60fps mode looks unlikely, on the reasoning that you can't strip out RTGI and that hitting 60 would demand drastic resolution cuts. **That's analysis, not a Rockstar statement** — no performance modes have been announced for any platform. DF also speculated that the Xbox Series S version may need to drop ray tracing entirely at sub-1080p, which is likewise informed guesswork.

## What Trailer 2 visibly shows

The distinction that matters here: footage shows what happens on screen, not how the game plays. Rockstar has confirmed remarkably few systems. What the trailer shows is this:

- **Jason is shown collecting cash during a store shakedown** — but the trailer alone doesn't establish the player-controlled robbery mechanics behind it.
- **Jason and Lucia are shown carrying out a bank robbery** at Sinfrontera National Bank — but Rockstar has not detailed how heists function as a gameplay system, or whether a planning layer exists.
- **Lucia is shown fighting in a cage** — but no formal MMA or combat-sport system has been announced.
- **Jason is shown weightlifting** on the beach — but Rockstar has not confirmed a *San Andreas*-style fitness or stat system, however strongly the shot invites the comparison.
- **Tactical police units are shown breaching a building** — but police AI, dispatch behaviour and the wanted system remain undetailed.
- **Lucia's ankle monitor and community service** are confirmed story elements. They are not a confirmed "parole system," and nothing indicates they gate map access.
- **The footage shows cars, motorcycles, boats, jet skis, airboats, helicopters and aircraft** across land, sea and air.

Rockstar's accompanying screenshots depict activities including basketball, mini-golf, pool, fishing, kayaking, scuba diving and dirt-bike riding. Which of these are fully playable activities has not been individually detailed.

## Brands, callbacks and Easter eggs

**Sinfrontera National Bank**, **Only Raw Records**, **Jack of Hearts**, **Brian's Boat Works & Marina**, **Delights Cabaret**, and **Allied Crystal** in Ambrosia are all new. **Ammu-Nation**, **Sprunk**, **eCola**, **Gruppe Sechs**, and manufacturers **Declasse**, **Vapid**, **Pegassi** and **Benefactor** all return.

Two visual gags are unambiguous: a PlayStation 5 and DualSense-style controller sitting in Jason and Lucia's living room, and a giant roadside whale statue parodying Florida Keys tourist-trap architecture. In-game banknotes also appear to reuse the portraits from *Red Dead Redemption 2*.

The Ammu-Nation commercial features a mulleted man named Phil, which a lot of people have read as **Phil Cassidy** from *Vice City*. That's a reasonable read and it is **not confirmed** — the timeline makes a direct return awkward, and Rockstar has form for winking rather than committing.

## What the trailer does not confirm

| Claim | Status |
|---|---|
| A percentage of buildings are enterable | No official statement exists, at any figure |
| The map is twice the size of *GTA V* | No dimensions published |
| Dynamic hurricanes | Weather is shown; destructive storm systems are not confirmed |
| 60fps console mode | Unannounced. DF thinks unlikely — that's analysis |
| Six-star wanted level | Nothing shown supports a specific star count |
| Vehicle fuel system | No fuel gauge or refuelling shown |
| Day-one GTA Online | Marketing has focused entirely on single-player |
| Full heist planning mechanics | Cinematic heist footage doesn't confirm a planning layer |

One further caution: weapon attachments visible in the trailer suggest customisation, but **no customisation system has been announced.** Different attachments across shots is evidence of variety, not of a menu.

## The questions Trailer 2 leaves open

- What is the score that goes wrong, and how early does it happen?
- How does character switching work outside missions?
- Are Jason and Lucia always together, or does the story separate them?
- How much does Lucia's ankle monitor actually constrain early exploration?
- Can players own or run businesses like Only Raw Records or the marina?
- What does Raul Bautista's recruitment pitch actually lead to?
- How do corrupt police factions interact with the wanted system?

## The bottom line

Trailer 2's real achievement was structural. It took a setting everyone already wanted and gave it a spine: a landlord who owns your time, a partner just out of prison, a bank robbery that presumably goes wrong, and a state full of people with more leverage than you.

Fifteen months on, it's still the only substantial thing Rockstar has shown. August 27 is when that finally changes.
