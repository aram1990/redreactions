Pokémon Champions' Regulation Set M-C is live, running from **September 8, 2026 at 7:00 p.m. PDT through December 1, 2026 at 5:59 p.m. PST** (worldwide, that's effectively September 9 through December 2 in most other timezones). It's the biggest single roster update the game has had, adding six new Mega Evolutions and 24 newly battle-eligible Pokémon on top of everything already legal under Regulation Sets M-A and M-B.

## What Regulation M-C Actually Changes

M-C doesn't replace the prior roster — it adds to it. Everything legal under M-A and M-B stays legal. The two things worth understanding are what's new: three ordinary Mega Evolutions (Mega Salamence, Mega Golisopod, Mega Baxcalibur) and, distinct from those, three Z Mega Evolutions (Mega Absol Z, Mega Garchomp Z, Mega Lucario Z) — a separate category explained in full in [our Z Mega Evolution guide](/articles/z-mega-evolution-explained-pokemon-champions/), since the mechanic works differently enough to deserve its own explanation rather than a repeat here.

## The Three New Ordinary Mega Evolutions

**Mega Salamence** gets the Ability **Aerilate**, which converts Salamence's Normal-type moves into Flying-type and boosts their power — a real shift for a Pokémon whose movepool already leans Normal-type, since moves that previously matched its dual Dragon/Flying typing awkwardly now hit as a third, boosted type.

**Mega Golisopod** becomes **Steel/Bug**-typed with the Ability **Tough Claws**, which boosts the power of contact moves. That typing combination leaves it with only one type weakness — Fire — a notably narrow profile for a Mega Evolution.

**Mega Baxcalibur** gets **Thermal Exchange**, an Ability that converts incoming Fire-type hits into an Attack boost instead of a burn. Combined with Baxcalibur's existing Dragon/Ice typing and a Mega Evolution's usual stat and bulk increase, that Fire immunity-plus-boost pattern makes its own Ice- and Dragon-type attacks meaningfully harder to answer for teams built around Dragon, Flying or Ground-type cores that would otherwise threaten it.

## The 24 Newly Available Pokémon

Pokémon.com's own announcement highlights **Rillaboom** by name as a Pokémon "that has made an impact in past VGC tournaments," while directing players to the in-game Recruit menu for the complete roster rather than publishing an exhaustive list itself. Specialist tracking coverage lists the full 24 as: Wigglytuff, Persian, Alolan Persian, Farfetch'd, Mr. Mime, Swalot, Salamence, Gogoat, Golisopod, Rillaboom, Cinderace, Inteleon, Thievul, Toxtricity, Grapploct, Perrserker, Sirfetch'd, Pincurchin, Indeedee, Pawmot, Arboliva, Squawkabilly, Mabosstiff and Baxcalibur.

Worth noting: Salamence, Golisopod and Baxcalibur appear on both lists — they're newly available as base Pokémon *and* they're the three species getting new ordinary Mega Evolutions this regulation, which is why Pokémon.com singles them out as headline additions rather than treating the Mega Evolutions and the roster expansion as two unrelated updates.

## What Changes Strategically

This is worth being disciplined about: Pokémon Champions is a live, ongoing competitive game, and Regulation M-C is ten days old as of this writing. There isn't meaningful tournament or ladder data yet to support claims about what's actually dominant, and this guide isn't going to manufacture a tier list to fill that gap.

What can be said with confidence, based on verified Ability text rather than results: Mega Baxcalibur's Thermal Exchange gives it a genuine answer to Fire-type attacks that would otherwise threaten a Dragon/Ice typing, which matters against any team leaning on Fire coverage specifically to check Dragon-types. Mega Golisopod's Steel/Bug typing with only a Fire weakness is a defensively narrow profile that's unusual for a Mega Evolution, most of which trade bulk for exploitable weaknesses elsewhere. Mega Salamence's Aerilate turns an already-established attacker's Normal-type options into a third offensive type, which is a real answer to teams that built specifically around resisting Dragon and Flying-type damage from standard Salamence.

None of that is the same as saying any of the three "breaks" the format — that's a claim only actual tournament results can support, and this article isn't going to get ahead of the data that doesn't exist yet.

## Early Team-Building Considerations

With confirmed Abilities and typings as the only solid ground to build on this early into the regulation, a few practical starting points: teams that previously relied on Fire-type coverage specifically to answer Dragon-types now have to account for Mega Baxcalibur turning that matchup around. Teams built around exploiting a second type weakness on Bug or Steel-type Pokémon get less mileage against Mega Golisopod specifically, given how narrow its weakness profile is. And Rillaboom's return to legal status is worth factoring into any team already planning around Grassy Terrain support, given its established VGC track record.

Beyond that, the honest advice is to wait for actual results. Six new Mega Evolutions and 24 newly legal Pokémon is a large enough shift that early theorycrafting is more likely to be wrong than right.

## What Changed From M-B

The core distinction between M-B and M-C is additive, not a rules overhaul: no eligibility restrictions were lifted from the prior regulation, and nothing legal under M-B lost its legal status. M-C is a roster expansion — six new Mega Evolutions and 24 newly available Pokémon layered on top of an unchanged existing legal pool, rather than a format reset.
