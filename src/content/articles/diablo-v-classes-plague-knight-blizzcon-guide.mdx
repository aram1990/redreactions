Diablo V's opening BlizzCon teaser only confirmed that the game exists and targets Spring 2029. The follow-up panel, "Diablo V: Forging the Next Era," filled in far more: the game's first three classes, its premise, and a handful of returning systems. Here's everything Blizzard has actually confirmed — and what's still genuinely unknown.

## CONFIRMED BY BLIZZARD

- Official title: **Diablo V**, targeting **Spring 2029**
- **Platforms remain unannounced** — Blizzard's own materials name no platforms
- Setting: Sanctuary has fallen — "the heroes are gone, and Diablo has won"; players become the **Heir to Westmarch**
- First three revealed classes: **Demon Hunter**, **Monk**, and the all-new **Plague Knight**
- Game Director **Joe Shely** stated Diablo V "will ship with more classes than any Diablo game before it" — a claim about launch class count relative to prior games, not a specific number, and not a claim about total classes ever added post-launch
- Blizzard's own wording: "These are only the first of the classes we'll reveal on the road to Diablo V"
- A **caravan system**: players build a caravan of allies who travel with them through "the scorched wastes," serving as vendors and companions
- **Procedural generation**: a "procedural landscape where terrain, points of interest, monsters, and reward can change when you return"
- Itemization "inspired by Diablo II": stat requirements on gear, larger items taking up more inventory space, and the return of **gear sets** and **Diablo II-style Runes and Runewords**
- Development updates begin in **2027**: Blizzard says it plans to "share regular updates offering a closer look at our progress, the ideas guiding development, and features taking shape along the way"

## The First Three Classes

**Demon Hunter** returns as described by Blizzard: hunters who "stalk the forces of Hell with twin crossbows, striking from the shadows and leaping across the battlefield to annihilate their prey" — the same ranged, mobile identity the class has carried since Diablo III.

**Monk** also returns, "battering demons to a pulp with fists and calling down holy power to smite those who stand in their path" — melee-focused, martial-arts-driven, with an explicitly holy dimension to its power.

![Official Diablo V concept art of a bald, robed warrior wearing prayer beads, consistent with the returning Monk class.](/images/articles/diablo-v-classes-plague-knight-blizzcon-guide/diablo-v-monk-concept-art.webp "Blizzard Entertainment")

**Plague Knight** is the new addition, and Blizzard's own description leans hard into a control-and-attrition identity: "the embodiment of walking death," roaming the battlefield "spreading disease and pestilence, ultimately consuming it for power." That's a heavily armored, close-quarters class built around applying and later harvesting disease effects — genuinely distinct from anything currently in the Diablo class roster, though Blizzard hasn't detailed specific skills, a resource system, or how disease-consumption translates into concrete combat mechanics yet.

## How Many Classes Will Launch?

Be precise about what's actually been said here: Joe Shely's confirmed line is that Diablo V "will ship with more classes than any Diablo game before it" — a comparison to prior Diablo games' launch rosters, not a specific number and not a claim about the eventual total across the game's full support lifecycle. Blizzard has explicitly framed Demon Hunter, Monk, and Plague Knight as only the first reveals, with more classes still to come before launch.

## What the Panel Revealed About Sanctuary

The confirmed premise is a genuine departure for the series: previous Diablo games put players in the position of stopping an apocalypse in progress. Diablo V starts after that apocalypse has already happened and been lost. Blizzard's framing poses it directly: "what must you become when the world has already fallen?" The Heir to Westmarch and their caravan travel through this transformed Sanctuary, with the procedural landscape system meaning returning locations won't necessarily look the same twice.

![Official Diablo V concept art showing a ruined, fire-lit cathedral overtaken by corrupted growth.](/images/articles/diablo-v-classes-plague-knight-blizzcon-guide/diablo-v-westmarch-cathedral-ruins-concept-art.webp "Blizzard Entertainment")

## Returning Systems and Itemization

Blizzard is explicitly pitching itemization "inspired by Diablo II" — a signal to longtime fans that gear will matter more granularly than some more recent entries. Stat requirements return, inventory space is tied to item size again, and both gear sets and Diablo II's Runes and Runewords system are confirmed to be coming back. None of this has been shown in a playable build; it's described, not yet demonstrated.

![Official Diablo V concept art of a heavily armored warrior holding a sword, from the game's BlizzCon 2026 panel gallery.](/images/articles/diablo-v-classes-plague-knight-blizzcon-guide/diablo-v-armored-warrior-concept-art.webp "Blizzard Entertainment")

## What Blizzard Still Hasn't Confirmed

No platforms have been announced. There's no exact release date beyond the Spring 2029 window, no beta date, no final class count, no confirmed skill trees, no UI, no multiplayer structure, and nothing on monetization, a battle pass, cross-play, or Game Pass availability. Concept art and panel footage shown so far should be read as early development material, not a preview of final gameplay — Blizzard itself describes Diablo V as "still early in development."

## Bottom Line

Diablo V now has a confirmed identity beyond its teaser: a post-apocalyptic Sanctuary, three announced classes with Plague Knight as the genuinely new one, a Diablo II-inspired itemization pass, and a promise of more classes to come before its Spring 2029 target. Treat everything else — final roster size, platforms, exact release date — as unconfirmed until Blizzard's promised 2027 updates start filling in the rest.
