North Beach Games finally showed what *Stranded Deep 2* actually is on August 31, and it isn't just a second helping of the original's tropical island loop. The islands are gone. In their place is a desert built around one continuous river, and a survival structure rebuilt around the fact that the water — not a fixed patch of sand — is now the thing you live on.

The studio, developing and publishing through its Prague team, confirmed original *Stranded Deep* co-creator Sam Edwards is Creative Director on the sequel. North Beach describes it as a new survival adventure rather than a direct continuation of the first game's story. Steam Early Access is planned for **early 2027**; the Steam store page itself still lists the release date as "to be announced," which is worth noting since the more specific window comes from the developer's own FAQ, not Steam's release-date field.

## Islands out, an endless river in

The original game was defined by hopping between a limited set of Pacific islands. *Stranded Deep 2* replaces that with a procedurally generated river that keeps extending as you travel downstream, with hand-crafted locations scattered through that generated world rather than everything being algorithm-built. North Beach describes the setting as spanning canyons, dunes, valleys and prairies, with rapids and calmer stretches of water in between — meaning the water itself becomes a hazard to navigate on top of everything already threatening you on land.

## Your base can travel with you

This is the structural change that matters most. Instead of committing to one static camp and making round trips back to it, players can build a floating home directly on the river and take the whole base downstream with them — pack up and continue to the next stretch, or settle on the banks if you'd rather stay put. North Beach hasn't detailed exactly how large or elaborate these mobile bases can get, so treat the concept itself — a base that moves with you instead of anchoring you to one spot — as the confirmed part, not any specific scale.

## The world reacts to what you do

North Beach is building the survival systems — health, hunger, thirst, environmental exposure — around a world with actual terrain deformation: players can reshape land and redirect the river's flow rather than just building on top of unchangeable geography. Crafting is tied to the specific materials you gather rather than a generic resource pool, so what you build depends on what the local environment actually offers.

The wildlife is designed as a real food chain rather than a fixed set of scripted encounters. North Beach says animals interact with each other, not just with the player — small fish and lizards share the world with apex predators like crocodiles and hippos, and the studio's own framing is that you're not guaranteed to be at the top of that chain.

## Cartographer 2.0 and co-op

The Cartographer, the original game's custom-map tool, returns significantly expanded as **Cartographer 2.0**. North Beach says it gives players more ways to build their own locations and experiences and share them with friends and the community. Official material also uses "across platforms" language for that sharing, but North Beach hasn't specified which additional platforms are actually involved yet — worth flagging as an intention rather than a confirmed technical detail.

Co-op is a confirmed development priority — North Beach describes multiplayer as something the game has been built around from the beginning and a first-class feature, and Steam lists Co-op among the game's tags. But Steam's own Early Access description also says co-op will be deepened over the course of development, which means the exact feature set available on day one of Early Access is still an open question rather than something already spelled out in full.

## What Early Access actually includes

Per Steam's own listing, *Stranded Deep 2* launches into Early Access as a playable survival experience built around kayak and raft river exploration, the core health/hunger/thirst/exposure loop, hand-crafted locations, and several biomes. North Beach's plan for the Early Access period itself is to add more locations, biomes, wildlife and map-editor content alongside deeper crafting, progression and co-op over time — those are stated as planned expansions during Early Access, not features already present at launch. Controller support and Steam Deck verification are both listed as Early Access goals rather than day-one guarantees, and mod support is planned with details still to come.

North Beach currently estimates Early Access will run roughly **two to three years** before a 1.0 release — an estimate, not a promised date, and no full-release timeline has been set.

## Early 2027 on Steam — what about consoles?

North Beach has said plainly that it plans to bring *Stranded Deep 2* to other services and platforms, including consoles. That's a real statement of intent, but it stops there: no specific console platforms have been named, no console release window has been given, and nothing suggests a console version launching alongside Steam Early Access. Anyone expecting a PS5 or Xbox version to show up at the same time as the PC Early Access build is getting ahead of what North Beach has actually confirmed.
