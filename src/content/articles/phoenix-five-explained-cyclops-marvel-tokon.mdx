Phoenix Cyclops isn't a *Marvel Tōkon: Fighting Souls* invention. Arc System Works has been direct about it: this version of Scott Summers "draws inspiration from his Phoenix Five incarnation from *Avengers vs. X-Men*." That 2012 comic event is where the Phoenix Force — a cosmic force tied to life, death and rebirth — split apart during a confrontation on the Moon and bonded with five mutants at once, Cyclops among them. He wasn't the intended host, and he didn't start the story anywhere near the power level Tōkon is building its character around.

## Why the Phoenix was coming for Hope Summers

The Phoenix's return wasn't originally about Cyclops at all. It was drawn toward Hope Summers, a young mutant whose arrival mattered because of what had happened years earlier: the Scarlet Witch's "M-Day" had wiped out the vast majority of Earth's mutant population, and Hope represented the possibility of that changing. The X-Men, led by Cyclops, saw the Phoenix's arrival as a potential rebirth for mutantkind. The Avengers saw the same cosmic force and feared a planet-level threat, given the Phoenix's destructive history. That disagreement is what pulled the two teams into open conflict.

## How the Phoenix Five were created

The breaking point happened during a confrontation on the Moon. Iron Man moved to disrupt the incoming Phoenix Force before it could reach Earth — and Hope herself refused its power when the moment came. Instead of dissipating or picking a single new host, the Phoenix Force split into five pieces and bonded with five X-Men simultaneously: Cyclops, Emma Frost, Colossus, Magik and Namor. None of them sought this out as a personal upgrade; it's the result of the Phoenix's arrival going sideways, not a deliberate transfer to Scott Summers specifically. Marvel's own account marks *Avengers vs. X-Men* (2012) #5 as the Phoenix Five's first appearance.

## What the Phoenix Five actually tried to do

This is worth being precise about, because "the Phoenix Five" gets flattened into "five X-Men who turned evil" a lot more often than the comic supports. With that combined cosmic power, the Five set out to build what the story calls a Pax Utopia — growing food for the hungry, ending armed conflicts, and destroying weapons on a global scale. It reads, initially, as five people trying to actually fix the world with power that finally makes that possible.

What follows isn't a sudden villain turn so much as an escalation: control creeping into what started as intervention, the Five becoming more erratic and more willing to police the world by force as the story goes on. That shift is what eventually brings the Avengers and X-Men together against their own former allies.

## Why it fell apart — and how Cyclops ended up with it all

Once both teams turn against the Five, they discover something important: five divided hosts are far more vulnerable than the Phoenix Force in one body. Namor is the first to fall, and rather than the Phoenix simply vanishing, his portion gets redistributed among the four hosts still standing. Spider-Man then goads Colossus and Magik into turning on each other, and that fight takes both of them out of the equation — their shares of the Phoenix folding into what's left. That leaves two: Cyclops and Emma Frost.

When Professor Xavier confronts Cyclops directly, trying to talk him down, Cyclops instead turns on Emma and takes her remaining share of the Phoenix Force by force. That's the specific moment he becomes Dark Phoenix — not a starting point, but the end of a chain of redistributions he didn't fully control. At that power level, in the same confrontation, Cyclops kills Charles Xavier.

## What happened to the Phoenix afterward

Even as Dark Phoenix, Cyclops doesn't hold the power for long. Hope Summers and Scarlet Witch work together to eject the Phoenix Force from him. It briefly bonds with Hope, who uses it to help undo damage the Five caused, and then, with Wanda Maximoff's help, the Force is expelled from Earth entirely — with some of its power used specifically to restore the potential for new mutant births after the damage M-Day had done. That resolution is the actual payoff to why Hope mattered from the start of the story.

## Phoenix Cyclops isn't Jean Grey

Worth stating plainly, since it's the most common mix-up: Jean Grey is the character most associated with the Phoenix Force across Marvel history, but Phoenix Five Cyclops is a completely separate event, years after Jean's own most famous Phoenix stories. Scott doesn't inherit Jean's identity, this isn't a fusion of the two characters, and nothing about the Phoenix arriving in *Avengers vs. X-Men* required Jean to be involved at all. The Phoenix Force has had multiple hosts across Marvel's history, and being possessed by it doesn't automatically mean becoming Dark Phoenix — that specific escalation is what happens to Cyclops here, not a universal rule.

## Why Marvel Tōkon chose this version

*Marvel Tōkon: Fighting Souls* launched August 6, 2026 on PS5 and PC, and Phoenix Cyclops — voiced by Ray Chase — was revealed as the game's first additional character for Year 1 content. PlayStation's own announcement is explicit that this design draws on the Phoenix Five incarnation specifically, keeping Cyclops' signature optic blasts and hand-to-hand combat while adding the Phoenix Force on top. Arc System Works has said more detail on how those abilities translate into actual gameplay is still to come — there's no confirmed mechanic, meter system or transformation to describe yet, and nothing officially released should be treated as more than the visual and conceptual inspiration for now.

It's also worth being precise about what "inspired by" does and doesn't mean here: Tōkon pulling from the Phoenix Five arc doesn't confirm this version of Cyclops lived through the exact Earth-616 *Avengers vs. X-Men* story beat for beat. The comic is the source material; whatever backstory Tōkon actually gives its own Phoenix Cyclops is a separate question the game hasn't fully answered yet. Several outlets have reported a fall 2026 window for his release, but PlayStation's own announcement doesn't specify an exact date — treat that timing as unconfirmed until Arc System Works says so directly.
