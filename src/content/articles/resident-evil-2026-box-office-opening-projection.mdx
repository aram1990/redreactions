Zach Cregger's *Resident Evil* is now forecast for a $40–50 million domestic opening weekend, according to Deadline and Variety — up from the roughly $35 million Deadline was reporting in early September. If that range holds when the weekend is tallied, it would beat the franchise's existing opening-weekend record by a wide margin — a mark *Resident Evil: Afterlife* has held since 2010 at $26.65 million. Boxoffice Pro's own long-range forecast had already raised its range from $30–40 million to $40–50 million as pre-release tracking strengthened; Deadline's own number has now moved to match it. Independent tracking services cited by Variety believe the film could clear $50 million and get close to $60 million, helped by the best preview numbers in the franchise's history: $8–9 million on Thursday night, according to Deadline, more than any previous *Resident Evil* film's entire preview total. None of that is a confirmed result yet — previews are real, reported numbers, but the full opening weekend isn't final until Monday.

## How the Projection Stacks Up Against Every Previous Film

Using consistent domestic 3-day opening weekend figures from Box Office Mojo, here's where Cregger's film would land against the seven previous theatrical entries:

| Film | Domestic opening weekend |
| --- | --- |
| Resident Evil (2002) | $17,707,106 |
| Resident Evil: Apocalypse (2004) | $23,036,273 |
| Resident Evil: Extinction (2007) | $23,678,580 |
| Resident Evil: Afterlife (2010) | $26,650,264 |
| Resident Evil: Retribution (2012) | $21,052,227 |
| Resident Evil: The Final Chapter (2017) | $13,601,682 |
| Resident Evil: Welcome to Raccoon City (2021) | $5,319,280 |
| **Resident Evil (2026)** | **~$40,000,000–$50,000,000 (forecast, not final)** |

At the low end of that range, the new film would open roughly 50% above *Afterlife*'s $26.65 million record; at the high end, it would be closer to 88% above it. If the film clears $50 million, as some independent trackers now expect, the margin widens further still. Either way, the comparison only means something once the actual number is reported Monday morning — projections in both directions have been wrong before.

## Why This Record Would Actually Mean Something

The franchise's opening-weekend trajectory has been on a long decline. It peaked with *Afterlife* in 2010, then dropped through *Retribution* and *The Final Chapter* before *Welcome to Raccoon City* opened to just $5.3 million in 2021 — a collapse steep enough that Constantin Film moved on from that continuity entirely rather than continuing it. A $40–50 million opening wouldn't just edge past a 16-year-old record; it would reverse a trend that had been pointing toward the franchise's box-office relevance running out.

That context is also why the projection shouldn't be read as more than what it is. A strong tracking number reflects awareness, presale activity and audience-interest polling — it says nothing about whether the film is good, whether longtime fans approve of Cregger's approach, or whether it will hold well in its second and third weekends. Franchises have opened big and faded fast before.

## What "Tracking" Actually Measures

Box-office tracking blends several inputs: audience awareness surveys, ticket presales, comparisons to similar past releases, and exhibitor-reported demand. Different trade outlets weigh those inputs differently — Deadline's own number started lower than Boxoffice Pro's before converging on roughly the same $40–50 million range as release week arrived. Even real preview receipts, like Thursday night's $8–9 million, are actual reported figures rather than projections, but they still aren't the same thing as a finished three-day weekend — that's why coverage uses words like "tracking toward" and "forecast" rather than "earned" or "made." Actual opening numbers won't exist until after the weekend plays out.

## What the Movie Itself Is Doing Differently

Part of the interest driving this tracking is that Cregger isn't continuing the Paul W.S. Anderson/Milla Jovovich series or *Welcome to Raccoon City* — this is a full reboot built around a new character, Bryan, in a story Cregger has said takes place in the world of *Resident Evil 2*. For what that means for viewers who know the games versus those who don't, [our full breakdown of the movie's game connections](/articles/resident-evil-2026-movie-game-connections/) covers what Cregger has actually confirmed about the adaptation.

*Resident Evil* opens wide domestically on September 18, 2026, after Thursday preview screenings already began posting record numbers for the franchise. Full opening-weekend figures will be reported after the weekend closes.
