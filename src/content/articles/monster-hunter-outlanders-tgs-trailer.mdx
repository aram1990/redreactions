Capcom and TiMi Studio Group closed out Capcom Spotlight at Tokyo Game Show 2026 with a new trailer for *Monster Hunter Outlanders*, the free-to-play mobile entry in the series. It's a broader gameplay showcase rather than a single-reveal moment, and it lands roughly a month after the game's original Elder Dragon was first unveiled at gamescom.

<YouTube id="Oe0oNy0se1s" title="Tokyo Game Show 2026 Trailer" />

## What's Actually New Here

The gamescom reveal already introduced the game's Elder Dragon and opened pre-registration; this TGS trailer is a different kind of showcase. Reporting on the footage describes hunters racing across "sweeping, rain-soaked vistas" with "jagged rock formations and dense swamps," fighting "avian, draconic, and primordial threats," and using environmental hazards like "explosive flora, tidal patterns, and lava flows" as part of combat. It's a wider look at the game's world and moment-to-moment hunting rather than a follow-up monster reveal.

## The Elder Dragon: Naming It Correctly

Coverage of the flagship Elder Dragon has used two different English spellings — **Ruger Aidal** and **Luga Ealda** — depending on the outlet and whether it's translating directly from Japanese materials. The official Monster Hunter Outlanders English channels and press coverage most consistently use **Ruger Aidal**, and that's the spelling this article uses; treat "Luga Ealda" as an alternate romanization seen in some Japanese-sourced coverage rather than a second, different monster. Per its gamescom reveal, Ruger Aidal is wreathed in "golden and iridescent light," resembling a "second sun," and during its introduction it "surrounds itself with an intense orb of light before releasing a powerful heat wave that scorches the surrounding area." Capcom and TiMi haven't confirmed how directly this TGS trailer's hunt footage features Ruger Aidal specifically versus other monsters shown alongside it.

## A Playable Demo at TGS

Beyond the trailer itself, *Monster Hunter Outlanders* had a hands-on demo running at the Tokyo Game Show show floor. Reporting on that demo describes it as set in a region called **Kuchihate no Fuchi**, featuring traversal tools like gliders and ziplines, and hunts against monsters including a **Radiant Radobaan** and an **Azure Rathalos** — both variant-styled versions of classic Monster Hunter monsters rather than new original creatures. That's separate, playable content beyond what the trailer itself shows.

## What's Confirmed About the Game Itself

*Monster Hunter Outlanders* is a free-to-play mobile title co-developed by Capcom and TiMi Studio Group, coming to iOS and Android. Confirmed systems include seven weapon types at launch — among them Long Sword, Dual Blades, and Heavy Bowgun — resource gathering, gear crafting, and temporary base camps, with quests playable solo or in online co-op squads of up to four hunters. Cooperative play is built around coordination rather than just showing up together: reporting on the trailer describes players setting traps, using environmental hazards together, and lining up synchronized attacks rather than just fighting side by side. Global pre-registration is open now through the game's official site and the App Store and Google Play, and the game carries persistent loot progression that scales across sessions rather than resetting.

## Release Date: Still Not Confirmed

Despite some regional app-store listings and secondhand aggregation citing a December 15, 2026 date, neither the TGS trailer nor Capcom and TiMi's official materials have confirmed a release date. A storefront metadata date is not the same as an official announcement, and coverage that has specifically checked the primary sources notes that "any calendar date appearing outside those sources should be treated as unconfirmed." Treat *Monster Hunter Outlanders* as still without a confirmed release date until Capcom or TiMi state one directly.

## What's Still Unknown

Beyond the release date, Capcom and TiMi haven't detailed a full monster roster, confirmed how many regions will be available at launch, or specified how Ruger Aidal factors into the game's story versus being an optional high-difficulty hunt. Nothing in the TGS trailer or its coverage should be read as confirming endgame content, a live-service roadmap, or specific monetization details beyond the game being free-to-play.
